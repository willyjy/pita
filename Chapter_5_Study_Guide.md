### 5.0 Introduction

**Definition**: Medical and Health Insurance (MHIT) = umbrella term for insurance contracts mitigating health-related risks.

**Historical Terminology Evolution:**
- Accident insurance
- Health insurance  
- Accident and health insurance
- Accident and sickness insurance
- Disability insurance
→ **Now unified as**: Medical and Health Insurance (MHIT)

**Why MHIT is Essential:**

| Factor | Impact | Malaysian Context |
|--------|--------|-------------------|
| **Spiraling Medical Costs** | Substantial financial burden | Private hospital costs escalating 10-15% annually |
| **Unpredictable Health** | Anyone can fall ill anytime | Thousands hospitalized annually |
| **Financial Strain** | Medical bills can devastate finances | Single major surgery: RM50,000-300,000+ |
| **Growing Awareness** | More people recognize need | Market expansion in Malaysia |

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: MHIT is not just "nice to have" - it's a **NECESSITY** in modern risk management.

📌 **EXAM QUESTION LINK**: Q3, Q4 test understanding of health concerns and definition of health insurance.

### 5.1 Healthcare Issues in Malaysia

**Five Major Issues** (HIGH EXAM FREQUENCY):

#### Issue 1: Shortage of Qualified Medical Personnel

| Problem | Details | Impact |
|---------|---------|--------|
| **Growing Demand** | Population growth + aging population | Insufficient doctors/specialists |
| **Production Gap** | Medical graduate output < demand | Longer wait times |
| **Specialist Shortage** | Particularly acute in specialized fields | Limited access to specialized care |

---

#### Issue 2: Rising Healthcare Costs

| Factor | Cause | Effect |
|--------|-------|--------|
| **Inflation** | General economic inflation | All medical costs increase |
| **Medical Inflation** | 10-15% annually (higher than general) | Treatments more expensive yearly |
| **Technology Costs** | Advanced equipment expensive | Diagnostic/treatment costs rise |
| **Medication Costs** | New drugs costly | Pharmaceutical expenses increase |

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Medical inflation (10-15%) is HIGHER than general inflation (3-5%).

---

#### Issue 3: Access and Distribution of Medical Care

| Location | Facilities | Quality | Access |
|----------|-----------|---------|--------|
| **Urban (KL, JB, Penang)** | Advanced medical facilities | High quality | Good access |
| **Rural Areas** | Basic facilities | Variable quality | Poor access |

**Disparity Example:**
```
Kuala Lumpur: 20+ major private hospitals, 100+ specialists
Rural Kelantan: 2 government hospitals, limited specialists
```

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: "Uneven distribution" is a key healthcare issue in Malaysia.

---

#### Issue 4: Quality of Medical Care Variation

**Factors Affecting Quality:**
- Funding availability
- Resource allocation
- Access to technology
- Medical expertise availability
- Governance and oversight

**Private vs Public:**
- **Private** (urban): Advanced tech, high expertise, high cost
- **Public** (subsidized): Variable quality, longer wait, affordable

---

#### Issue 5: Emerging Healthcare Challenges

| Challenge Type | Examples | Impact |
|---------------|----------|--------|
| **NCDs** (Non-Communicable Diseases) | Diabetes, heart disease, cancer | Chronic, long-term treatment needed |
| **Mental Health** | Depression, anxiety, stress-related | Growing recognition, needs coverage |
| **Lifestyle Changes** | Sedentary lifestyle, poor diet | Increasing health issues |
| **Environmental** | Pollution, climate change effects | New health risks emerging |

### 5.2.a MEDICAL REIMBURSEMENT INSURANCE/TAKAFUL

**Definition**: Reimburses actual medical expenses incurred for treatment.

**Also Known As**:
- Hospital and Surgical Insurance
- Hospitalization Insurance
- Major Medical Insurance

---

#### 5.2.a.i Coverage

**What's Covered** (Typical):

| Category | Examples | Malaysian Context |
|----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Hospitalization** | Room & board, ICU | Private hospital: RM200-1,000+/day |
| **Surgery** | Surgical fees, operating room | Major surgery: RM10,000-100,000+ |
| **Specialist Fees** | Consultation, treatment | RM200-500+ per visit |
| **Diagnostic Tests** | X-ray, MRI, CT scan, blood tests | MRI: RM1,000-3,000 |
| **Medication** | Prescribed drugs during hospitalization | Variable |
| **Emergency Treatment** | A&E services | Critical in accidents |
| **Pre/Post Hospitalization** | Before/after hospital visits | Usually 60/90 days coverage |

---

#### 5.2.a.ii Annual Limit

**Definition**: Maximum amount insurer pays per policy year.

**Common Annual Limits in Malaysia:**

| Plan Type | Annual Limit | Typical Premium (Monthly) | Suitability |
|-----------|--------------|--------------------------|-------------|
| **Basic** | RM50,000 - RM100,000 | RM100 - RM200 | Budget-conscious, young, healthy |
| **Standard** | RM150,000 - RM300,000 | RM250 - RM400 | Average needs, families |
| **Comprehensive** | RM500,000 - RM1,000,000 | RM500 - RM800 | High coverage needs, chronic conditions risk |
| **Premium** | RM1,000,000+ | RM800+ | Maximum protection, serious illness risk |

⚠️ **EXAM TIP**: Higher annual limit = Higher premium, but better protection against catastrophic medical costs.

---

#### 5.2.a.iii Lifetime Limit

**Definition**: Maximum total amount insurer pays over entire policy lifetime.

**Modern Trend**: Many insurers now offer **NO LIFETIME LIMIT** or very high limits (RM5-10 million).

**Why Important:**
- Chronic diseases require ongoing treatment
- Multiple hospitalizations over lifetime
- Major illnesses (cancer) can exceed annual limits multiple times

---

#### 5.2.a.iv Exclusions

**Common Exclusions** (MUST MEMORIZE):

| Exclusion Type | Examples | Rationale |
|---------------|----------|-----------|
| **Pre-existing Conditions** | Illnesses diagnosed before policy | Adverse selection prevention |
| **Cosmetic Surgery** | Elective procedures for appearance | Not medically necessary |
| **Normal Pregnancy** | Standard childbirth | Not illness/injury |
| **Dental Treatment** | Unless accident-related | Separate dental coverage |
| **War/Riot Injuries** | Conflict-related injuries | Uninsurable/unpredictable |
| **Self-inflicted Injuries** | Suicide attempts, self-harm | Moral hazard |
| **HIV/AIDS** | Some policies exclude | Risk management |
| **Alternative Medicine** | Traditional/complementary therapies | Unless specified |
| **Experimental Treatments** | Unproven procedures | Medical necessity |
| **Routine Checkups** | Preventive health screenings | Not treatment for illness |

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: "Normal pregnancy" is excluded, but **complications** of pregnancy may be covered.

---

#### 5.2.a.vi Waiting Periods

**Definition**: Time period after policy inception before certain benefits payable.

**Types of Waiting Periods:**

| Type | Duration | Purpose | Applies To |
|------|----------|---------|------------|
| **Initial Waiting Period** | 30 days | Prevent immediate claims after purchase | All illnesses (except accidents) |
| **Specific Illness Waiting Period** | 120 days | High-cost conditions | Specified illnesses only |
| **Pre-existing Condition** | 12-36 months OR permanent exclusion | Prevent adverse selection | Known conditions |

**Example:**
```
Policy Start Date: January 1, 2025

Accident (Feb 1): ✅ COVERED (no waiting for accidents)
Flu (Feb 15): ❌ NOT COVERED (within 30-day initial waiting)
Surgery for hernia (March 1): ✅ COVERED (after 30 days, not specified illness)
Cancer treatment (April 1): ❌ NOT COVERED (within 120-day specific illness waiting)
Cancer treatment (June 1): ✅ COVERED (after 120 days for specified illness)
```

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: **Accidents** have NO waiting period; coverage is immediate.

---

#### 5.2.a.vii Pre-existing Illness

**Definition**: Medical condition that existed BEFORE policy purchase, whether diagnosed or not.

**Three Approaches:**

**Approach 1: Permanent Exclusion**
```
Pre-existing: Diabetes diagnosed before policy
Coverage: Never covered throughout policy life
```

**Approach 2: Waiting Period (12-36 months)**
```
Pre-existing: Hypertension known before policy
Waiting: Not covered for 24 months
After 24 months: ✅ Covered for hypertension
```

**Approach 3: Loaded Premium**
```
Pre-existing: Heart condition
Coverage: Covered from start
Premium: Higher premium charged
```

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Most policies **EXCLUDE** pre-existing conditions permanently or impose long waiting periods.

---

#### 5.2.a.viii Reasonable and Customary Charges (R&C)

**Definition**: Amount insurer deems reasonable for specific treatment in specific geographic area.

**How R&C is Determined:**
1. Survey of charges in the area
2. Medical necessity of treatment
3. Complexity of procedure
4. Doctor's qualifications/experience
5. Hospital classification

**Example Scenario:**
```
Treatment: Appendectomy in Kuala Lumpur
Hospital A Bill: RM15,000
Hospital B Bill: RM12,000  
Hospital C Bill: RM10,000
Insurer's R&C Assessment: RM12,000 (median/average)

If you go to Hospital A:
Your Bill: RM15,000
Insurer Pays (R&C): RM12,000
You Pay: RM3,000 (excess over R&C)
```

---

#### 5.2.a.xi Cashless Admission Facility

**Definition**: Direct billing between hospital and insurer; policyholder doesn't pay upfront.

**How It Works:**

**Traditional Method (Reimbursement):**
```
Step 1: Hospital bills patient → Patient pays RM50,000
Step 2: Patient submits claim to insurer
Step 3: Insurer reimburses patient RM50,000
Issue: Need upfront cash
```

**Cashless Method:**
```
Step 1: Hospital bills insurer directly → Patient pays RM0 upfront
Step 2: Insurer pays hospital RM50,000
Step 3: Patient only pays excess (if any)
Benefit: No upfront payment needed
```

---

#### 5.2.a.xiii Medical Inflation

**Definition**: Rate at which medical costs increase annually.

**Malaysian Context:**

| Type | Rate | Impact |
|------|------|--------|
| **General Inflation** | 3-5% annually | Overall price increases |
| **Medical Inflation** | 10-15% annually | Healthcare costs rise faster |

**Why Medical Inflation Higher:**
- Advanced technology costs
- New expensive treatments
- Specialized care demand
- Pharmaceutical costs
- Aging population

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Choose policies with **inflation-adjusted** annual limits to maintain coverage value.

---

#### 5.2.a.xiv Major Medical Insurance

**Definition**: High-limit insurance for catastrophic health events; bridges gap that basic plans don't cover.

**Key Features:**

| Feature | Description | Modern Trend |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| **Extensive Coverage** | Broad range of medical expenses | Comprehensive |
| **High Maximum Limits** | RM1 million+ | Now RM1-2 million common |
| **Top-Up Options** | Secondary riders to increase limit | Add RM500,000-1 million more |
| **Flexibility** | Often paired with hospitalization plans | Customizable |
| **Deductibles** | Predetermined amount before insurer pays | Various options |
| **Co-insurance** | Cost-sharing mechanism | Percentage split |

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: Major Medical Insurance is classified under **Medical Reimbursement** category.

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 6** *(Links to Section 5.2.a)*

**Question**: "All of following are health insurance schemes in Malaysia, EXCEPT:
A. Critical illnesses insurance
B. Funeral insurance
C. Major medical insurance
D. Hospital and surgical insurance"

**Answer**: B (Funeral insurance)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **Funeral insurance** pays a benefit when someone **DIES** - that's **LIFE INSURANCE**, not health insurance. 

**DISTINCTION TO REMEMBER**:
- **Health Insurance** = Covers **TREATMENT** while ALIVE (illness, injury, hospitalization)
- **Life Insurance** = Pays on **DEATH** (funeral, estate, beneficiaries)

**KEYWORDS**:
- ✅ Critical illness = **Health** (pays on diagnosis during life)
- ✅ Major medical = **Health** (pays for treatment)
- ✅ Hospital & surgical = **Health** (pays for hospitalization)
- ❌ Funeral = **Life** (pays on death)

### 5.2.b CRITICAL ILLNESS (DREAD DISEASE) INSURANCE

**Origin**: 1980s in South Africa; expanded globally including Malaysia.

**Definition**: Lump sum payment upon diagnosis of specified critical illnesses.

---

#### 5.2.b.i Coverage and Policy Types

**Common Covered Illnesses** (Malaysian Context):

| Category | Illnesses Typically Covered | LIAM Guidelines |
|----------|----------------------------|-----------------|
| **Cancer** | Major cancers (all stages) | ✅ Standard |
| **Heart Conditions** | Heart attack, coronary artery bypass surgery | ✅ Standard |
| **Stroke** | Cerebrovascular accident with permanent symptoms | ✅ Standard |
| **Kidney Failure** | End-stage renal failure requiring dialysis | ✅ Standard |
| **Major Organ Transplant** | Heart, lung, liver, kidney, pancreas, bone marrow | ✅ Standard |
| **Neurodegenerative** | Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, multiple sclerosis | Extended coverage |
| **Others** | Paralysis, blindness, deafness, coma | Extended coverage |

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: Life Insurance Association of Malaysia (LIAM) provides guideline list, but coverage VARIES between insurers.

---

**Policy Structure Options:**

**1. Standalone Critical Illness Policy**
```
Function: Independent policy, not attached to life insurance
Premium: Separate premium payment
Payout: Lump sum on diagnosis
Death Benefit: Only if death from covered illness within survival period
```

**2. Critical Illness Rider (Attached to Life Policy)**
```
Function: Attached to life insurance policy
Premium: Lower than standalone (shared with life policy)
Payout: Two sub-types:
  a) Accelerated Benefit: Reduces life insurance death benefit
  b) Non-Accelerated (Additional): Separate payout, doesn't affect death benefit
```

---

#### 5.2.b.ii Policy Features for Financial Advisors

**Feature 1: Single vs. Multiple Claim Policies**

| Type | Description | Example | Suitability |
|------|-------------|---------|-------------|
| **Single Claim** | Pays once then terminates | Cancer diagnosed → RM200,000 paid → Policy ends | Lower cost, basic protection |
| **Multiple Claim** | Allows additional claims for different illnesses | Heart attack → RM200,000 → Policy continues → Later: Stroke → Another RM200,000 | Higher cost, comprehensive protection |

**Multiple Claim Conditions:**
- Different illness category
- Waiting period between claims (e.g., 1-3 years)
- May have limit on total claims (e.g., 3 claims maximum)

---

**Feature 2: Coverage for Early to Advanced Stages**

**Traditional:** Only pays for advanced/late-stage illness
**Modern:** Staged benefits

**Example:**
```
Total Sum Assured: RM200,000

Early Stage Cancer (Stage 1):
Payout: 25% = RM50,000
Remaining Coverage: RM150,000

Intermediate Stage Cancer (Stage 2-3):
Payout: 50% = RM100,000
Remaining Coverage: RM50,000

Advanced Stage Cancer (Stage 4):
Payout: 100% = RM200,000 (full remaining amount)
```

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Early/intermediate stage benefits provide financial support for treatment sooner.

---

**Feature 3: Waiting and Survival Periods**

**Waiting Period:**
```
Definition: Time after policy inception before claims can be made
Typical: 90-120 days
Exception: Accidents have NO waiting period

Example:
Policy Date: Jan 1, 2025
Cancer diagnosed: March 1, 2025 (Within 90 days)
Result: NOT COVERED (within waiting period)

Cancer diagnosed: May 1, 2025 (After 90 days)
Result: ✅ COVERED
```

**Survival Period:**
```
Definition: Time insured must survive after diagnosis to qualify for payout
Typical: 14-30 days
Purpose: Confirm illness severity

Example:
Diagnosis: Heart attack on June 1
Survival Period: 14 days
Must survive until: June 15
Payout: If alive on June 15 ✅
If dies June 10: Survival period not met ❌ (but life insurance pays instead if rider)
```

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: Both waiting period (before diagnosis) and survival period (after diagnosis) must be met.

---

**Feature 4: Accelerated vs. Non-Accelerated Benefits**

**Accelerated Benefit (More Common):**
```
Setup:
Life Insurance Sum Assured: RM500,000
CI Rider Sum Assured: RM200,000

Scenario: Cancer diagnosed
CI Payout: RM200,000 ✅
Remaining Life Insurance: RM500,000 - RM200,000 = RM300,000

If later dies:
Death Benefit: RM300,000 only
```

**Non-Accelerated Benefit (Additional Benefit):**
```
Setup:
Life Insurance Sum Assured: RM500,000
CI Rider Sum Assured: RM200,000 (Additional)

Scenario: Cancer diagnosed
CI Payout: RM200,000 ✅
Life Insurance: RM500,000 (UNCHANGED)

If later dies:
Death Benefit: RM500,000 (full amount)
Total Received: RM200,000 (CI) + RM500,000 (Death) = RM700,000
```

📌 **EXAM TIP**: 
- **Accelerated** = CI reduces death benefit (more common, lower premium)
- **Non-Accelerated** = CI additional to death benefit (less common, higher premium)

---

### 5.2.c LONG-TERM CARE (LTC) INSURANCE

**Global Context**: 
- USA: 40% of elderly need nursing home care; 60% by age 75
- Malaysia: Underdeveloped market, growing need due to aging population

**Definition**: Covers costs when individuals cannot perform daily activities independently due to illness, disability, or cognitive impairment.

---

#### 5.2.c.i Understanding Long-term Care

**Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) - CRITICAL CONCEPT:**

| ADL | Description | Example |
|-----|-------------|---------|
| **Bathing** | Ability to wash oneself | Can shower independently |
| **Dressing** | Ability to put on clothes | Can dress without help |
| **Toileting** | Ability to use bathroom | Can use toilet independently |
| **Transferring** | Ability to move from bed to chair | Can walk/move without assistance |
| **Continence** | Bladder and bowel control | Can control bodily functions |
| **Feeding** | Ability to eat independently | Can feed self |

**LTC Trigger**: Typically inability to perform 2-3 ADLs independently.

---

#### 5.2.c.ii Types of Care Covered

| Care Type | Setting | Services | Malaysian Context |
|-----------|---------|----------|-------------------|
| **Nursing Home Care** | Nursing facility | 24-hour skilled nursing | Limited facilities in Malaysia |
| **Assisted Living** | Assisted living facility | Help with ADLs, medication management | Growing in urban areas |
| **Home Care Services** | Patient's home | Skilled nursing, PT, OT at home | Preferred in Malaysian culture |
| **Adult Day-care** | Day-care center | Supervision, social activities, ADL assistance | Limited availability |
| **Respite Care** | Various | Temporary relief for family caregivers | Important for family support |

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 10** *(Links to Section 5.2.c)*

**Question**: "Coverage of long-term care insurance includes:
i. Nursing home care
ii. Home care services
iii. Social services
iv. Assisted living facilities

A. i & ii only
B. i, ii & iii only
C. i, ii & iv only
D. All the above"

**Answer**: C (i, ii & iv only)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: Option (iii) "Social services" is the TRAP! 

**Why "Social services" NOT covered:**
- **Too BROAD** and general
- LTC covers **SPECIFIC HEALTHCARE** settings/services
- Social services include non-medical support (welfare, community programs, social work)
- LTC focuses on **MEDICAL/HEALTHCARE** support for ADLs

**WHAT IS COVERED**:
- ✅ **Nursing home** = Medical care facility (24-hour skilled nursing)
- ✅ **Home care** = Healthcare services at home (nursing, PT, OT)
- ✅ **Assisted living** = Residential facility with ADL support & medication management
- ❌ **Social services** = Too general, not healthcare-specific

**MEMORY TIP**: LTC covers **MEDICAL/HEALTHCARE** care settings, not general social welfare programs.

### 5.2.d HOSPITAL INCOME INSURANCE

**Definition**: Daily cash benefit for each day hospitalized; NOT reimbursement of actual costs.

**Key Distinction:**
- Medical Reimbursement: Pays **actual bills**
- Hospital Income: Pays **fixed daily amount** regardless of actual costs

---

#### Key Features

**Daily Cash Benefit Structure:**

**Example:**
```
Policy: RM200/day hospital income
Hospitalization: 5 days for illness
Actual Hospital Bill: RM30,000 (paid by medical insurance or self)
Hospital Income Payout: RM200 × 5 = RM1,000 (regardless of actual bill)
Use: Can use RM1,000 for any purpose (loss of income, transport, childcare, etc.)
```

**Flexibility of Use:**
- Cover household expenses
- Compensate for lost income
- Transportation costs
- Childcare during hospitalization
- Meals for family members
- Any other needs

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Hospital Income provides **cash flow** during hospitalization, complementing medical reimbursement insurance.

### 5.3 DISABILITY INCOME INSURANCE

**Also Called**: Permanent Health Insurance ✅ (This is the exam term!)

**Definition**: Replaces portion of income lost due to disability from illness or injury.

---

#### 5.3.a.i The Essence of Disability Income Insurance

**Purpose**: Provide monthly benefits to replace lost income when unable to work due to disability.

**Key Points:**
- Does NOT cover medical expenses (that's medical insurance)
- Covers **INCOME LOSS** only
- Typically replaces 50-70% of income (not 100%)
- Prevents over-insurance and malingering

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 1** *(Links to Section 5.3)*

**Question**: "Disability Income Insurance is also called ___.
A. Disability care insurance
B. Total disability insurance
C. Permanent health insurance
D. Income loss insurance"

**Answer**: C (Permanent Health Insurance)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **"Permanent Health Insurance"** is British/traditional terminology for disability income insurance.

**WHY "Permanent Health"?**
- "Permanent" = Long-term income protection (not temporary)
- "Health" = Health-related inability to work
- Used in UK, Malaysia, and former British colonies

**TERMINOLOGY VARIATIONS**:
- UK/Malaysia: **Permanent Health Insurance** ✅
- USA: Disability Income Insurance
- Modern: Both terms used interchangeably

**EXAM TIP**: When you see "Permanent Health Insurance" in exam, immediately think **DISABILITY INCOME** replacement.

---

#### 5.3.a.ii Definition of Total Disability

**TWO Main Approaches:**

**1. Own Occupation (More Liberal):**
```
Definition: Unable to perform duties of YOUR SPECIFIC occupation
Example: Surgeon who loses hand dexterity
Result: Considered totally disabled (can't do surgery)
Even if: Could work in another field
```

**2. Any Occupation (More Restrictive):**
```
Definition: Unable to perform duties of ANY occupation for which reasonably qualified
Example: Surgeon who loses hand dexterity
Result: NOT considered totally disabled if can do admin work, teaching, etc.
Only disabled if: Can't do ANY job at all
```

📌 **EXAM TIP**: "Own Occupation" is MORE FAVORABLE to policyholder but has HIGHER premiums.

---

**Income-Loss Approach (Modern Definition):**

**Focus**: Loss of income of the insured (not job performance)

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 2** *(Links to Section 5.3.a.ii)*

**Question**: "How does income-loss approach define disability?
A. Focuses on loss of income of insured
B. Focuses on level of job performance
C. Focuses on hurt to family
D. Focuses on length and severity"

**Answer**: A (Focuses on loss of income)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: The name says it all: **INCOME-LOSS** approach!

**MODERN vs TRADITIONAL**:
- **Traditional**: Can you do your job? (job duties focus)
- **Income-Loss**: How much income have you lost? (financial impact focus)

**EXAMPLE TO UNDERSTAND**:
```
Traditional Approach:
Question: Can surgeon perform surgery?
Answer: No → Disabled

Income-Loss Approach:
Question: How much income lost?
Before: RM15,000/month (surgery)
Now: RM7,500/month (teaching/consulting)
Answer: 50% income loss → Partial disability benefit based on loss percentage
```

**KEYWORDS**: **"Income-loss"** = Financial focus, not job duty focus

---

#### 5.3.a.iii Variations in Definition

**Defining Injury:**
- **Accidental bodily harm**
- Must be unforeseen
- NOT self-inflicted
- Differentiates accidental vs. deliberate

**Defining Sickness:**
- Manifests AFTER policy in effect
- Excludes pre-existing conditions
- Diagnosed and treated during active policy period

---

#### 5.3.a.iv Comprehensive Overview - Partial and Residual Disability

**Partial Disability:**

```
Definition: Inability to perform SOME (but not all) duties of occupation
Typical Benefit: 50% of total disability monthly indemnity
Duration: Up to 6 months
Requirement: Usually follows period of total disability

Example:
Total Disability Benefit: RM3,000/month
Partial Disability Benefit: RM1,500/month (50%)
Maximum Duration: 6 months
```

---

**Residual Disability:**

```
Definition: Can perform some work but suffers loss of income
Benefit: PROPORTIONATE to income loss
Formula: (Loss of Income / Prior Income) × Monthly Indemnity
```

**🔢 CRITICAL FORMULA (MUST MEMORIZE):**

```
Residual Indemnity = (Loss of Income / Prior Income) × Monthly Indemnity
```

**Example Calculation:**
```
Prior Income: RM3,000/month
Current Income: RM1,500/month (due to disability)
Loss of Income: RM3,000 - RM1,500 = RM1,500
Monthly Indemnity: RM2,500

Residual Indemnity = (RM1,500 / RM3,000) × RM2,500
                   = 0.5 × RM2,500
                   = RM1,250/month
```

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Residual disability formula is HIGH PROBABILITY exam question. Practice calculations!

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 8** *(Links to Section 5.3)*

**Question**: "Following statements regarding disability income insurance are incorrect, EXCEPT:
A. Provides monthly benefits to policyholders who lose jobs due to illness/accidents, replacing portion of lost income
B. Typically covers 100% of policyholder's income
C. Also known as critical illness insurance
D. Presumptive disability = completely unable to perform occupation"

**Answer**: A (Correct statement)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: Analyze each statement carefully:

**A** ✅ **CORRECT** - This is the accurate definition:
- **"Monthly benefits"** = Regular income replacement ✅
- **"Portion"** = 50-70%, NOT 100% ✅
- **"Lost income"** = Focus on income loss ✅

**B** ❌ WRONG - **NOT 100%**!
- Typical: **50-70%** of income only
- Why not 100%? Prevent malingering (faking disability to avoid work)

**C** ❌ WRONG - Completely different products:
- **Disability Income** = Monthly income replacement when can't work
- **Critical Illness** = Lump sum on diagnosis of specific disease
- NOT the same!

**D** ❌ WRONG - Misunderstands presumptive disability:
- **Presumptive Disability** = Specific losses (e.g., loss of 2 limbs, sight in both eyes, hearing in both ears) that AUTOMATICALLY qualify as total disability
- Doesn't require proving can't work
- Even if COULD work, still considered totally disabled

**KEYWORDS TO REMEMBER**:
- "Monthly benefits" ✅
- "Portion" (not 100%) ✅
- "Lost income" ✅

### 5.4 Computing Disability Income Need

**Purpose**: Calculate appropriate coverage amount for disability income insurance.

**Approach**: Similar to Capital Conservation from Chapter 3.

**🔢 FORMULA:**

```
Capital Needed = Annual Income to Replace / Interest Rate

OR

Capital = A / r
```

**Where:**
- A = Annual income that needs to be replaced
- r = Expected investment return rate (as decimal)

---

**Example 1:**
```
Current Monthly Income: RM6,000
Annual Income: RM6,000 × 12 = RM72,000
Income to Replace: 60% = RM43,200 (typical replacement ratio)
Interest Rate: 6% = 0.06

Capital Needed = RM43,200 / 0.06 = RM720,000

Interpretation: Need RM720,000 capital which, invested at 6%, generates RM43,200 annually to replace lost income.
```

---

**Factors to Consider:**

| Factor | Consideration | Impact |
|--------|---------------|--------|
| **Replacement Ratio** | Typically 50-70%, not 100% | Lower than actual income to prevent malingering |
| **Existing Coverage** | EPF, SOCSO, group insurance | Reduce individual need |
| **Expenses** | Fixed expenses continue during disability | May need full amount |
| **Savings** | Emergency fund available | Can supplement |
| **Family Support** | Dual income household | May reduce need |

- **Disclosure** = Full, clear information about product
- **Transparency** = Honest, visible pricing and terms

**WHY DISCLOSURE REQUIREMENTS EXIST**:
1. Complex products (hard for average person to understand)
2. Information asymmetry (insurer knows more than buyer)
3. Long-term commitments (decades of premiums)
4. High stakes (health coverage needs)

**WHAT REGULATION REQUIRES**:
- Clear explanation of benefits ✅
- Visible exclusions/limitations ✅
- Premium rate tables ✅
- Historical premium increases ✅
- Sample policy contracts ✅
- 30-day BNM pre-launch review ✅

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Disclosure" = Make information known
- "Transparency" = Clear and visible
- "Prevent mis-selling" = Consumer protection goal

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 3** *(Links to Section 5.0 - Health Concerns)*

**Question**: "Heddy Chan worried about health. Main concerns if health fails?
i. Escalating costs of medical treatment
ii. Lost EPF contributions
iii. Loss of income"

**Answer**: C (i & iii only)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: Distinguish between **PRIMARY** and **SECONDARY** concerns!

**PRIMARY CONCERNS (IMMEDIATE & MAJOR)**:
- ✅ **(i) Medical costs** - Immediate, huge financial burden
  - Surgery: RM50,000-300,000+
  - Hospitalization: RM1,000+ per day
  - Treatment: Ongoing expenses
  - **Direct impact on finances NOW**

- ✅ **(iii) Income loss** - Can't work, no money coming in
  - Family support needs continue
  - Bills still need to be paid
  - Lifestyle affected immediately
  - **Loss of earning capacity**

**SECONDARY CONCERN (INDIRECT)**:
- ❌ **(ii) EPF contributions** - Yes, contributions stop, but:
  - Not an immediate crisis
  - EPF is for retirement (future)
  - Can be addressed later
  - Not a primary worry when facing health crisis

**ANALOGY**:
```
House on fire (health crisis):
- Primary concern: Put out fire (medical costs), save family (income for support)
- Secondary concern: Missing next month's house insurance payment (EPF contributions)
```

**EXAM TIP**: When question asks for **MAIN/PRIMARY** concerns, focus on **IMMEDIATE, DIRECT, CRITICAL** impacts.

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 4** *(Links to Section 5.0)*

**Question**: "Following insurance classified as health insurance, EXCEPT:
A. Accident insurance
B. Disability insurance
C. Life insurance
D. Sickness insurance"

**Answer**: C (Life insurance)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **Simple but crucial distinction**!

**HEALTH INSURANCE**:
- Purpose: Pay for **TREATMENT** of illness/injury
- Trigger: **ILLNESS or INJURY** occurs
- Payout: **WHILE ALIVE**
- Examples:
  - ✅ Accident insurance (treatment of injuries)
  - ✅ Disability insurance (income when can't work due to health)
  - ✅ Sickness insurance (treatment of illnesses)

**LIFE INSURANCE**:
- Purpose: Financial protection for **BENEFICIARIES**
- Trigger: **DEATH** of insured
- Payout: **AFTER DEATH**
- Example:
  - ❌ Life insurance (pays on death, not for treatment)

**MEMORY TIP**:
```
HEALTH = ALIVE + TREATMENT
LIFE = DEATH + BENEFICIARIES
```

**COMMON CONFUSION**: Some think life insurance is health-related because:
- Can die from illness
- May have health requirements to qualify
- BUT: Payout is on **DEATH**, not for health treatment!
