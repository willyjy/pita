Chapter 7: General Insurance Products in Insurance Planning

Comprehensive Exam Preparation Guide with Question Mapping

📚 Chapter Overview

General insurance protects against financial losses from specific risks. Unlike life insurance (person), general insurance covers PROPERTY, LIABILITY, and specific PERILS. This chapter covers motor, property, marine, liability, engineering insurance and more.

Exam Weight: 12-15% of total exam
Study Time Recommendation: 7-9 hours

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you must be able to:

  1. Explain three core general insurance principles (Indemnity, Contribution, Subrogation)
  2. Distinguish motor insurance types (Act, Third Party, Comprehensive)
  3. Identify property insurance types (Fire, All Risk, Business Interruption)
  4. Understand liability insurance categories
  5. Explain engineering insurance (CAR, EAR, Machinery Breakdown)
  6. Know bonds types (Tender, Performance, Advance Payment)
  7. Apply general insurance in financial planning

7.1 Three Core Principles of General Insurance

CRITICAL FRAMEWORK (HIGH EXAM FREQUENCY):

THREE FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES
├── 1. INDEMNITY (Restore to original position)
├── 2. CONTRIBUTION (Share among insurers)
└── 3. SUBROGATION (Recover from third party)

Principle 1: INDEMNITY

Definition: Restore insured to financial position BEFORE loss - no profit, no loss.

Key Points: - Compensation = ACTUAL LOSS only - Cannot profit from insurance - Prevents unjust enrichment - Based on replacement/repair cost

Example:

House damaged by fire:
Original value: RM200,000
Damage: RM50,000 repairs needed

Insurance pays: RM50,000 (actual loss)
NOT: RM200,000 (full value)
NOT: RM60,000 (more than loss)

Result: House restored to pre-loss condition

What Indemnity Means: - Cannot claim more than loss - Cannot rebuild better than before - Cannot upgrade using insurance money - Return to SAME condition, not improved

📌 EXAM TIP: Indemnity = Compensate ACTUAL loss, no profit for insured.

Principle 2: CONTRIBUTION

Definition: When multiple policies cover same risk, insurers share claim proportionally.

Key Points: - Can have multiple policies on same asset - Cannot claim MORE than loss from all policies combined - Each insurer pays proportionate share - Prevents over-compensation

Formula:

Contribution = (Policy A Sum / Total Sum Insured) × Loss

Example:
Fire destroys warehouse worth RM500,000
Policy A: RM300,000
Policy B: RM200,000
Total: RM500,000

Loss: RM100,000

Policy A pays: (RM300,000 / RM500,000) × RM100,000 = RM60,000
Policy B pays: (RM200,000 / RM500,000) × RM100,000 = RM40,000
Total: RM100,000

Example Scenario:

Car insured with:
Insurer A: RM80,000
Insurer B: RM60,000
Total: RM140,000

Accident damage: RM28,000

Insurer A pays: (80/140) × 28,000 = RM16,000
Insurer B pays: (60/140) × 28,000 = RM12,000
Total: RM28,000 (actual loss)

📌 EXAM TIP: Contribution ensures fair sharing among multiple insurers - no double compensation.

Principle 3: SUBROGATION

Definition: After paying claim, insurer can recover from responsible third party.

Key Points: - Insurer “steps into shoes” of insured - Can sue third party for recovery - Only after insured fully compensated - Prevents insured from double recovery

When Applies: - Third party caused the loss - Third party legally liable - After insurer pays claim - Insured must cooperate

Example:

Your car hit by negligent driver:
Damage: RM20,000

STEP 1: Your insurer pays you RM20,000 (you're made whole)
STEP 2: Your insurer sues negligent driver (subrogation)
STEP 3: Insurer recovers RM20,000 from driver
STEP 4: Insurer keeps recovery (recoups claim payment)

You benefit: Immediate payment without lawsuit
Insurer benefit: Recovers claim cost
Negligent party: Pays for damage caused

Important: - Does NOT apply to life insurance (non-indemnity) - Insured cannot compromise third party’s liability - Insured must assist insurer in recovery

📌 EXAM TIP: Subrogation = Insurer’s right to recover from liable third party AFTER paying claim.

7.3 MOTOR INSURANCE

7.3.1 Legal Requirement (Road Transport Act 1987)

Mandatory: ALL vehicles on public roads MUST have motor insurance.

Minimum: Third-party coverage (Act Cover)

Penalties for Non-Compliance: - Fine: Up to RM1,000 - Imprisonment: Up to 3 months - Both fine AND imprisonment - License disqualification: 12 months

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Motor insurance is COMPULSORY under RTA 1987.

7.3.2 Types of Motor Insurance Coverage

FOUR Coverage Levels (MEMORIZE):

Coverage Type What’s Covered Premium Common Use
1. Act Cover Third-party death/injury ONLY Lowest Rarely written
2. Third Party Act + Third-party property damage Low Budget option
3. Third Party Fire & Theft (TPFT) Third Party + Own vehicle fire/theft Medium Mid-range
4. Comprehensive All above + Own vehicle accident damage Highest Most complete

Detailed Comparison:

COVERAGE LEVELS (Lowest → Highest)

ACT COVER (Minimum Legal):
✅ Third-party death/bodily injury
❌ Third-party property damage
❌ Own vehicle damage
❌ Fire/theft

THIRD PARTY:
✅ Third-party death/injury
✅ Third-party property damage
❌ Own vehicle damage
❌ Fire/theft

THIRD PARTY FIRE & THEFT:
✅ All Third Party coverage
✅ Own vehicle fire damage
✅ Own vehicle theft
❌ Own vehicle accident damage

COMPREHENSIVE:
✅ All TPFT coverage
✅ Own vehicle accident damage
✅ Most complete protection

Example Scenarios:

Scenario 1: You hit another car
Third Party: Covers other car damage ✅
Comprehensive: Covers other car + YOUR car ✅

Scenario 2: Your car stolen
Third Party: NO coverage ❌
TPFT: Covers theft ✅
Comprehensive: Covers theft ✅

Scenario 3: You hit tree (own fault)
Third Party: NO coverage ❌
TPFT: NO coverage ❌
Comprehensive: Covers your car ✅

📌 EXAM TIP: Comprehensive = Everything covered; Third Party = Only others’ loss; TPFT = TP + Fire + Theft.

Type Description Example

7.3.4 E-Hailing Insurance

Special Category: For Grab, Uber, etc.

Covers: - Enhanced liability (transporting passengers for hire) - Increased risks of passenger transport - Higher usage/mileage

Required: Cannot use personal car insurance for commercial e-hailing.

7.3.5 No Claim Discount (NCD)

Definition: Discount for not making claims.

Accumulation: - Year 1 no claim: 25% NCD - Year 2 no claim: 30% NCD - Year 3 no claim: 38.33% NCD - Year 4 no claim: 45% NCD - Year 5+ no claim: 55% NCD (maximum)

Transfer: Can transfer NCD to new vehicle if same owner.

📝 EXAM QUESTION 10 (Links to Section 7.3.5 - NCD)

Question: “To capitalize on no claim discount for motor cover, the following must be performed: A. Increase in premium as mandated must be paid B. No claim discount applies beyond basic premium C. Evidence of entitlement from previous insurer must be obtained ✅ D. All the above”

Answer: C (Evidence of entitlement required)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Must PROVE NCD entitlement from previous insurer!

ANALYSIS:

C ✅ CORRECT: - Must obtain NCD certificate from previous insurer - Proves claim-free record - Required for NCD transfer - Cannot just claim verbally

Example:

Switching insurers:
Old Insurer: 3 years no claim (38.33% NCD)
New Insurer: Requires NCD certificate from old insurer
Without certificate: Start from 0% NCD
With certificate: Continue with 38.33% NCD

A ❌ Wrong: - NCD = DISCOUNT (reduces premium) - Not an increase in premium - Opposite concept

B ❌ Misleading: - NCD applies TO the premium (reduces it) - Not “beyond” basic premium - Unclear statement

KEYWORDS: - “Evidence of entitlement” = NCD certificate - “Previous insurer” = Transfer proof - “Must be obtained” = Required documentation

MEMORY TIP: “No Proof, No Discount” (need certificate)

7.3.6 Motor Insurance Premium Liberalization (2017)

Key Change: From tariffed rates to risk-based pricing.

Details: - Insurers can set own premiums based on risk assessment - Factors: Driving record, vehicle type, usage, location - Promotes competition - Encourages safe driving - Minimum premium: RM20 (still applicable for tariff products)

7.3.7 Common Motor Insurance Exclusions

What’s NOT Covered: - Wear and tear - Mechanical/electrical breakdown - Driving under influence of alcohol/drugs - Unauthorized drivers - Use for illegal purposes - Negligent maintenance - Consequential losses

7.4 NON-MOTOR INSURANCE

7.4.A PROPERTY INSURANCE

Five Main Types:

  1. Fire and Special Perils
  2. All Risk Insurance
  3. Business Interruption/Consequential Loss
  4. Houseowners Insurance
  5. Burglary Insurance
7.4.A.I Fire and Special Perils Insurance

Covers: - Fire damage - Lightning - Explosion/implosion - Aircraft damage - Impact by vehicles - Riot, strike, malicious damage

Excludes: - War, terrorism - Nuclear risks - Wear and tear - Consequential losses (lost profits)

Example:

Factory catches fire:
Building damage: RM500,000 ✅ Covered
Machinery damage: RM200,000 ✅ Covered
Lost profits (3 months): RM300,000 ❌ NOT covered (need Business Interruption)
7.4.A.III Business Interruption/Consequential Loss Insurance

Purpose: Cover lost profits due to property damage.

Covers: - Lost gross profit - Increased cost of working - Fixed costs continuing during interruption

Trigger: Must be property damage covered by underlying policy (e.g., fire).

📝 EXAM QUESTION 2 (Links to Section 7.4.A.III - Business Interruption)

Question: “Mr. Hew compensated when machine broke down. Amount covers loss of profits due to production interruption. What policy? A. Machinery Breakdown (Loss of Profit) Policy ✅ B. Fire (Consequential Loss) Policy C. Machinery & Equipment Policy D. Liability (Loss of Profit) Policy”

Answer: A (Machinery Breakdown Loss of Profit)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Loss of PROFITS from MACHINERY breakdown = Specific policy!

ANALYSIS:

A ✅ CORRECT: - Machinery Breakdown (Loss of Profit) Policy - Covers lost profits when machinery breaks down - Consequential loss from equipment failure - Exactly what question describes

B ❌ Wrong trigger: - Fire (Consequential Loss) = Lost profits from FIRE - Not machinery breakdown - Different peril

C ❌ Wrong coverage: - Machinery & Equipment = PHYSICAL damage to machine - Does NOT cover lost profits - Only repair/replacement costs

D ❌ No such policy: - Liability insurance covers legal liability to others - Not loss of profits from own machinery

POLICY MATCHING:

PHYSICAL Damage Coverage:
Machinery & Equipment Policy = Repair/replace machine

CONSEQUENTIAL Coverage (Lost Profits):
Machinery Breakdown (Loss of Profit) = Income loss from breakdown

Must have BOTH for complete protection!

KEYWORDS: - “Loss of profits” = Consequential coverage - “Machine broke down” = Machinery breakdown trigger - “Interruption of production” = Business interruption

MEMORY TIP: “Machine breaks → Profit lost → Loss of Profit Policy”

7.4.A.IV Houseowners vs Householders Insurance

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:

Aspect Houseowners Householders
Insured Property OWNER TENANT/Occupier
Building Coverage ✅ YES ❌ NO
Contents Coverage ✅ YES ✅ YES
Liability ✅ Owner’s liability ✅ Occupier’s liability

Example:

Mr. Lee OWNS house: Houseowners policy
- Building structure ✅
- Contents ✅
- Owner's liability ✅

Mr. Tan RENTS apartment: Householders policy
- Building ❌ (landlord's responsibility)
- Contents ✅
- Tenant's liability ✅

7.4.D LIABILITY INSURANCE

Purpose: Protect against legal liability to third parties.

Main Types:

  1. Public Liability - General public/visitors
  2. Product Liability - Defective products
  3. Professional Indemnity - Professional errors
  4. Directors & Officers (D&O) - Management decisions
  5. Employer’s Liability - Employees (workplace accidents)
  6. Workmen’s Compensation - Statutory coverage for workmen
7.4.D.VI Employer’s Liability Insurance

Purpose: Cover employer’s legal liability for employee accidents.

What’s Covered: - Employer’s negligence - Breach of duty (safety measures) - Common law liability

What’s NOT Covered: - Employee’s own negligence (unless employer also negligent) - Statutory liability under Workmen’s Compensation Act

📝 EXAM QUESTION 7 (Links to Section 7.4.D.VI - Employer’s Liability)

Question: “Which falls under Employer’s Liability Policy? Employer’s liability for accidents to employee from: i. Employer’s personal negligence ii. Employer’s breach of duty (safety measures) iii. Employee’s own negligence

A. I only B. II only C. I & II only ✅ D. I & III only”

Answer: C (I & II only)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Employer’s Liability covers EMPLOYER’S fault, not employee’s own negligence!

ANALYSIS:

i. Employer’s personal negligence ✅ COVERED: - Employer failed to supervise properly - Employer gave unsafe instructions - Employer’s direct actions caused accident - EMPLOYER AT FAULT

ii. Employer’s breach of duty (safety) ✅ COVERED: - Failed to provide safe working environment - Inadequate safety equipment - No safety training - Poor maintenance - EMPLOYER FAILED DUTY OF CARE

iii. Employee’s own negligence ❌ NOT COVERED: - Employee was careless - Employee ignored safety rules - Employee’s own fault - NO EMPLOYER LIABILITY (employee responsible)

PRINCIPLE:

Employer's Liability Insurance:
Covers: Employer's FAULT ✅
Does NOT cover: Employee's FAULT ❌

Rationale: Employer only liable when employer at fault

EXAMPLE SCENARIOS:

Scenario 1: ✅ COVERED
Worker falls from height
Reason: Employer didn't provide safety harness
Result: EMPLOYER'S breach of duty (covered)

Scenario 2: ✅ COVERED
Worker injured by faulty machine
Reason: Employer failed to maintain equipment
Result: EMPLOYER'S negligence (covered)

Scenario 3: ❌ NOT COVERED
Worker ignores warning signs, gets injured
Reason: Worker's own negligence
No employer breach of duty
Result: NOT covered (employee at fault)

KEYWORDS: - “Employer’s negligence” = Employer at fault ✅ - “Employer’s breach of duty” = Employer failed obligation ✅ - “Employee’s own negligence” = Employee at fault ❌

MEMORY TIP: “Employer’s Liability = Employer’s FAULT only”

7.4.D.VII Workmen’s Compensation Insurance

Legal Requirement: Employers MUST cover “workmen” without SOCSO.

Purpose: Statutory compensation for workplace injuries.

📝 EXAM QUESTION 6 (Links to Section 7.4.D.VII - Workmen’s Compensation)

Question: “Which TRUE under Workmen’s Compensation Policy as according to law? A. Employer must cover employees classified as ‘workmen’ and without SOCSO ✅ B. Employer must cover employees with SOCSO C. No legal requirement to cover workmen D. Fixed amount to cover all workmen”

Answer: A (Workmen without SOCSO must be covered)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Workmen’s Compensation is for employees NOT covered by SOCSO!

ANALYSIS:

A ✅ CORRECT: - Employers MUST insure “workmen” - BUT: Only if NOT covered by SOCSO - Two separate systems: - SOCSO = Social security (most employees) - Workmen’s Comp = Insurance (workmen not in SOCSO)

WHO ARE “WORKMEN”?: - Manual laborers - Certain categories of workers - Earning below specified amount - NOT covered by SOCSO

B ❌ Wrong - mutually exclusive: - If employee HAS SOCSO → NO Workmen’s Comp needed - SOCSO provides coverage already - Would be double coverage

C ❌ False - it IS required: - Workmen’s Compensation Act REQUIRES coverage - Legal obligation for employers - Penalties for non-compliance

D ❌ Not fixed amount: - Coverage varies by wages, risk category - Not a fixed amount for all

COVERAGE FRAMEWORK:

Employee Categories:

SOCSO-covered employees:
→ Protected by SOCSO ✅
→ NO Workmen's Comp needed ❌

"Workmen" without SOCSO:
→ NOT protected by SOCSO ❌
→ MUST have Workmen's Comp ✅

Purpose: Ensure ALL employees protected

KEYWORDS: - “Workmen” = Specific employee category - “Without SOCSO” = Gap that needs filling - “Must cover” = Legal requirement

MEMORY TIP: “No SOCSO → Need Workmen’s Comp” (ensure protection)

7.4.E ENGINEERING INSURANCE

Three Main Types:

  1. Contractor’s All Risks (CAR)
  2. Erection All Risks (EAR)
  3. Machinery Breakdown
7.4.E.I Contractor’s All Risks (CAR) Insurance

Purpose: Protect construction projects during building phase.

Covers: - Construction works - Materials on site - Equipment used - Third-party liability

Typical Projects: Buildings, bridges, roads, tunnels.

📝 EXAM QUESTION 1 (Links to Section 7.4.E.I - CAR Insurance)

Question: “Mr. Kanesan asks for policy covering faulty design/construction of machines and damage during installation. Which suitable? A. Machinery Breakdown Policy B. Contractor’s All Risks Policy ✅ C. Comprehensive Machine Policy D. Loss of Profit policy”

Answer: B (Contractor’s All Risks)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: During INSTALLATION = Construction phase = CAR policy!

ANALYSIS:

B ✅ CORRECT - CAR Policy: - “During installation” = Construction/erection phase ✅ - “Faulty design/construction” = Construction risks ✅ - Covers work in progress - Includes installation damage

A ❌ Machinery Breakdown: - For OPERATIONAL machines (after installation) - Not during installation/construction - Wrong phase

C ❌ Comprehensive Machine: - For machines IN USE (operational) - Not installation phase - Wrong coverage period

D ❌ Loss of Profit: - Consequential loss (lost income) - Not physical damage during construction - Wrong coverage type

PHASE MATCHING:

CONSTRUCTION PHASE (installing/building):
→ Contractor's All Risks (CAR) ✅
→ Erection All Risks (EAR) ✅

OPERATIONAL PHASE (after installed, in use):
→ Machinery Breakdown ✅
→ Machinery & Equipment ✅

CONSEQUENTIAL (lost profits):
→ Loss of Profit policies ✅

KEYWORDS: - “Damage during installation” = Construction phase - “Faulty design/construction” = CAR coverage - “Machines” = Could be EAR too, but CAR covers broader construction

MEMORY TIP: “Installing = CAR/EAR; Operating = Machinery policies”

📝 EXAM QUESTION 8 (Links to Section 7.4.E - CAR Exclusions)

Question: “Key exclusions of Contractor’s All Risk Policy: i. Loss due to war ii. Loss due to wilful act of insured iii. Loss due to nuclear radiation

A. I only B. I & II only C. I & III only D. All the above ✅”

Answer: D (All the above)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: CAR excludes WAR, WILFUL ACTS, and NUCLEAR risks!

ANALYSIS:

i. War ✅ EXCLUDED: - War, invasion, civil war - Uninsurable risk (catastrophic) - Standard exclusion all policies

ii. Wilful act of insured ✅ EXCLUDED: - Intentional damage by insured - Fraud prevention - Moral hazard control - Cannot profit from own wrongdoing

iii. Nuclear radiation ✅ EXCLUDED: - Nuclear incident, radioactive contamination - Catastrophic risk - Uninsurable - Standard exclusion

COMMON EXCLUSIONS IN GENERAL INSURANCE:

Almost ALL general insurance excludes:
❌ War and war-like operations
❌ Nuclear risks
❌ Intentional acts by insured
❌ Wear and tear
❌ Inherent vice

These are STANDARD exclusions

KEYWORDS: - “War” = Catastrophic, uninsurable - “Wilful act” = Intentional damage (moral hazard) - “Nuclear” = Catastrophic risk

MEMORY TIP: “WON’T cover War, Own Acts, Nuclear” (WON acronym)

7.4.E.II Erection All Risks (EAR) Insurance

Purpose: Cover installation/erection of machinery and equipment.

Covers: - Installation of plant and machinery - Testing and commissioning - Damage during erection - Third-party liability

Difference from CAR: EAR = Machinery installation; CAR = Building construction.

7.4.F BONDS

Definition: Guarantee that party will fulfill contractual obligations.

Three Main Construction Bonds (MEMORIZE):

  1. Tender Bond
  2. Performance Bond
  3. Advance Payment Bond

📝 EXAM QUESTION 3, 4, 5 (Links to Section 7.4.F - Bonds)

Question 3: “Which NOT a type of bond in construction? A. Tender Bond B. Performance C. Banking Security Bond ✅ D. Advance Payment Bond”

Answer: C (Banking Security Bond - NOT construction bond)

Question 4: “Which bond required for submission of tenders? A. Tender Bond ✅ B. Performance C. Banking Security Bond D. Advance Payment Bond”

Answer: A (Tender Bond)

Question 5: “Which bond ensures contractor fulfills duties within specified period? A. Tender Bond B. Performance ✅ C. Banking Security Bond D. Advance Payment Bond”

Answer: B (Performance Bond)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: THREE construction bonds - Tender, Performance, Advance Payment!

COMPREHENSIVE BOND ANALYSIS:

1. TENDER BOND (Question 4):

Purpose: Guarantee bidder will honor tender if accepted.

When Required: Submission of tender/bid for contract.

Protects: Project owner (principal) against contractor backing out.

Amount: Usually 2-5% of tender value.

Example:

Company submits tender for RM10 million project
Tender Bond: RM200,000 (2%)

If company wins but backs out:
Principal keeps RM200,000 (compensates for wasted time/re-tendering)

2. PERFORMANCE BOND (Question 5):

Purpose: Ensure contractor completes project as specified.

When Required: After contract awarded, during project execution.

Protects: Owner against contractor default, delays, poor quality.

Amount: Usually 10% of contract value.

Example:

Building contract: RM10 million
Performance Bond: RM1 million (10%)

If contractor abandons project or fails to complete:
Owner claims RM1 million to hire another contractor

3. ADVANCE PAYMENT BOND:

Purpose: Guarantee refund if contractor doesn’t perform after receiving advance.

When Required: When contractor receives upfront payment.

Protects: Owner’s advance payment.

Amount: Usually equals advance payment amount.

Example:

Contract: RM10 million
Advance payment: RM2 million (20%)
Advance Payment Bond: RM2 million

If contractor takes money and doesn't work:
Owner claims RM2 million back from bond

4. BANKING SECURITY BOND (Question 3) - ❌ NOT A CONSTRUCTION BOND: - NOT specific to construction industry - General banking/financial guarantee - WRONG ANSWER (this is the trap!)

BOND SEQUENCE IN PROJECT:

PROJECT LIFECYCLE:

STEP 1: Tendering
→ TENDER BOND (ensure serious bidders)

STEP 2: Contract Award
→ PERFORMANCE BOND (ensure completion)
→ ADVANCE PAYMENT BOND (if advance given)

STEP 3: Project Execution
→ Bonds remain active until completion

STEP 4: Completion
→ Bonds released

KEYWORDS: - “Tender” = Bidding stage - “Performance” = Execution/completion - “Advance Payment” = Protect upfront payment - “Banking Security” = NOT construction bond

MEMORY TIP:

TAP for Construction:
T = Tender Bond (bidding)
A = Advance Payment Bond (upfront $)
P = Performance Bond (completion)

7.5 Motor Insurance Rules (Malaysia)

📝 EXAM QUESTION 9 (Links to Section 7.3 - Motor Insurance Rules)

Question: “Rules governing motor insurance Malaysia. Identify correct: i. No official receipt required ii. Post-dated cheques can be accepted iii. Premiums received within 7 days from risk commences

A. I & II only B. I, II & III only C. I & IV only D. All the above”

Answer: C (Wait - seems error in question, “IV” not listed)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Cash Before Cover principle - payment BEFORE coverage starts!

CORRECT RULES:

Cash Before Cover Principle: - Premium must be paid BEFORE coverage starts - Official receipt MUST be issued - Post-dated cheques generally NOT accepted (payment must clear) - No credit extended

LIKELY ANSWER: Based on standard practice, proper rules are: - Official receipt IS required ✅ - Post-dated cheques NOT accepted ❌ - Premium paid BEFORE cover (not after 7 days) ✅

Note: Question may have formatting error. Standard rule = “Cash before cover, no credit”.
