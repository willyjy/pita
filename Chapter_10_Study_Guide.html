Chapter 10: Consumer Protection and Life Insurance Industry Code of Practice

Comprehensive Exam Preparation Guide with Question Mapping

📚 Chapter Overview

Consumer protection is the cornerstone of modern insurance regulation. This chapter covers the eight basic consumer rights, regulatory frameworks (BNM, FSA 2013), industry associations (LIAM, PIAM), agent professionalism requirements, and the Code of Good Practice.

Exam Weight: 6-8% of total exam
Study Time Recommendation: 5-7 hours

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you must be able to:

  1. List and explain the Eight Basic Consumer Rights
  2. Understand regulatory frameworks (FSA 2013, BNM’s role)
  3. Know agent entry requirements and CPD hours
  4. Apply Code of Good Practice provisions
  5. Explain policy surrender and free-look period rules
  6. Identify roles of LIAM, PIAM, and Ombudsman
  7. Understand digital transformation and consumer protection

10.1 Eight Basic Consumer Rights

CRITICAL CONCEPT (HIGH EXAM FREQUENCY) - MEMORIZE ALL EIGHT:

EIGHT BASIC CONSUMER RIGHTS
├── 1. Right to SAFETY
├── 2. Right to INFORMATION
├── 3. Right to CHOICE
├── 4. Right to BE HEARD
├── 5. Right to REDRESS
├── 6. Right to CONSUMER EDUCATION
├── 7. Right to SATISFACTION
└── 8. Right to BASIC GOODS & SERVICES

1. Right to SAFETY

Definition: Protection against products/services hazardous to health or life.

In Insurance Context: - Products must be financially sound - Insurers must maintain solvency - Risk-Based Capital (RBC) framework ensures stability - Protection from mis-selling

Example:

Consumer buys life insurance expecting protection
Insurer must be financially stable to pay claims
BNM monitors insurers' capital adequacy
Ensures claims can be paid when needed

2. Right to INFORMATION

Definition: Access to facts needed to make informed choices.

In Insurance Context: - Product Disclosure Sheet provided before purchase - Clear explanation of benefits, exclusions, charges - Sales illustrations with guaranteed and non-guaranteed benefits - Policy Information Statement after issuance

Key Documents:

Document When Content
Product Disclosure Sheet Before purchase Features, benefits, exclusions, risks
Sales Illustration During proposal Benefit projections, assumptions
Policy Document After issuance Full terms and conditions
Policy Information Statement With policy Premium payment, contact details

3. Right to CHOICE

Definition: Access to variety of products at competitive prices.

FSA 2013 Enhancements: - Product Comparator Tool - Compare products across insurers - Direct distribution channels - Commission-free pure protection products - Multiple insurers in market (competition) - Online platforms for comparison

Example:

Consumer wants term insurance
Can use Product Comparator to compare:
- Insurer A: RM500/year, sum assured RM500,000
- Insurer B: RM550/year, sum assured RM500,000, critical illness rider
- Insurer C: RM480/year, sum assured RM500,000 (online, no commission)

Makes informed choice based on needs and budget

4. Right to BE HEARD

Definition: Assurance that consumer interests receive consideration.

Mechanisms: - Complaints channels at insurers - Ombudsman for Financial Services (OFS) - Independent dispute resolution - BNM supervision of market conduct - Industry associations (LIAM, PIAM) oversight

Complaint Process:

STEP 1: Complaint to Insurer (direct)
↓ (If not satisfied)
STEP 2: Escalate to Insurer's Head Office
↓ (If still not satisfied)
STEP 3: Ombudsman for Financial Services (OFS)
↓
Independent review and binding decision

5. Right to REDRESS

Definition: Fair settlement of just claims, including compensation.

Insurance Context: - Timely claims processing - Fair claims assessment - Compensation for losses - Appeals process available

FSA 2013 Provisions: - Insurers must handle claims professionally - Clear claims procedures - Timely notification of claim decisions - OFS can award compensation up to specified limits

6. Right to CONSUMER EDUCATION

Definition: Acquire knowledge and skills for informed decisions.

BNM Initiatives: - Financial literacy programs - Consumer education websites (e.g., BNMTELELINK, BNMLINK) - Service Guide (from July 1, 2016) - Explains services to expect - Self-assessment tools and financial calculators - Public awareness campaigns

Service Guide Coverage (July 1, 2016): - Before purchasing: Needs analysis, product explanation - When buying: Application assistance, nomination guidance - During term: Policy servicing, claims assistance

7. Right to SATISFACTION

Definition: Fulfillment of basic needs for quality of life.

Insurance Context: - Products meet actual needs - Suitable recommendations - Quality service delivery - Professional conduct by agents

Agent’s Role: - Conduct needs analysis (Customer Fact Find) - Recommend suitable products - Provide ongoing service - Act in client’s best interest

8. Right to BASIC GOODS & SERVICES

Definition: Access to essential goods and services.

Insurance Context: - Basic insurance products accessible to all - Affordable pure protection products - Direct channels (online, walk-in) reduce costs - Commission-free products available (from July 1, 2017)

📝 EXAM QUESTION 4 (Links to Section 10.1 - Eight Basic Rights)

Question: “Which is NOT one of basic rights by International Consumer Movement? A. Right to satisfaction from products/services B. Right to digital access and privacy ✅ C. Right to basic goods and services D. Right to be heard in disputes”

Answer: B (Right to digital access and privacy)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Only EIGHT recognized basic consumer rights - digital access NOT one of them!

THE EIGHT BASIC RIGHTS (memorize): 1. ✅ Safety 2. ✅ Information 3. ✅ Choice 4. ✅ Be Heard 5. ✅ Redress 6. ✅ Consumer Education 7. ✅ Satisfaction (Option A - IS a basic right) 8. ✅ Basic Goods & Services (Option C - IS a basic right)

ANALYSIS:

A ✅ IS a basic right: - Right to Satisfaction (#7) - Products/services meet needs - Quality of life fulfillment

B ❌ NOT a basic right (CORRECT ANSWER): - Digital access/privacy = Modern concern - NOT part of original 8 basic rights - May be covered under other regulations (data protection) - But NOT a recognized consumer right in the traditional list

C ✅ IS a basic right: - Right to Basic Goods & Services (#8) - Access to essential products

D ✅ IS a basic right: - Right to Be Heard (#4) - Consumer interests considered - Complaints mechanisms

KEYWORDS: - “NOT one of” = Identify the exception - “International Consumer Movement” = Eight basic rights - “Digital access and privacy” = Modern issue, not basic right

MEMORY TIP: “Eight Classic Rights” (before digital age) - Digital access came later!

10.2 Financial Services Act 2013 - Key Consumer Protections

10.2.1 Policy Surrender Liberalization

FSA 2013 Major Change: More flexible surrender terms.

Policy Surrender Rules:

Policy Type Surrender Allowed Refund
Single Premium At ANY time Cash value provided
Regular Premium (>3 years) After 3 years in force Cash value provided
Regular Premium (<3 years) Before 3 years Cash value (if applicable)

📝 EXAM QUESTION 5 (Links to Section 10.2.1 - Policy Surrender)

Question: “Under FSA 2013, insurer allowed to provide cash value in: I. Single premium policies at any time II. Life policies after 3 years III. Life policies before 3 years

A. i only B. i & ii only C. i & iii only D. All the above ✅”

Answer: D (All the above)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: FSA 2013 LIBERALIZED surrender - cash value available in ALL THREE situations!

DETAILED ANALYSIS:

I. Single Premium Policies at ANY TIME ✅ TRUE: - Can surrender immediately or anytime after - No waiting period required - Full cash value provided (subject to charges)

Example:

Buy single premium policy: RM100,000
After 1 year, need money: CAN surrender
Receive: Cash value (e.g., RM95,000 after charges)

II. Life Policies AFTER 3 Years ✅ TRUE: - Regular premium policies in force >3 years - Cash value accumulation - Standard surrender provision

Example:

Buy regular premium policy: RM200/month
Year 4: Want to surrender
Cash value: Available (accumulated over 3+ years)

III. Life Policies BEFORE 3 Years ✅ TRUE: - Even policies <3 years can have cash value - Depends on policy type and premium paid - FSA 2013 allows flexibility

Example:

Buy policy, paid 2 years premiums
Need to surrender after 2 years
May receive cash value (if policy has any accumulated)
More flexible than old Insurance Act 1996

FSA 2013 IMPROVEMENT:

Old Insurance Act 1996:
- Strict 3-year rule
- Limited surrender options
- Less consumer-friendly

FSA 2013:
- More flexible surrender terms
- Consumer empowerment
- Greater control over policies

KEYWORDS: - “Single premium at any time” = Immediate flexibility - “After 3 years” = Standard rule - “Before 3 years” = FSA 2013 liberalization - “All the above” = All three scenarios valid

MEMORY TIP: “FSA 2013 = More Freedom to Surrender” (consumer-friendly reform)

10.2.2 Free-Look Period (Cooling-Off Period)

Definition: Period during which policyholder can return policy for refund.

FSA 2013 Rules:

Aspect Details
Period 15 days from policy receipt
Applies to All life insurance policies
Refund Full premium MINUS medical examination costs
No explanation required Can return without justification

📝 EXAM QUESTION 6 (Links to Section 10.2.2 - Free-Look Period)

Question: “Maggie wants to return policy few days after receiving. According to FSA 2013, which accurately describes her rights? A. Must pay administrative fees + medical costs B. Must return within 15 days for refund minus medical costs ✅ C. Cannot return once issued and delivered D. Must provide detailed written explanation”

Answer: B (Return within 15 days, refund minus medical costs)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: 15-day free-look = Consumer protection, minimal deductions!

CORRECT PROVISION (Option B): - 15 days from policy receipt (not from application) - Full refund of premiums paid - MINUS: Only medical examination costs (if incurred) - NO administrative fees deducted - NO explanation required

Example:

Maggie's Policy Details:
Premium paid: RM5,000
Medical exam cost (paid by insurer): RM300
Administrative costs: RM150

Within 15 Days, She Returns:
Refund: RM5,000 - RM300 = RM4,700
(Medical exam cost deducted only)
NOT deducted: Administrative fees

After 15 Days:
Free-look expired, standard surrender rules apply

WHY OTHERS WRONG:

A ❌ Must pay administrative fees: - FALSE - Only medical costs deducted - Administrative fees NOT charged

C ❌ Cannot return once issued: - FALSE - Can return within 15 days - Free-look period specifically for this

D ❌ Must provide explanation: - FALSE - NO justification needed - Consumer’s absolute right

CONSUMER PROTECTION RATIONALE: - Allows time to review policy - Read and understand terms - Change mind without penalty - Fair to consumer and insurer (medical costs real expense)

KEYWORDS: - “15 days” = Free-look period - “Minus medical costs” = Only deduction - “Full refund” = Consumer protection

MEMORY TIP: “15 Days, Medical Only” (simple refund rule)

10.3 Agent Requirements and Professionalism

10.3.1 Entry Requirements for Life Insurance Agents

CRITICAL REQUIREMENTS (HIGH EXAM FREQUENCY):

Requirement Details Authority
1. Minimum Age 18 years Legal capacity
2. Examination Pass PCEIA (or PCIL combined) Malaysian Insurance Institute (MII)
3. Registration Register with LIAM Industry oversight
4. Academic SPM/MCE with 5 passes (including BM) Basic education
5. Training 20 hours within 6 months of appointment Initial competency
6. Character Not undischarged bankrupt, no criminal record Fit and proper

📝 EXAM QUESTION 7 (Links to Section 10.3.1 - Agent Requirements)

Question: “Requirements for life insurance agent: I. 18 years of age II. Passed pre-contract examination III. Registration with NAMLIA IV. Registration with LIAM

A. i & ii only B. i, ii & iii C. i, ii & iv ✅ D. All the above”

Answer: C (i, ii & iv)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Register with LIAM (NOT NAMLIA)!

REQUIREMENT ANALYSIS:

I. 18 years of age ✅ CORRECT: - Legal age of majority - Can enter contracts - Minimum requirement by BNM

II. Passed pre-contract examination ✅ CORRECT: - PCEIA - Pre-Contract Examination for Insurance Agents - Administered by MII - OR PCIL - Combined exam (Life + Investment-Linked) - Mandatory before appointment

III. Registration with NAMLIA ❌ WRONG: - NO such organization! - NAMLIA does NOT exist - Common exam trap!

IV. Registration with LIAM ✅ CORRECT: - LIAM - Life Insurance Association of Malaysia - ALL life insurance agents must register - Industry requirement

CORRECT ORGANIZATIONS:

LIAM - Life Insurance Association of Malaysia ✅
(For LIFE insurance agents)

PIAM - Persatuan Insurans Am Malaysia ✅
(For GENERAL insurance agents)

NAMLIA - DOES NOT EXIST ❌
(Exam trap!)

COMPLETE ENTRY PROCESS: 1. Meet age requirement (18+) 2. Academic qualification (SPM 5 passes) 3. Pass PCEIA or PCIL exam 4. Register with LIAM 5. Complete 20 hours training (within 6 months) 6. Appointed by insurer 7. Begin practicing

KEYWORDS: - “18 years” = Minimum age - “Pre-contract examination” = PCEIA/PCIL - “LIAM” = Life Insurance Association Malaysia - “NAMLIA” = Does NOT exist (trap!)

MEMORY TIP: “LIAM for LIFE, PIAM for PROPERTY” (remember the real associations)

10.3.2 Continuous Professional Development (CPD)

CPD Hours Required (CRITICAL - MEMORIZE):

Agent Category Time Period CPD Hours Components
Newly Appointed Life/Family Takaful First 6 months 20 hours Initial training
Existing Life/Family Takaful (>1 year) Annual 30 hours Technical + Non-technical
Newly Appointed General Insurance First 6 months 12 hours Initial training
Existing General Insurance (>1 year) Annual 20 hours Technical + Non-technical

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: - Life agents: 20 hours (new), 30 hours (existing) - General agents: 12 hours (new), 20 hours (existing)

10.3.3 Additional Qualifications (Effective Jan 1, 2024)

Mandatory for Life/Family Takaful Agents:

Must Pass ONE of these: - RFP Module 2 - Registered Financial Planner (for life insurance) - CFP Module 2 - Certified Financial Planner (equivalent) - FCLP - Fellow Certified Life Practitioner (equivalent) - Shariah RFP Module 2 / IFP Module 2 - Islamic Financial Planner (for Takaful)

Focus: Risk Management & Insurance/Takaful Planning

Transition Period: - Agents appointed BEFORE Jan 1, 2024: Exempted (grandfathered) - Agents appointed ON/AFTER Jan 1, 2024: MUST pass within reasonable time

10.4 Code of Good Practice for Life Insurance Products

Purpose: Ensure ethical standards in product design, pricing, and selling.

Date of Application: On or after July 1, 1997 (all life products)

📝 EXAM QUESTION 9 (Links to Section 10.4 - Code Application Date)

Question: “Which TRUE regarding Code of Good Practice for agents/insurers? A. Applies only to products after 1st July 1997 B. Applies to products on or after 1st July 1997 ✅ C. Regulates actual sales talk by agents D. Regulates mortality table selection”

Answer: B (Applies on or after July 1, 1997)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: “ON or AFTER” (inclusive of July 1, 1997) - not “AFTER” (exclusive)!

KEY DISTINCTION:

"AFTER July 1, 1997" = From July 2, 1997 onwards (exclusive)
"ON OR AFTER July 1, 1997" = From July 1, 1997 onwards (inclusive) ✅

ANALYSIS:

A ❌ “Only to products AFTER July 1”: - Would exclude July 1, 1997 itself - WRONG - includes products from July 1 itself

B ✅ “Products ON OR AFTER July 1, 1997”: - CORRECT - inclusive of the date - All products from July 1, 1997 onwards - This is the official application date

C ❌ “Regulates actual sales talk”: - Code focuses on product design, sales illustrations, disclosures - NOT about regulating specific sales conversations - Too narrow interpretation

D ❌ “Regulates mortality table”: - Mortality tables = Actuarial/technical matter - Code focuses on consumer protection, not technical calculations - Wrong scope

WHAT CODE ACTUALLY COVERS: - Product design ethics - Fair pricing - Sales illustrations standards - Disclosure requirements - Proposal form guidelines - Policy information statements

KEYWORDS: - “On or after” = Inclusive date - “July 1, 1997” = Effective date - “Life products” = Scope

📝 EXAM QUESTION 10 (Links to Section 10.4 - Code Objectives)

Question: “Code objectives: I. Design to ethical standards II. Fair pricing III. Truth in selling with disclosures IV. Proper advice to proposers

A. i only B. i & ii only C. i, ii & iii only D. All the above ✅”

Answer: D (All the above)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Code has COMPREHENSIVE objectives - all four areas covered!

FOUR KEY OBJECTIVES:

I. Design to Ethical Standards ✅: - Products meet real needs - Not designed to exploit consumers - Suitable product features - Fair terms and conditions

II. Fair Pricing ✅: - Premiums reasonable - Charges transparent - No hidden costs - Value for money

III. Truth in Selling with Full Disclosures ✅: - Accurate sales illustrations - Complete information provided - Material facts disclosed - No misleading representations

IV. Proper Advice to Proposers ✅: - Suitable recommendations - Needs analysis conducted - Client’s best interest - Professional conduct

COMPREHENSIVE PROTECTION:

Product Stage → Objective
────────────────────────────
Design → Ethical standards (I)
Pricing → Fair pricing (II)
Selling → Truth/Disclosure (III)
Advice → Proper guidance (IV)

KEYWORDS: - “All the above” = Comprehensive coverage - “Objectives” = Multiple goals - “Code of Good Practice” = Industry standards

10.4.1 Sales Illustrations

Requirements:

Aspect Details
Actuary Review Every 2 years (appropriateness of assumptions)
Information Current, accurate, not misleading
Risk Statement Clear statement of risks borne by policy owner
Benefits Shown BOTH guaranteed AND non-guaranteed (with assumptions)

📝 EXAM QUESTION 2 (Links to Section 10.4.1 - Sales Illustrations)

Question: “Which FALSE about sales illustration? A. Actuary reviews assumptions every 2 years B. Information current, accurate, not misleading C. Must contain clear statement of risks D. Should only present guaranteed benefits, avoiding non-guaranteed ✅”

Answer: D (Should only present guaranteed - FALSE)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Sales illustrations MUST show BOTH guaranteed AND non-guaranteed benefits!

ANALYSIS:

A ✅ TRUE statement: - Appointed Actuary reviews assumptions - At least once every 2 years - Ensures appropriateness

B ✅ TRUE statement: - All information must be current - Accurate and not misleading - Consumer protection

C ✅ TRUE statement: - Must clearly state risks - Policy owner understands risks borne - Transparency requirement

D ❌ FALSE statement (CORRECT ANSWER): - Should show BOTH guaranteed AND non-guaranteed - Non-guaranteed benefits with clear assumptions - Complete picture for consumer - NOT limited to guaranteed only

WHY SHOW BOTH?

GUARANTEED Benefits:
- Death benefit (sum assured)
- Guaranteed cash values
- Fixed benefits

NON-GUARANTEED Benefits:
- Investment returns (unit-linked)
- Dividends/bonuses (participating)
- Variable benefits

Consumer needs to see:
- Minimum guaranteed (worst case)
- Potential non-guaranteed (upside)
- Make informed decision

KEYWORDS: - “FALSE” = Identify incorrect statement - “Only guaranteed” = Wrong (too restrictive) - “Both guaranteed and non-guaranteed” = Correct approach

MEMORY TIP: “Show ALL Benefits, Guaranteed + Maybe” (complete picture)

10.4.2 Policy Information Statement

Must Include: - ✅ Premium payment facilities - ✅ Contact person for enquiries/service - ✅ Importance of advising address changes - ❌ NOT legal advice (e.g., will writing) - outside scope

📝 EXAM QUESTION 3 (Links to Section 10.4.2 - Policy Information)

Question: “Policy information statement shall include all EXCEPT: A. Facility for premium payment B. Facility to provide legal advice (will writing) ✅ C. Person to contact for enquiry/service D. Importance of advising address changes”

Answer: B (Legal advice facility - NOT included)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Policy information = POLICY-RELATED info, NOT general legal services!

WHAT’S INCLUDED:

A ✅ Premium payment facilities: - Payment methods available - Payment due dates - Payment channels

C ✅ Contact person: - Who to contact for service - Enquiry channels - Customer service details

D ✅ Address change importance: - Keep insurer updated - Ensure policy documents received - Claims communication

WHAT’S NOT INCLUDED:

B ❌ Legal advice facilities: - Will writing = Separate legal service - NOT part of insurance policy service - Outside scope of policy information - Would need separate legal professional

SCOPE OF POLICY INFORMATION: - Policy-specific services - Insurance-related matters - Ongoing policy administration - NOT general legal/financial advice

KEYWORDS: - “EXCEPT” = Identify what’s NOT included - “Legal advice” = Beyond policy scope - “Policy information” = Policy-related only

10.5 Misleading Sales Materials

Code Requirement: Materials must be accurate and current.

📝 EXAM QUESTION 1 (Links to Section 10.5 - Misleading Materials)

Question: “Zoom Life has misleading brochures due to changed circumstances. What should company do per Code? A. Continue distribution, money already spent B. Continue distribution, no Code violation C. Must withdraw immediately or breach Code ✅ D. Continue with letter to rectify”

Answer: C (Must withdraw immediately or breach Code)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Misleading materials = IMMEDIATE WITHDRAWAL required (consumer protection priority)!

CODE PRINCIPLE: - Accuracy is paramount - Materials must reflect current circumstances - NO misleading information allowed - Consumer protection over company costs

ANALYSIS:

A ❌ Continue because money spent: - Cost is NOT justification for misleading consumers - Consumer protection > Company cost - Violates Code principles

B ❌ Continue, no violation: - FALSE - misleading materials = Code violation - Cannot continue distribution

C ✅ MUST WITHDRAW immediately: - CORRECT - only acceptable action - Prevents consumer harm - Code compliance requirement - Breach if continues

D ❌ Continue with rectification letter: - NOT sufficient - Still distributing misleading materials - Letter doesn’t fix original brochure - Must withdraw, then issue corrected version

PROPER ACTION:

1. STOP distribution immediately ✅
2. Withdraw existing brochures ✅
3. Correct the information ✅
4. Print new accurate materials ✅
5. Then distribute corrected version ✅

KEYWORDS: - “Misleading” = Inaccurate information - “Must withdraw” = Immediate action - “Breach Code” = Violation if continues

MEMORY TIP: “Misleading = STOP NOW” (consumer protection priority)

10.6 Regulatory Bodies

10.6.1 Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM)

Established: January 26, 1959

Role: Central bank and financial regulator

Functions: - Monetary policy - Financial stability - Regulate financial institutions - Consumer protection - Financial education

Key Legislation: - Central Bank of Malaysia Act 2009 - Financial Services Act 2013 - Islamic Financial Services Act 2013

10.6.2 Life Insurance Association of Malaysia (LIAM)

Role: Industry association for life insurers

Functions: - Agent registration - Industry standards - Training coordination (with MII) - Code of Good Practice oversight - Industry representation

10.6.3 Persatuan Insurans Am Malaysia (PIAM)

Role: General Insurance Association of Malaysia

Functions: - General insurance industry representation - Agent registration (general insurance) - Industry standards for general insurance - Training and development

10.6.4 Malaysian Insurance Institute (MII)

Role: Training and examination body

Functions: - Conduct PCEIA, PCIL examinations - Professional training programs - CPD courses - Industry education

10.6.5 Ombudsman for Financial Services (OFS)

Role: Independent dispute resolution

Functions: - Receive consumer complaints (after insurer escalation) - Investigate disputes - Make binding decisions (up to certain limits) - Alternative to court litigation - Free service for consumers

Process: 1. Complaint to insurer first 2. If unsatisfied, escalate to OFS 3. OFS investigates 4. Makes binding decision 5. Insurer must comply

10.7 Agent Code of Conduct (Key Provisions)

What Agent MUST Do:

  1. Identify and explain arrangements include insurance
  2. Make known he is agent of specific company/companies
  3. Ensure suitability - policy meets needs and resources
  4. Give advice only on matters he is knowledgeable
  5. Treat information confidentially

What Agent MUST NOT Do:

  1. NOT disclose referral source without consent
  2. NOT make inaccurate/unfair criticisms of insurers
  3. NOT make misleading comparisons without clarifying differences
  4. NOT prevent client from disclosing material facts
  5. NOT induce client to make misrepresentation

📝 EXAM QUESTION 8 (Links to Section 10.7 - Code Purpose)

Question: “Which NOT a purpose of Code of Good Practice? A. Encourage digital technologies for customer engagement ✅ B. Regulate sales illustrations for clarity C. Provide guidelines on proposal form disclosures D. Establish guidelines for ethical product design”

Answer: A (Digital technologies - NOT a purpose)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Code focuses on TRADITIONAL consumer protection, not technology adoption!

ACTUAL CODE PURPOSES:

B ✅ IS a purpose: - Regulate sales illustrations - Ensure clarity and transparency - Protect consumers from misleading projections

C ✅ IS a purpose: - Guidelines on proposal form disclosures - Protect consumer interests - Ensure material facts disclosed

D ✅ IS a purpose: - Ethical product design - Products meet actual needs - Fair terms and conditions

NOT A PURPOSE:

A ❌ Digital technologies: - Digital transformation = Separate initiative - Code established in 1997 (pre-digital era) - Code focuses on ethics, not technology - Technology = Tool, not Code objective

CODE FOCUS AREAS: - Product design ethics - Fair pricing - Sales illustration standards - Disclosure requirements - Agent conduct - Consumer protection

KEYWORDS: - “NOT one of purposes” = Identify exception - “Digital technologies” = Modern concern, not original Code purpose - “Code of Good Practice” = Consumer protection focus
