Chapter 1: Understanding Risks

Comprehensive Exam Preparation Guide

📚 Chapter Overview

This chapter forms the foundation of risk management and insurance planning. It explores fundamental concepts that will be tested extensively in the exam. Mastering the distinctions between risk, peril, and hazard is critical for exam success.

Exam Weight: This is a foundational chapter - expect 10-15% of exam questions from this material.

Study Time Recommendation: 8-10 hours

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you must be able to:

  1. Define and distinguish between risk, peril, and hazard with precision
  2. Classify different types of risks (fundamental, particular, pure, speculative, etc.)
  3. Identify how human behaviour influences risk perception and assessment
  4. Apply demographic factors to predict risk tolerance
  5. Evaluate client risk tolerance using both qualitative and quantitative methods

📖 Complete Chapter Summary

Section 1.1: Introduction to Risk Management

🔑 Key Definition (MEMORIZE THIS)

Risk = “Uncertainty about financial loss from an exposure”

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: This is THE definition you must know word-for-word. Do NOT confuse with general definitions of risk from other contexts.

Two Forms of Loss: 1. Actual Losses - Losses that have already occurred 2. Potential Losses (Loss Exposures) - Possible future losses

1.1.2 Terminology Associated with Loss

Term Definition Example Memory Aid
Peril The SOURCE or CAUSE of loss Fire, theft, death, illness “P” for “Problem that causes”
Hazard CONDITION that INCREASES probability/severity of loss Slippery road, smoking, carelessness “H” for “Helper of loss”

⚠️ COMMON EXAM TRAP: Students often reverse peril and hazard. Remember: Peril CAUSES, Hazard INCREASES

Types of Hazards (Critical for Exam)

Hazard Type Definition Malaysian Example Exam Tip
Physical Hazard Tangible condition increasing loss likelihood Overweight individual with family history of hypertension; oil-spilled road Look for PHYSICAL characteristics in scenarios
Moral Hazard Dishonesty/character flaws increasing loss Fabricating insurance claims; pre-existing conditions not disclosed Watch for FRAUD and DISHONESTY keywords
Morale Hazard Indifference/carelessness due to insurance Not locking doors because “I have insurance”; speeding because “car is insured” Remember: “Morale = More Careless”

📌 MEMORY TRICK: - Moral = Immoral (dishonest) - Morale = Low morale (don’t care attitude)

1.1.2.1 Scenario Analysis: Physical Hazard

Case Study: Mr. Lim (Overweight with Hypertension History)

Facts: - 40 years old - Overweight - Family history of hypertension

Insurance Implications: 1. Higher premiums due to elevated risk 2. More stringent underwriting process 3. Potential for policy exclusions or loading

Malaysian Context Example: - Prevalence of diabetes and heart disease in Malaysia - Higher incidence among certain ethnic groups - Impact of Malaysian diet (high sugar, high carb)

⚠️ EXAM SCENARIO ALERT: Questions may present similar cases and ask you to: - Identify the type of hazard (PHYSICAL) - Recommend appropriate insurance solutions - Suggest risk mitigation strategies

1.1.2.2 Scenario Analysis: Moral Hazard

Key Characteristics: 1. Risk-taking behaviour knowing insurer pays 2. Fraudulent claims 3. Erosion of trust

Malaysian Example: Early Medical Card Claim - Policyholder submits expensive medical claim shortly after purchase - Investigation reveals pre-existing condition - Constitutes moral hazard through non-disclosure

⚠️ EXAM TIP: If scenario mentions “shortly after policy purchase” + “expensive claim” = likely testing moral hazard concept

1.1.2.3 Scenario Analysis: Morale Hazard

Case Study: Mr. Alex (Comprehensive Insurance, Careless Behaviour)

Characteristics: - Has comprehensive coverage - Leaves car unlocked - Doesn’t secure home - Takes unnecessary risks

Why This is Morale Hazard (NOT Moral): - No fraud or dishonesty - Simply careless because “insurance will cover” - Attitude: “Why bother being careful?”

📌 EXAM DISTINCTION: - Moral = Intentional dishonesty - Morale = Unintentional carelessness

1.1.3 Risk vs. Chance

Aspect Risk Chance
Focus Possibility of LOSS Possibility of GAIN
Outcome Unfavourable/Negative Favourable/Positive
Example Business failure Winning lottery
In Malaysian Context Café failing in competitive F&B sector Winning Sports Toto

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Questions may present scenarios asking you to identify whether something represents “risk” or “chance” - focus on whether outcome is gain or loss.

1.1.4 Relationship Between Risk, Peril, and Hazard

The Critical Triangle (MEMORIZE THIS RELATIONSHIP):

HAZARD → Increases likelihood → PERIL → Causes → RISK (Possibility of Loss)

Example Chain: 1. Hazard: Smoking (physical hazard) 2. Peril: Heart disease 3. Risk: Financial loss from medical expenses/death

Table: Risk-Peril-Hazard in Insurance Contexts

Concept Life Insurance Medical Insurance Malaysian Example
Risk Financial loss from death of breadwinner Financial burden from medical expenses Loss of income affecting children’s education; high hospital costs
Peril Death from illness, accident, natural causes Medical condition requiring treatment Death from common Malaysian health issues (heart disease, diabetes); road accidents
Hazard Smoking, dangerous hobbies, lifestyle High-risk occupation, extreme sports Construction work in Malaysia; popular extreme sports (jungle trekking)

⚠️ EXAM STRATEGY: When given a scenario, systematically identify: 1. What is the RISK (financial loss potential)? 2. What is the PERIL (what causes the loss)? 3. What are the HAZARDS (what increases the likelihood)?

Section 1.2: Types of Risks

Classification Framework

6 Major Risk Categories (Know distinctions cold):

Risk Type Definition Characteristics Insurability Examples
Fundamental Risks Affect entire economy/large groups Widespread impact; impersonal; often societal/natural Usually NOT insurable Hyperinflation, earthquakes, war, climate change, pandemic
Particular Risks Affect only individuals Personal; limited impact Generally INSURABLE Robbery, house fire, car theft
Dynamic Risks Result from changes in economy/society Unpredictable; evolving Difficult to insure Technological change, regulatory changes, digital economy risks
Static Risks Result from irregular events Stable; predictable patterns Easily insurable Death, fire (normal occurrence)
Pure Risks Only loss or no loss (no gain) No opportunity for profit INSURABLE Property damage, liability, premature death
Speculative Risks Possibility of loss OR gain Investment nature NOT insurable Stock market, business ventures, gambling

1.2.1 Fundamental Risks - Malaysian Context

Climate Change Impact on Malaysia:

Real-World Examples: 1. Severe flooding in Eastern states (Kelantan, Terengganu, Pahang) 2. Changing monsoon patterns affecting agriculture 3. Rising sea levels threatening coastal areas

Economic Consequences: - Infrastructure damage - Agricultural disruption (rubber, palm oil plantations) - Tourism sector impact - Insurance underwriting challenges

Technological Advancement: Digital Economy

Malaysian Context: - Rapid e-commerce growth - Digital finance adoption (e-wallets, fintech) - New risks: Cybersecurity, digital fraud, data breaches

⚠️ EXAM TIP: If question mentions “affects entire community/economy” or “cannot prevent individually” → Fundamental Risk

1.2.2 Particular Risks - Malaysian Context

Case Study: Mr. Lee’s Home Security

Scenario: - Urban homeowner in Kuala Lumpur - Concerned about burglaries - Individual risk (not community-wide)

Mitigation Actions: 1. Install CCTV systems 2. Hire security guards 3. Join neighbourhood watch 4. Install alarm systems

Key Point: Individual can reduce this risk through personal action

⚠️ EXAM DISTINCTION: - Fundamental Risk = Cannot control individually - Particular Risk = Can control through personal action

1.2.3 Dynamic Risks

Characteristics: - Result from changes in society/economy - Difficult to predict - Continuously evolving

Malaysian Examples: 1. Technology: Shift to Industry 4.0, automation replacing jobs 2. Regulatory: Changes in EPF/SOCSO regulations 3. Economic: Currency fluctuations, trade policy changes 4. Digital Transformation: E-invoice implementation, digital tax systems

1.2.4 Static Risks

Characteristics: - Regular occurrence patterns - Predictable through statistics - Stable over time

Examples: - Death (mortality tables) - Fire occurrence (historical data) - Accident frequency (actuarial data)

📌 Memory Aid: “Static” = “Statistics” = Predictable from data

1.2.5 Pure Risks

Key Characteristic: Only two possible outcomes - Loss or No Loss (NEVER a gain)

Properties: 1. Insurable 2. Fortuitous (happens by chance) 3. Definite and measurable 4. Large number of similar exposures

Examples: - Premature death - Property damage - Disability - Liability claims

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Pure risk questions often test understanding that there is NO possibility of gain

1.2.6 Speculative Risks

Key Characteristic: Three possible outcomes - Loss, No Change, OR Gain

Properties: 1. NOT insurable 2. Voluntarily undertaken 3. Potential for profit

Examples: - Stock market investments - Business ventures - Currency trading - Property speculation

📌 EXAM TIP: If scenario involves potential profit/gain → Speculative Risk → NOT INSURABLE

Section 1.3: Human Behaviour and Risk

1.3.1 Perceived Risk vs. Objective Risk

Type Definition Based On Example
Objective Risk Actual, measurable risk based on facts/statistics Data, evidence, actuarial tables Statistical probability of car accident based on traffic data
Perceived Risk Individual’s subjective assessment of risk Personal beliefs, emotions, experience Feeling air travel is dangerous despite safety statistics

Malaysian Example: - Perceived: Many Malaysians fear flying (post-MH370) - Objective: Air travel statistically safer than driving on Malaysian highways

⚠️ EXAM SCENARIO: “Alan feels scuba diving is dangerous even though it’s safe with proper training” - This is PERCEIVED RISK (not objective risk)

1.3.2 Categories of Risk Behaviour

Three Main Categories:

Category Attitude Behaviour Investment Profile
Risk Averse Views risk as danger Avoids uncertainty; prefers safety Fixed deposits, bonds, conservative investments
Risk Neutral Indifferent to risk Focuses on expected return regardless of risk Balanced approach
Risk Seeking Views risk as opportunity Actively pursues risk for higher returns Aggressive equity investments, speculative assets

Special Category:

Thrill Seeker = Person who consistently seeks out or creates risks in ALL life situations - NOT just in investments - In all aspects of life (sports, career, lifestyle)

⚠️ EXAM TRAP: Don’t confuse “risk seeking” (investment style) with “thrill seeker” (lifestyle)

1.3.3 Factors Affecting Rational Decision-Making

Cognitive Biases (Common Exam Topics):

Bias Definition Malaysian Example Impact
Availability Bias Recent/memorable events disproportionately influence decisions Reading about insurance claim rejection in newspaper, then fearing all insurers won’t pay Overestimate likelihood of rare events
Overconfidence Bias Excessive confidence in own judgment Investor thinks they can time the market Underestimate risks
Anchoring Bias Over-rely on first piece of information First premium quote becomes reference point Poor comparison
Confirmation Bias Seek information confirming existing beliefs Only read success stories about property investment Ignore warning signs

Malaysian Exam Scenario - Availability Bias: “Ah Fatt reads newspaper article about insurer not paying claim. Now concerned about buying life insurance.” - This is AVAILABILITY BIAS (recent information overly influences decision)

⚠️ EXAM STRATEGY: When scenario shows someone making irrational decision based on one incident → likely testing availability bias

1.3.4 Risk Taker vs. Thrill Seeker

Critical Distinction for Exam:

Aspect Risk Taker Thrill Seeker
Scope Specific contexts (e.g., investments) ALL life situations
Motivation Higher returns/rewards Excitement itself
Rationality Can be rational (calculated risk) Often irrational
Example Entrepreneur starting business Base jumper, extreme sports enthusiast

1.3.5 Risk Tolerance and Demographics

Key Demographic Factors (HIGH EXAM PROBABILITY):

Factor Impact on Risk Tolerance Explanation
Age Younger → Higher tolerance More time to recover from losses
Older → Lower tolerance Need capital preservation
Wealth Wealthier → Higher tolerance Can afford losses
Less wealthy → Lower tolerance Cannot afford losses
Occupation Stable job → Can take more investment risk Secure income stream
Unstable job → Lower investment risk tolerance Need liquidity
Family Size More dependents → Lower tolerance Greater financial responsibilities
Fewer dependents → Higher tolerance Less obligations
Education Higher education → Often higher tolerance Better understanding of risk/return
Cultural Factors Varies by culture Malaysian context: Different ethnic groups may have different savings/investment cultures

⚠️ EXAM QUESTION PATTERN: “Choose correct combination of demographic characteristics that influence risk tolerance” - ANSWER: Wealth, Age, Occupation (Family size often included)

Section 1.4: Measuring Risk Tolerance

1.4.1 Qualitative vs. Quantitative Approaches

Approach Method Advantages Disadvantages When to Use
Qualitative Interviews, discussions, open-ended questions Rich insights, understand motivations Subjective, hard to compare Initial assessment, understanding context
Quantitative Standardized questionnaires, numerical scoring Objective, comparable, consistent May miss nuances Formal risk profiling, documentation

Best Practice: Use BOTH approaches for comprehensive assessment

1.4.2 Effective Question Framing

Poor Question Examples: ❌ “Are you a risk taker?” (Too vague) ❌ “Do you like risk?” (Unclear context)

Good Question Examples: ✅ “If your investment portfolio decreased by 20% in one year, would you: a) Sell immediately, b) Hold, c) Buy more?” ✅ “What is the maximum percentage loss you could tolerate in one year before you would want to change your investment strategy?”

Malaysian Context Questions: ✅ “Would you invest in Amanah Saham Bumiputera (ASB) or equity unit trusts?” ✅ “How comfortable are you investing outside EPF contributions?”

1.4.3 Key Considerations in Risk Assessment

Five Critical Areas:

  1. Risk Capacity (Financial ability to take risk)
    • Net worth
    • Income stability
    • Emergency funds
    • Debt levels
  2. Risk Tolerance (Psychological comfort with risk)
    • Emotional reactions
    • Sleep-at-night factor
    • Past experiences
  3. Risk Required (Risk needed to meet goals)
    • Financial goals
    • Time horizon
    • Return requirements
  4. Risk Perception (How client views risk)
    • Subjective assessment
    • Influenced by biases
    • Cultural factors
  5. Risk Behaviour (How client actually acts)
    • Past investment decisions
    • Portfolio composition
    • Response to market volatility

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Don’t confuse risk CAPACITY (ability) with risk TOLERANCE (willingness)

1.4.4 Assessing Through Current Portfolio

Analysis Framework:

Portfolio Characteristic What It Indicates Malaysian Example
High equity allocation Higher risk tolerance OR misunderstanding of risk 80% in Bursa Malaysia stocks
Multiple insurance policies Risk averse OR sophisticated risk management Life, medical, critical illness, investment-linked all with different insurers
Frequent job changes Risk taking OR career growth focus Moving between MNCs every 2-3 years
Variable income Risk taking (entrepreneur) OR industry norm Full-time business owner vs commission-based sales
Property investments Risk seeking OR cultural preference Multiple properties (Malaysian preference for property)

🎯 Case Studies Analysis

Case Study 1: Hafiz (35-year-old Entrepreneur)

Profile: - Age: 35 - Just sold successful startup - Diversified investment portfolio - No dependents

Question: Primary strategy for next financial steps?

Options Analysis: - A. Reinvest all in new startup → ❌ Too risky, no diversification - B. Balanced portfolio, long-term growth → ✅ CORRECT - C. Significant portion to fixed deposits → ❌ Too conservative for age/profile - D. High-risk, high-return → ❌ Assumes entrepreneurial = risk seeking in all areas

Answer: B

Rationale: - Age 35 = Long time horizon - Successful exit = Has capital - No dependents = Can take some risk - Already entrepreneurial = Don’t need 100% high risk - Balanced approach best aligns with situation

⚠️ EXAM TIP: Don’t assume entrepreneur wants all high-risk. Consider complete profile.

Case Study 2: Mei Lin (50-year-old Teacher)

Profile: - Age: 50 - Teacher (stable income) - Risk-averse - Most savings in fixed deposits and bonds - Planning for retirement

Question: How to advise for retirement preparation?

Options Analysis: - A. Maintain current strategy → ❌ Too conservative, won’t grow enough - B. Gradually shift to higher-yielding options → ✅ CORRECT - C. Immediately diversify into high-risk stocks → ❌ Against risk profile - D. Invest in overseas markets → ❌ Doesn’t match risk tolerance

Answer: B

Rationale: - Age 50 = Still 10-15 years to retirement - Risk-averse = Must respect tolerance - “Gradually” = Not shocking to client - “Higher-yielding” ≠ “high-risk” - Can improve returns without drastically increasing risk

⚠️ EXAM TIP: “Gradually” is key word showing respect for client’s risk aversion

Case Study 3: Raj (40-year-old IT Professional)

Profile: - Age: 40 - IT professional in Penang - Moderate risk appetite - Stable job - Unfamiliar with stock investments

Question: Approach to assess suitability for stock market investment?

Options Analysis: - A. Structured questionnaire to evaluate knowledge and risk tolerance → ✅ CORRECT - B. Immediately recommend blue-chips → ❌ Skips assessment - C. High-risk portfolio (stable job justification) → ❌ Ignores lack of knowledge - D. Advise against due to lack of familiarity → ❌ Too restrictive

Answer: A

Rationale: - “Unfamiliar” = Need education assessment - Moderate risk appetite = Need to verify - Structured approach = Professional standard - Must assess BEFORE recommending

⚠️ EXAM TIP: When client lacks knowledge, ALWAYS assess first before recommending

Case Study 4: Farah (28-year-old Freelance Designer)

Profile: - Age: 28 - Freelance graphic designer - Goal: Buy house in 5 years - Some savings - Moderately aggressive risk tolerance

Question: Which investment strategy aligns best?

Options Analysis: - A. High-risk for higher returns → ❌ Too risky for 5-year goal - B. Conservative (government bonds) → ❌ Too conservative for profile - C. Balanced mix of equities and bonds → ✅ CORRECT - D. Solely real estate → ❌ Need liquidity for house down payment

Answer: C

Rationale: - 5 years = Medium-term - Moderately aggressive = Can handle some volatility - Specific goal = Need balance between growth and stability - Equities = Growth potential - Bonds = Stability component

⚠️ EXAM TIP: Match investment strategy to BOTH risk tolerance AND time horizon

💡 Exam Preparation - Critical Concepts

Must-Memorize Definitions

  1. Risk: “Uncertainty about financial loss from an exposure”
  2. Peril: The source or cause of loss
  3. Hazard: Condition that increases probability/severity of loss
  4. Pure Risk: Only loss or no loss (never gain) - INSURABLE
  5. Speculative Risk: Loss, no change, OR gain - NOT INSURABLE
  6. Fundamental Risk: Affects entire economy/large groups - Usually NOT INSURABLE
  7. Particular Risk: Affects only individuals - Generally INSURABLE

Common Exam Traps

Trap What Students Think Reality How to Avoid
Moral vs Morale Same thing Different: Moral = dishonest, Morale = careless Remember: Moral = Immoral (fraud)
Risk vs Chance Interchangeable Risk = focus on loss, Chance = focus on gain Look for gain/loss in scenario
Peril vs Hazard Same thing Peril causes, Hazard increases Peril = Problem, Hazard = Helper
Entrepreneur = Risk Seeker All entrepreneurs are risk seeking May be calculated, not seeking risk for thrill Analyze complete profile
Insurance = Solution Insurance solves all risks Only solves PURE risks Check if speculative or fundamental risk

Question Pattern Recognition

If Question Contains… Likely Testing… Key Words to Watch
“Shortly after policy purchase” + “expensive claim” Moral hazard Pre-existing, undisclosed, fraud
“Don’t bother locking car because insured” Morale hazard Careless, indifferent, complacent
“Affects entire economy/community” Fundamental risk Widespread, cannot control, economy-wide
“Can prevent through personal action” Particular risk Individual, personal, controllable
“Potential for profit” Speculative risk Gain, investment, business
“Only loss or no loss” Pure risk No gain, insurable
“Recent news article influences decision” Availability bias Recent, memorable, newspaper
“Combination of demographics” Age, wealth, occupation NOT population density, NOT evidence

Malaysian Context - Key Points

  1. Common Health Issues: Heart disease, diabetes (mention in physical hazard scenarios)
  2. Popular Investments: ASB, property, EPF (mention in risk tolerance examples)
  3. Insurance Products: Life, medical, investment-linked (mention in scenarios)
  4. Cultural Factors: Property preference, multi-generational planning
  5. Regulatory Bodies: BNM, LIAM, PIAM (know their roles)
  6. Local Events: Flooding (fundamental risk), burglary in urban areas (particular risk)

📝 Self-Assessment Questions (30 Questions)

Section A: Fundamental Concepts (Questions 1-10)

1. In financial planning, risk is best defined as: - A. The chance of an investment loss - B. Uncertainty about financial loss from an exposure ✅ - C. The chance of a physical loss - D. The chance of an income loss

Exam Tip: This is a word-for-word definition question. Other options are incomplete.

2. Which of the following correctly describes the relationship between peril and hazard? - A. Peril increases the likelihood of loss; hazard causes the loss - B. Peril causes the loss; hazard increases the likelihood of loss ✅ - C. They are interchangeable terms - D. Peril is insurable; hazard is not

Exam Tip: Most common mix-up in the exam. Remember: Peril CAUSES, Hazard INCREASES.

3. Mr. Ahmad leaves his car unlocked in a shopping mall parking lot because he has comprehensive insurance. This is an example of: - A. Physical hazard - B. Moral hazard - C. Morale hazard ✅ - D. Speculative risk

Exam Tip: No fraud (not moral), just carelessness due to insurance (morale).

4. A policyholder submits a claim for a pre-existing medical condition that was not disclosed during application. This represents: - A. Physical hazard - B. Moral hazard ✅ - C. Morale hazard - D. Fundamental risk

Exam Tip: Non-disclosure/fraud = Moral hazard

5. Fire that burns down a house is an example of: - A. Risk - B. Peril ✅ - C. Hazard - D. Loss exposure

Exam Tip: Fire is the CAUSE of loss = Peril

6. Storing flammable materials near an electrical panel is an example of: - A. Peril - B. Pure risk - C. Physical hazard ✅ - D. Moral hazard

Exam Tip: Tangible condition that increases likelihood = Physical hazard

7. The possibility of financial loss due to the death of a breadwinner represents: - A. Peril - B. Risk ✅ - C. Hazard - D. Pure loss

Exam Tip: “Possibility of loss” = definition of Risk

8. Which statement about risk and chance is correct? - A. Both focus on unfavorable outcomes - B. Risk focuses on loss; chance focuses on gain ✅ - C. They are synonymous in financial planning - D. Chance is always insurable; risk is not

Exam Tip: Key distinction is loss vs. gain focus

9. Being overweight with a family history of diabetes is classified as what type of hazard for life insurance? - A. Moral hazard - B. Morale hazard - C. Physical hazard ✅ - D. Speculative hazard

Exam Tip: Physical/health condition = Physical hazard

10. In the risk-peril-hazard relationship, what role does hazard play? - A. Causes the loss to occur - B. Represents the possibility of loss - C. Increases the likelihood or severity of loss ✅ - D. Provides insurance coverage

Exam Tip: Hazard is the “multiplier” of risk

Section B: Types of Risks (Questions 11-18)

11. Severe flooding affecting multiple states in Malaysia is an example of: - A. Particular risk - B. Fundamental risk ✅ - C. Pure risk only - D. Dynamic risk only

Exam Tip: Affects large groups/economy = Fundamental. (Can also be pure, but “fundamental” is the best answer for the widespread impact)

12. A house fire affecting only one family is: - A. Fundamental risk - B. Particular risk ✅ - C. Speculative risk - D. Dynamic risk

Exam Tip: Affects only individual/family = Particular

13. Which of the following is a speculative risk? - A. Death of a key employee - B. Fire destroying inventory - C. Starting a new business venture ✅ - D. Liability claim from customer injury

Exam Tip: Only speculative risk has potential for GAIN

14. Premature death is classified as: - A. Pure risk ✅ - B. Speculative risk - C. Dynamic risk - D. Fundamental risk

Exam Tip: Only loss or no loss (no gain possibility) = Pure risk

15. The rapid growth of e-commerce in Malaysia creating cybersecurity risks is an example of: - A. Static risk - B. Dynamic risk ✅ - C. Pure risk - D. Particular risk

Exam Tip: Results from changes in economy/technology = Dynamic

16. Which type of risk is generally NOT insurable? - A. Pure risk - B. Particular risk - C. Static risk - D. Speculative risk ✅

Exam Tip: Speculative risks (potential for gain) are NOT insurable

17. Death following predictable mortality patterns represents: - A. Dynamic risk - B. Static risk ✅ - C. Fundamental risk - D. Speculative risk

Exam Tip: Predictable from statistics = Static risk

18. Which combination correctly matches risk types with insurability? - A. Pure risk - insurable; Speculative risk - not insurable ✅ - B. Fundamental risk - always insurable; Particular risk - not insurable - C. Dynamic risk - insurable; Static risk - not insurable - D. All risks are insurable if premium is high enough

Exam Tip: Pure vs Speculative is the key insurability distinction

Section C: Human Behaviour and Risk (Questions 19-24)

19. Alan feels scuba diving is dangerous even though statistics show it’s safe with proper training. Alan’s judgment is based on: - A. Objective risk - B. Risk indifference - C. Risk aversion - D. Perceived risk ✅

Exam Tip: Personal belief vs. facts = Perceived risk

20. A person who is “consistently seeking out or creating risks in ALL life situations” is termed a: - A. Risk seeker - B. Thrill seeker ✅ - C. Risk neutral person - D. Speculative investor

Exam Tip: “ALL life situations” is key phrase = Thrill seeker

21. Ah Fatt reads a newspaper article about an insurer not paying a claim and becomes concerned about buying life insurance. This is an example of: - A. Rationality bias - B. Availability bias ✅ - C. Intimacy bias - D. Confirmation bias

Exam Tip: Recent memorable information disproportionately influences decision = Availability bias

22. A person who prefers government employment for job security would most likely be: - A. Risk averse ✅ - B. Risk indifferent - C. Thrill seeking - D. Risk seeking

Exam Tip: Prefers security/certainty = Risk averse

23. Which factor does NOT affect an individual’s ability to think rationally about risk? - A. Perception of the risk - B. When one does not face a detriment from the risk ✅ - C. Overconfidence - D. Individual’s beliefs and value system

Exam Tip: If no detriment (no stake in outcome), rationality is less affected

24. Objective risk is best defined as: - A. Risk involving loss of face - B. Risk based on person’s beliefs and value system - C. Risk involving loss of freedom - D. Risk based on facts and statistical evidence ✅

Exam Tip: “Objective” = based on facts/data, not emotions

Section D: Risk Tolerance and Demographics (Questions 25-30)

25. Which combination of demographic characteristics most influences attitude toward risk? - A. Wealth, age, and family size - B. Family size, marital status, and sex - C. Family size, age, and population density - D. Wealth, age, and occupation ✅

Exam Tip: This exact question is in the book. Wealth, age, occupation are the key three.

26. A person willing to accept high returns for an increase in risk level is: - A. Risk tolerant ✅ - B. Risk averse - C. Risk indifferent - D. Irrational

Exam Tip: Higher return for higher risk acceptance = Risk tolerant

27. When assessing a client’s risk tolerance, a financial planner should: - A. Find out the client’s net worth only - B. Understand the client’s risk tolerance level ✅ - C. Always recommend lower risk investments - D. Advise additional insurance only

Exam Tip: Understanding risk tolerance is fundamental to financial planning

28. A 28-year-old with no dependents would generally have: - A. Lower risk tolerance than a 50-year-old with three children - B. Higher risk tolerance than a 50-year-old with three children ✅ - C. Same risk tolerance regardless of dependents - D. No ability to assess risk tolerance

Exam Tip: Younger age + no dependents = Higher risk capacity and often higher tolerance

29. Which is the BEST method for assessing a client’s risk tolerance? - A. Qualitative approach only - B. Quantitative approach only - C. Combination of both qualitative and quantitative approaches ✅ - D. Assessing current portfolio only

Exam Tip: Best practice uses BOTH methods for comprehensive assessment

💭 Memory Aids & Mnemonics

    • Risk → Possibility
    • Hazard → Increases
  2. Moral vs Morale:

    • Morale = More + Less (more careless, less careful)

    • Pure
    • Insurable
    • Two outcomes (loss or no loss)
    • Speculative is opposite
  4. F.P.D.S. for Risk Categories:
    • Fundamental (affects many)
    • Particular (affects individual)
    • Dynamic (changing)
    • Static (stable)
