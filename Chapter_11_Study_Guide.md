## Comprehensive Exam Preparation Guide with Question Mapping

## 📚 Chapter Overview

SOCSO (Pertubuhan Keselamatan Sosial - PERKESO) is Malaysia's social security system providing protection to employees against work-related and non-work-related injuries, disabilities, and death. Understanding SOCSO is essential for comprehensive financial planning.

**Exam Weight**: 5-7% of total exam  
**Study Time Recommendation**: 4-5 hours

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you must be able to:

1. **Explain** SOCSO's role and importance in Malaysia's social safety net
2. **Identify** eligibility criteria for SOCSO schemes
3. **Distinguish** between Employment Injury and Invalidity Pension schemes
4. **Calculate** contribution rates for employers and employees
5. **Understand** definitions of wages for contribution purposes
6. **Know** employer and employee responsibilities
7. **Identify** benefits under each scheme
8. **Understand** appeals process

## 📖 Complete Chapter Summary

### 11.1 Introduction to SOCSO

**Full Name**: 
- English: Social Security Organization (SOCSO)
- Malay: Pertubuhan Keselamatan Sosial (PERKESO)

**Established**: 
- Act: Employees' Social Security Act 1969
- Operations began: 1971

**Primary Mission**: Provide financial aid and support to employees and dependents in case of:
- Work-related injuries
- Disabilities
- Illnesses
- Death

---

### 11.2 SOCSO's Two Main Schemes

**CRITICAL FRAMEWORK** (HIGH EXAM FREQUENCY):

```
SOCSO TWO SCHEMES
├── 1. Employment Injury Insurance Scheme (EIIS)
│   └── Work-related injuries and diseases
└── 2. Invalidity Pension Scheme (IPS)
    └── Non-work-related disabilities and death
```

---

#### Scheme 1: Employment Injury Insurance Scheme (EIIS)

**Coverage**:
- **Work-related injuries** or diseases
- **Commuting accidents** (to/from work)
- Accidents during working hours

**Key Features**:

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Age Limit** | NO age limit |
| **Eligibility** | All registered SOCSO employees |
| **Type of Incidents** | Work-related only |
| **Coverage Period** | As long as employed and contributing |

**Benefits Provided**:
- Medical treatment
- Temporary disablement benefit
- Permanent disablement benefit
- Dependents' benefit (if death)
- Funeral benefit
- Rehabilitation services
- Education benefit for children

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 6** *(Links to Section 11.5 - EIIS Coverage)*

**Question**: "Employment Injury Insurance Scheme covers:
I. Diseases contracted due to work environment exposure
II. Accidents at workplace but employee not on duty
III. Accidents while out for lunch during break time

A. I and II
B. II and III
C. I and III ✅
D. None of the above"

**Answer**: C (I and III)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: EIIS covers **WORK-RELATED** incidents + reasonable work activities (including lunch breaks)!

**DETAILED ANALYSIS**:

**I. Diseases from work environment exposure** ✅ COVERED:
- **Occupational diseases** are covered
- Examples: Asbestosis (construction), Silicosis (mining), Chemical poisoning
- Must be contracted due to work exposure
- Established causal link to work environment

**Example**:
```
Factory worker exposed to toxic chemicals
Develops respiratory disease over 5 years
Medical evidence links disease to workplace exposure
Result: EIIS COVERS treatment and disability benefits
```

**II. Accidents at workplace BUT not on duty** ❌ NOT COVERED:
- **Key phrase**: "Not on duty"
- At workplace but NOT working = NOT covered
- Must be performing work duties or work-related activity

**Example**:
```
Employee stays after hours for personal reasons
Not working, just using office Wi-Fi for personal matters
Gets injured (e.g., falls down stairs)
Result: NOT COVERED (not on duty)
```

**III. Accidents during lunch break** ✅ COVERED:
- Lunch break = **REASONABLE WORK ACTIVITY**
- Part of normal work day
- Even if off premises (going to nearby restaurant)
- Commuting principle extends to lunch travel

**Example**:
```
Employee goes to nearby restaurant during lunch hour
Gets into accident crossing road
Result: COVERED (lunch break is work-related activity)
```

**COVERAGE PRINCIPLE**:
```
COVERED:
✅ While performing work duties
✅ Commuting to/from work
✅ During reasonable work breaks (lunch, tea)
✅ Work-related travel
✅ Occupational diseases

NOT COVERED:
❌ Personal activities at workplace (not on duty)
❌ Non-work-related incidents
❌ Intentional self-harm
```

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Work environment exposure" = Occupational disease ✅
- "Not on duty" = Personal time ❌
- "Lunch during break time" = Work-related activity ✅

**MEMORY TIP**: **"Working Hours + Work-Related = Covered"** (including reasonable breaks)

---

#### Scheme 2: Invalidity Pension Scheme (IPS)

**Coverage**:
- **Non-work-related** long-term illness or disability
- Death due to **any cause** (work or non-work)
- Permanent incapacity

**Key Features**:

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Age Limit** | Must be **UNDER 60** at time of diagnosis |
| **Eligibility** | All registered SOCSO employees |
| **Type of Incidents** | Non-work-related disabilities/death |
| **Medical Requirement** | Medical board certification of permanent incapacity |

**Benefits Provided**:
- Invalidity pension (monthly payments)
- Constant attendance allowance (for severely disabled)
- Survivors' pension (for dependents if death)
- Funeral benefit
- Education benefit for dependent children

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 7** *(Links to Section 11.6 - IPS Benefits)*

**Question**: "Under Invalidity Pension Scheme, employee/dependents receive:
I. Invalidity pension
II. Constant attendance allowance
III. Education loan for dependent children

A. I and II
B. II and III
C. I and III
D. All the above ✅"

**Answer**: D (All the above)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: IPS provides **COMPREHENSIVE support** - all three benefits available!

**BENEFIT ANALYSIS**:

**I. Invalidity Pension** ✅ PROVIDED:
- **Monthly pension** for permanently disabled employee
- Continues for life (as long as disability persists)
- Amount based on contribution history and degree of disability
- Replaces lost income

**Example**:
```
Employee suffers stroke (non-work-related)
Medical board certifies permanent 60% disability
Cannot work anymore
Receives: Monthly invalidity pension (e.g., RM1,500/month for life)
```

**II. Constant Attendance Allowance** ✅ PROVIDED:
- For **SEVERELY disabled** requiring constant care
- Additional monthly payment beyond pension
- Recognizes extra care costs
- Helps family cope with caregiving burden

**Example**:
```
Employee permanently bedridden after accident
Requires 24/7 care for basic needs (feeding, bathing, etc.)
Receives: Invalidity pension + Constant attendance allowance
(e.g., RM1,500 pension + RM500 attendance = RM2,000/month)
```

**III. Education Benefit for Dependent Children** ✅ PROVIDED:
- **NOT loan, but BENEFIT** (no repayment needed!)
- For dependent children's education
- Helps family maintain children's schooling
- Continues even after employee's death

**Example**:
```
Disabled employee has 2 children (age 10 and 12)
Both in school
SOCSO provides: Education benefit to support their studies
Ensures children can continue education despite family hardship
```

**IMPORTANT NOTE**:
```
Question says "Education LOAN"
Actually: Education BENEFIT (grant, not loan)
But answer still D - all three types of support provided
Don't get tricked by wording!
```

**COMPREHENSIVE IPS BENEFITS**:
1. ✅ Invalidity Pension (monthly income)
2. ✅ Constant Attendance Allowance (for severe cases)
3. ✅ Survivors' Pension (if employee dies)
4. ✅ Funeral Benefit
5. ✅ Education Benefit (for children)

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Invalidity pension" = Monthly payment for disability
- "Constant attendance" = Extra for severe disability
- "Education benefit" = Support for children (not loan)
- "All the above" = Comprehensive coverage

**MEMORY TIP**: **"IPS = Income + Care + Education"** (complete family support)

### 11.3 Eligibility for SOCSO Coverage

**Who is COVERED**:
- ✅ Permanent employees
- ✅ Temporary employees
- ✅ Casual workers
- ✅ Part-time workers
- ✅ Contract workers
- ✅ All wage levels (no minimum/maximum)

**Who is EXCLUDED** (CRITICAL - HIGH EXAM FREQUENCY):

| Excluded Category | Reason |
|-------------------|--------|
| **Self-employed persons** | No employer-employee relationship |
| **Domestic workers** | Special category, separate scheme |
| **Government servants** | Covered by government pension scheme |
| **Foreign workers** | Some voluntary coverage provisions |

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 2** *(Links to Section 11.3 - Eligibility)*

**Question**: "Which categories exempted from SOCSO?
I. Self-employed persons
II. Government servants
III. Contract workers

A. I and II ✅
B. II and III
C. I and III
D. All the above"

**Answer**: A (I and II)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **Self-employed** and **Government servants** exempted; **Contract workers** are COVERED!

**ANALYSIS**:

**I. Self-employed persons** ✅ EXEMPTED:
- Run own business
- No employer
- BUT: Can join **Self-Employment Social Security Scheme** (voluntary)
- Not automatically covered under SOCSO

**II. Government servants** ✅ EXEMPTED:
- Government employees
- Covered by **government pension scheme** instead
- Different social security system
- Federal, state, local government workers

**III. Contract workers** ❌ NOT EXEMPTED (ARE COVERED):
- **COVERED under SOCSO!**
- As long as employer-employee relationship exists
- Employed under contract of service
- Must be registered and contribute

**COMMON TRAP**:
```
Students think: "Contract" = Temporary = Exempted
REALITY: Contract workers = COVERED
```

**KEY DISTINCTION**:
```
EXEMPTED:
- Self-employed (NO employer)
- Government servants (Different scheme)
- Domestic workers (Special category)
- Foreign workers (Generally)

COVERED:
- Permanent workers ✅
- Temporary workers ✅
- Casual workers ✅
- Part-time workers ✅
- CONTRACT WORKERS ✅ (Don't confuse!)
```

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Self-employed" = Exempted (no employer)
- "Government servants" = Exempted (different scheme)
- "Contract workers" = COVERED (employed!)

**MEMORY TIP**: **"SGDF Out, Workers In"** (Self/Govt/Domestic/Foreign out; all workers in)

### 11.4 Contribution Rates

**CRITICAL NUMBERS** (MUST MEMORIZE):

| Party | Rate | Based On |
|-------|------|----------|
| **Employee** | **0.5%** | Monthly wages |
| **Employer** | **1.75%** | Monthly wages |
| **Total** | **2.25%** | Monthly wages |

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: Most common question - memorize 0.5% (employee) and 1.75% (employer)!

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 3** *(Links to Section 11.4 - Contribution Rates)*

**Question**: "What are contribution rates by employees and employers?
A. Employees 0.5% and employers 1.75% ✅
B. Employees 0.75% and employers 1.50%
C. Employees 0.5% and employers 1.50%
D. Employees 0.75% and employers 1.75%"

**Answer**: A (Employee 0.5%, Employer 1.75%)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **0.5% + 1.75% = 2.25% total** - Employee pays LESS, Employer pays MORE!

**MEMORIZATION TABLE**:

| | Employee | Employer | Total |
|-|----------|----------|-------|
| **Rate** | 0.5% | 1.75% | 2.25% |
| **Ratio** | 1 part | 3.5 parts | 4.5 parts |

**CALCULATION EXAMPLE**:
```
Employee Monthly Salary: RM3,000

Employee Contribution:
RM3,000 × 0.5% = RM15

Employer Contribution:
RM3,000 × 1.75% = RM52.50

Total to SOCSO:
RM15 + RM52.50 = RM67.50
```

**WHY THESE RATES?**:
- Employee bears **SMALLER** burden (0.5%)
- Employer bears **LARGER** burden (1.75%)
- Employer responsible for employee welfare
- Social security shared responsibility

**COMMON EXAM TRAPS**:

❌ **Trap 1**: Confusing rates
```
Wrong: 0.75% employee (EPF rate confusion)
Right: 0.5% employee
```

❌ **Trap 2**: Equal sharing
```
Wrong: Both pay 1.125% (equal)
Right: 0.5% + 1.75% (unequal)
```

❌ **Trap 3**: Reversed rates
```
Wrong: Employee 1.75%, Employer 0.5%
Right: Employee 0.5%, Employer 1.75%
```

**KEYWORDS**:
- "0.5%" = Employee (smaller)
- "1.75%" = Employer (larger)
- "Monthly wages" = Contribution base

**MEMORY TRICK**: 
```
"HALF percent for EMPLOYEE" (0.5%)
"Almost TWO percent for EMPLOYER" (1.75%)
```

**VISUAL MEMORY**:
```
Employee: 0.5% = 🪙 (small coin)
Employer: 1.75% = 🪙🪙🪙 (multiple coins - more than 3x employee)
```

### 11.5 Definition of "Wages" for SOCSO

**What is INCLUDED in "Wages"**:
- ✅ Basic salary
- ✅ Allowances (housing, transport, etc.)
- ✅ Commissions
- ✅ Overtime pay
- ✅ **Payments for leave** (annual leave, sick leave)

**What is EXCLUDED from "Wages"**:

| Excluded Item | Reason |
|---------------|--------|
| **Annual bonus** | One-time payment, not regular wages |
| **Employer's contribution to statutory funds** | Employer's separate obligation (EPF, SOCSO) |
| Service charge/tips | Not guaranteed, variable |
| Gratuity | Retirement payment |
| Retrenchment compensation | Termination payment |

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 5** *(Links to Section 11.5 - Wages Definition)*

**Question**: "Which payments NOT considered wages for SOCSO?
I. Annual bonus
II. Employer's contribution to statutory fund
III. Payments for leave

A. I and II ✅
B. II and III
C. I and III
D. None of the above"

**Answer**: A (I and II)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **Regular payments** = Wages; **One-time/Statutory** = NOT wages!

**DETAILED ANALYSIS**:

**I. Annual Bonus** ✅ NOT WAGES:
- **One-time** annual payment
- Not regular monthly income
- Discretionary (not guaranteed)
- EXCLUDED from SOCSO contribution calculation

**Example**:
```
Employee Monthly Salary: RM3,000
Annual Bonus: RM6,000 (2 months)

SOCSO Contribution Based On:
Monthly: RM3,000 only (NOT RM3,000 + RM500 bonus portion)
```

**II. Employer's Contribution to Statutory Fund** ✅ NOT WAGES:
- **Employer's separate legal obligation**
- Examples: EPF contribution, SOCSO contribution itself
- NOT part of employee's wages
- Already separate line item

**Example**:
```
Employee Salary: RM3,000
Employer EPF: RM390 (13%)
Employer SOCSO: RM52.50 (1.75%)

Wages for SOCSO calculation: RM3,000 only
(NOT RM3,000 + RM390 + RM52.50)
```

**III. Payments for Leave** ❌ **IS WAGES** (INCLUDED):
- **Regular income replacement**
- Annual leave pay, sick leave pay
- Part of normal employment terms
- INCLUDED in wages calculation

**Example**:
```
Employee takes 5 days annual leave
Receives: RM500 leave pay

This RM500 IS included in wages for SOCSO
Contributes: RM500 × 0.5% = RM2.50
```

**WAGES DETERMINATION PRINCIPLE**:
```
INCLUDED (IS Wages):
✅ Regular monthly payments
✅ Guaranteed employment income
✅ Salary, allowances, overtime
✅ Leave payments
✅ Commissions

EXCLUDED (NOT Wages):
❌ One-time bonuses
❌ Employer's statutory contributions
❌ Gratuity/retirement payments
❌ Retrenchment compensation
❌ Tips/service charges (variable)
```

**WHY THIS MATTERS**:
- Affects contribution calculation
- Higher "wages" = Higher contribution
- Must know what to include/exclude
- Compliance requirement

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Annual bonus" = One-time, NOT wages
- "Statutory fund contribution" = Separate, NOT wages
- "Leave payments" = Regular, IS wages

**MEMORY TIP**: **"Regular = Wages, One-time = Not Wages"**

### 11.6 Employer and Employee Categories

**TWO Types of Employers** (CRITICAL):

#### 1. Principal Employer

**Definition**: 
- Employer who **directly employs** the employee
- **Undertakes payment** of wages
- Handles all **service matters** (HR, administration)

**Example**:
```
ABC Manufacturing Sdn Bhd
↓ (directly hires)
Factory Worker
↓
ABC pays salary, manages employment
Result: ABC = Principal Employer
```

---

#### 2. Immediate Employer

**Definition**:
- Contractor/subcontractor who **directly supervises** work
- May not pay wages (principal employer pays)
- Employee works under immediate employer's supervision

**Example**:
```
ABC Company (Principal Employer)
↓ (contracts to)
XYZ Contractor (Immediate Employer)
↓ (supervises)
Worker on site
↓
Worker physically works for XYZ
But ABC pays wages
```

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 9 & 10** *(Links to Section 11.6 - Employer Types)*

**Question 9**: "Employers under SOCSO categorized into:
I. Principal employer
II. Immediate employer

A. I only
B. II only
C. I and II only ✅
D. Neither I nor II"

**Answer**: C (Both I and II)

**Question 10**: "Principal Employer refers to:
I. Employer who employed employee directly
II. Employer who undertakes payment of wages and service matters

A. I only
B. II only
C. I and II only ✅
D. Neither I nor II"

**Answer**: C (Both I and II)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **TWO employer types** exist; Principal = Direct employment + Wages/Service responsibility!

**COMPREHENSIVE ANALYSIS**:

**Question 9 - Two Categories**:
Both categories exist in SOCSO framework:
- **Principal Employer**: Main employer (direct employment, pays wages)
- **Immediate Employer**: On-site supervisor (may be contractor)

**Question 10 - Principal Employer Definition**:
BOTH statements define Principal Employer:

**I. Direct Employment** ✅:
- Hired employee directly
- Employment contract with principal
- Not through intermediary

**II. Payment & Service Matters** ✅:
- Pays wages to employee
- Handles HR administration
- Manages employment relationship
- Responsible for benefits, leave, etc.

**RELATIONSHIP DIAGRAM**:
```
Typical Scenario:

PRINCIPAL EMPLOYER
(Main Company)
    ├── Directly employs ✅
    ├── Pays wages ✅
    ├── Handles service matters ✅
    └── SOCSO registration ✅

IMMEDIATE EMPLOYER
(Contractor/Supervisor)
    ├── Supervises work day-to-day
    ├── May not pay wages
    └── On-site management
```

**PRACTICAL EXAMPLE**:
```
Construction Project:

ABC Developer (Principal Employer):
- Hires construction workers
- Pays monthly salaries
- Manages HR, benefits
- Registers with SOCSO

XYZ Contractor (Immediate Employer):
- Supervises workers on site
- Directs daily work
- Reports to principal
- NOT responsible for SOCSO
```

**RESPONSIBILITY ALLOCATION**:

| Responsibility | Principal Employer | Immediate Employer |
|----------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **SOCSO Registration** | ✅ YES | ❌ NO |
| **SOCSO Contributions** | ✅ YES | ❌ NO |
| **Wage Payment** | ✅ YES | Usually NO |
| **Day-to-day Supervision** | May delegate | ✅ YES |
| **Legal Liability** | ✅ PRIMARY | Secondary |

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Principal employer" = Direct hire + Wage payer
- "Immediate employer" = Supervisor/contractor
- "Both categories" = Two types exist
- "I and II" = Complete definition

**MEMORY TIP**: 
```
PRINCIPAL = PAYS (wages + contributions)
IMMEDIATE = IN-PERSON (supervision)
```

### 11.7 Key Facts About SOCSO

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 4** *(Links to Section 11.7 - SOCSO Key Facts)*

**Question**: "Which TRUE for SOCSO?
I. Scheme compulsory except for exempted workers
II. If have SOCSO + other insurance, can only claim from one
III. After age 60, can still be covered if employer contributes

A. I and II
B. II and III
C. I and III
D. None of the above ✅"

**Answer**: D (None of the above)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **Statement II and III are FALSE** - only Statement I is true, but "None" means all must be true!

**STATEMENT ANALYSIS**:

**I. Scheme compulsory except exempted workers** ✅ **TRUE**:
- SOCSO is **MANDATORY** for eligible workers
- Exemptions: Self-employed, government servants, domestic workers, foreign workers
- Not optional for covered employees

**II. Can only claim from one scheme** ❌ **FALSE**:
- Can claim from **BOTH SOCSO AND other insurance**!
- SOCSO is **SOCIAL SECURITY**, not insurance exclusion
- Other insurance (life, PA, medical) **INDEPENDENT**
- Can claim medical from SOCSO + medical insurance
- Can receive SOCSO pension + life insurance payout

**Example**:
```
Worker injured at work (EIIS coverage):

From SOCSO:
- Medical treatment (free)
- Temporary disablement benefit (RM2,000/month)
- Rehabilitation

From Personal Accident Insurance:
- RM50,000 lump sum payout
- Medical expense reimbursement

Result: CAN CLAIM FROM BOTH! ✅
```

**III. After age 60, can be covered if employer contributes** ❌ **FALSE**:
- **INVALIDITY scheme**: Must be UNDER 60 at diagnosis
- **EMPLOYMENT INJURY scheme**: No age limit for NEW injuries
- BUT: Once reach 60, **STOP contributing** to Invalidity Scheme
- NEW employees aged 60+ = Only Employment Injury coverage

**Correct Rule**:
```
Employee aged 58:
- Contributes to BOTH schemes ✅

Employee turns 60:
- STOPS contributing to Invalidity Scheme ❌
- CONTINUES Employment Injury coverage only ✅

Employee hired at age 62:
- Only Employment Injury coverage ✅
- NO Invalidity Scheme coverage ❌
```

**WHY "NONE OF THE ABOVE"?**:
- Statement I: TRUE ✅
- Statement II: FALSE ❌
- Statement III: FALSE ❌
- Question asks which are TRUE
- Only I is true, but answer options require multiple true statements
- Since II and III are false, answer = None of the above

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Compulsory" = Mandatory (TRUE)
- "Only claim from one" = FALSE (can claim multiple)
- "After 60 coverage" = FALSE (Invalidity stops at 60)

**MEMORY TIP**: **"SOCSO + Insurance = Both Okay!"** (not mutually exclusive)

### 11.8 SOCSO Mission Statements

I. Promote work safety/health of workers

A. I and II ✅
B. II and III
C. I and III
D. All the above"

**Answer**: A (I and II)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: Mission focuses on **SAFETY promotion** (workers AND employers), NOT fund strengthening!

**ANALYSIS**:

**I. Promote work safety/health of WORKERS** ✅ MISSION:
- Core SOCSO objective
- Prevent workplace injuries
- Protect employee welfare
- Education and awareness programs

**II. Promote work safety/health of EMPLOYERS** ✅ MISSION:
- Encourage safer workplaces
- Employer education programs
- Incentivize safety compliance
- Reduce overall claims

**III. Strengthen fund through contribution review** ❌ NOT MISSION:
- This is **OPERATIONAL STRATEGY**, not mission
- Mission = Social welfare goals
- Fund management = Implementation method
- Contribution rates = Technical matter

**DISTINCTION**:
```
MISSION (What we aim to achieve):
✅ Worker safety and health
✅ Employer safety promotion
✅ Social security protection
✅ Rehabilitation services

STRATEGIES (How we achieve it):
- Fund management
- Contribution rate setting
- Technology adoption
- Service delivery improvement
```

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Mission" = Core purpose
- "Promote safety/health" = Protection objective
- "Strengthen fund" = Strategy, not mission

### 11.9 Appeals Process

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 8** *(Links to Section 11.9 - Appeals)*

**Question**: "If employer/employee/dependents not satisfied with SOCSO decision, appeal to:
A. SOCSO Board
B. Social Security Appellate Board ✅
C. Medical and Hospital Appeal Board
D. Social Security Appellate Board" *(Note: B and D same - likely typo in original)*

**Answer**: B or D (Social Security Appellate Board)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: Appeals go to **INDEPENDENT** Appellate Board, NOT internal SOCSO Board!

**APPEALS STRUCTURE**:
```
STEP 1: SOCSO Decision (claim approval/rejection)
    ↓ (if not satisfied)
STEP 2: Appeal to Social Security Appellate Board
    ↓ (independent review)
STEP 3: Binding Decision
```

**WHY APPELLATE BOARD?**:
- **Independent** body (not SOCSO internal)
- Ensures **fair review**
- Legal expertise
- Quasi-judicial function

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Appeal" = Challenge decision
- "Appellate Board" = Independent review body
- NOT "SOCSO Board" (internal)

### Common Exam Traps

| Trap | Students Think | Reality | Remember |
|------|---------------|---------|----------|
| **Contract workers exempted** | "Contract" = not covered | Contract workers COVERED | All workers covered except SGDF |
| **Contribution rates** | 0.75% employee | 0.5% employee | HALF percent |
| **Only one claim** | SOCSO exclusive | Can claim SOCSO + Insurance | Both allowed |
| **Age 60 coverage** | Can continue if pay | Invalidity stops at 60 | Age limit for IPS |
| **Bonus is wages** | Include in contributions | Annual bonus EXCLUDED | One-time ≠ wages |
| **Leave not wages** | Excluded from wages | Leave payments INCLUDED | Regular payment |
| **Fund strengthening mission** | Part of mission | Strategy, not mission | Mission = welfare |
| **SOCSO Board appeal** | Internal appeal | Appellate Board (external) | Independent review |
