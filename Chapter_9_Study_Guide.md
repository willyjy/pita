### 9.3.1 Fundamental Insurance Principles

**THREE Core Principles (HIGH EXAM FREQUENCY):**

```
FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES
├── 1. Utmost Good Faith (Uberrimae Fidei)
├── 2. Insurable Interest
└── 3. Proximate Cause
```

---

#### Principle 1: UTMOST GOOD FAITH (Uberrimae Fidei)

**Definition**: Both parties must act with complete honesty and disclose all material facts.

**Duty of Disclosure** (FSA 2013, Schedule 9, Paragraph 3(4)(1)):

**Proposer MUST disclose:**
- Any matter **known** to be relevant to insurer's decision
- Any matter a **reasonable person** would expect to know is relevant

---

**What is a MATERIAL FACT?**

**Definition**: Fact that would influence insurer's decision to:
- Accept the risk
- Set premium amount
- Determine policy terms

**Example Material Facts:**
- Previous insurance declined/cancelled
- Criminal convictions
- Pre-existing medical conditions
- Previous claims history
- Occupation hazards
- Dangerous hobbies

---

**FOUR Ways to Breach Utmost Good Faith:**

| Breach Type | Description | Intent | Example |
|-------------|-------------|--------|---------|
| **1. Non-disclosure** | Failure to disclose material fact | Unintentional (considers it inconsequential) | Not mentioning previous minor accident |
| **2. Concealment** | Willful failure to disclose | **INTENTIONAL** (to get cheaper cover) | Hiding heart condition for life insurance |
| **3. Fraud** | Premeditated false information | **DELIBERATE DECEPTION** | Forging medical reports |
| **4. Innocent Misrepresentation** | Unintentional incorrect information | Accidental/mistake | Genuinely mistaken about past illness dates |

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: 
- **Non-disclosure** = Didn't think important (unintentional)
- **Concealment** = Knew important, hid it (willful)
- **Fraud** = Premeditated lies
- **Innocent Misrep** = Honest mistake

---

**Case Law Examples:**

**1. Abu Bakar v. Oriental Fire (1974) - NON-DISCLOSURE**

**Facts**: 
- Insured applied for fire insurance
- Question: "For what purposes premises occupied?"
- Answer: "Sundry shop downstairs, dwelling first floor"
- OMITTED: Four grinding mills for curry powder at back
- Fire occurred
- Insurer denied claim (failed to disclose mills)

**Held**: 
- ✅ Insurers NOT misled about nature of insurance
- ✅ NO evidence mills increased risk
- ✅ Insurers LIABLE - NO breach of utmost good faith

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Not ALL omissions = breach. Must be MATERIAL (affects risk assessment).

---

**2. Goh Chooi Leng v Public Life (1964) - CONCEALMENT**

**Facts**:
- Life assured applied for insurance (May 1960)
- Question: "Ever had advice about heart/lung/cough?"
- Answer: **"No"**
- TRUTH: Treated for tuberculosis, hospitalized Nov 1958-March 1959
- Life assured died
- Assignee claimed

**Held**:
- ❌ NON-DISCLOSURE of material fact
- ❌ Contract VOIDABLE
- ❌ Insurer NOT LIABLE

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Medical history is ALWAYS material for life insurance!

---

**3. United Oriental v. Mazzarol (1984) - INNOCENT MISREPRESENTATION**

**Facts**:
- Proposal form for boat insurance
- Question ONLY asked: Accidents to **OTHER boats** owned
- Did NOT ask: Accidents to **THIS boat**
- Insured boat sank
- Insurer avoided liability (previous accident to THIS boat not disclosed)

**Held**:
- ✅ Although NOT asked specifically
- ✅ Previous accident to THIS boat = MATERIAL FACT
- ✅ MUST be disclosed regardless
- ✅ Insurer can avoid contract

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Material facts must be disclosed **EVEN IF NOT SPECIFICALLY ASKED**!

---

**What NEED NOT Be Disclosed** (FSA 2013, Schedule 9, Paragraph 3(4)(2)):

| Exclusion | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Diminishes risk** | Makes risk LOWER | Installed sprinkler system (fire insurance) |
| **Common knowledge** | Public information | General crime statistics in area |
| **Known to insurer** | Insurer should know in ordinary course | Standard industry risks |
| **Waived by insurer** | Insurer doesn't require disclosure | Optional questions left blank |

**Deemed Waiver** (FSA 2013, Schedule 9, Paragraph 3(4)(3)):
If proposer:
- Fails to answer question
- Gives incomplete/irrelevant answer
AND insurer **doesn't pursue further**
→ Compliance with duty of disclosure **DEEMED WAIVED**

#### Principle 2: INSURABLE INTEREST

**Definition**: Policyholder must have legal or financial stake in subject matter insured.

**Purpose**: Prevent insurance from becoming **GAMBLING/WAGERING**.

---

**Insurable Interest in LIFE INSURANCE** (FSA 2013, Schedule 8, Paragraph 3):

**Requirement**: Must exist at **TIME OF CONTRACT INCEPTION**

**Who has insurable interest in your life?**

| Relationship | Insurable Interest | Example |
|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Yourself** | Unlimited | Own life policy |
| **Spouse** | YES | Husband insures wife's life |
| **Child (under 18)** | YES | Parent insures child |
| **Ward (under 18)** | YES | Guardian insures ward |
| **Employee** | YES | Employer insures key employee |
| **Person you depend on** | YES (wholly/partly dependent) | Elderly parent depends on adult child |

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: Life insurance insurable interest needed at **INCEPTION** only, NOT at claim time.

---

**Insurable Interest in GENERAL INSURANCE**:

**Requirement**: Must exist at **TIME OF LOSS/CLAIM**

**Who has insurable interest?**

| Party | Property | Reason |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **Owner** | Own property | Direct financial interest |
| **Mortgagee/Bank** | Mortgaged property | Loan security |
| **Business Partner** | Partner's contribution | Business continuity |
| **Creditor** | Debtor's property/life | Debt repayment ability |
| **Trustee/Bailee** | Property in care | Responsibility for safeguarding |

📌 **EXAM TIP**: 
- **Life Insurance**: Insurable interest at **INCEPTION**
- **General Insurance**: Insurable interest at **LOSS/CLAIM**

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 10** *(Links to Section 9.3.1 - Insurable Interest & Legal Purpose)*

**Question**: "Raymond Mah is hard drug pusher. Asks agent to insure goods including illegal drugs. Agent rejects. Why?
A. Illegal purpose – against public policy ✅
B. No insurable interest exist
C. Agent wrong, drugs can be insured
D. FSA 2013 specifically forbids dealing with drug pusher"

**Answer**: A (Illegal purpose – against public policy)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: Insurance contracts require **LEGAL PURPOSE** - cannot insure illegal activities!

**ANALYSIS**:

**A** ✅ **CORRECT** - Fundamental contract principle:
- Insurance contract requires **LEGAL PURPOSE**
- **Cannot promote illegal activities** (against public policy)
- Insuring illegal drugs = Facilitating crime
- **Public policy** prevents this

**WHY PUBLIC POLICY?**
```
If illegal goods could be insured:
→ Criminals protected from loss
→ Insurance encourages illegal trade
→ Society harmed
→ Against public interest
```

**B** ❌ WRONG - Technically he HAS insurable interest:
- He owns the drugs (property owner)
- Financial stake in their safety
- BUT: Insurable interest ALONE not enough
- Must ALSO have legal purpose!

**C** ❌ WRONG - Agent is CORRECT to refuse:
- Illegal goods cannot be insured
- Would violate public policy
- Agent could face legal liability

**D** ❌ WRONG - Too specific:
- FSA 2013 doesn't need SPECIFIC drug provision
- General **legal purpose** requirement covers it
- Broader public policy principle applies

**LEGAL BASIS**:
```
Valid Contract Requires:
1. Offer ✓
2. Acceptance ✓
3. Consideration ✓
4. Legal Capacity ✓
5. LEGAL PURPOSE ✗ (FAILED - illegal drugs)
6. Genuine Consent ✓

Result: Cannot form valid contract
```

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Illegal purpose" = Against law
- "Public policy" = Society's interests
- "Cannot promote illegal activities"

**SIMILAR EXAMPLES**:
- Cannot insure stolen goods
- Cannot insure proceeds of crime
- Cannot insure illegal gambling operation

**MEMORY TIP**: **"No Insurance for Illegal Business"** (Public policy protects society)

#### Principle 3: PROXIMATE CAUSE

**Definition**: The actual, real, or dominant cause that sets in motion chain of events leading to loss.

**Purpose**: Determine if insurer liable under policy (was loss caused by insured peril?).

**Key Concept**: "PROXIMATE" = **DOMINANT/EFFECTIVE** cause, not necessarily **NEAREST IN TIME**.

---

**Example: Smith v. Cornhill Insurance (1938)**

**Facts**:
- Motor policy: Death if "result solely of bodily injury by violent accidental means while riding insured car"
- Insured: Severe head injuries in car accident (fell down ravine)
- After accident: Wandered aimlessly through brushwood
- Stepped into stream → Shock of immersion → Heart failure → Death
- Insurer: Refused payment (shock of immersion = intervening factor)

**Held**:
- ✅ Accident = PRIMARY CAUSE
- ✅ Led to sequence: injury → wandering → immersion → shock → death
- ✅ NO intervening causes
- ✅ **UNBROKEN CHAIN** of events
- ✅ Insurer LIABLE

**Principle**: If unbroken chain of events from insured peril to loss, proximate cause = insured peril.

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Look for **UNBROKEN CHAIN** - no independent intervening cause that breaks the connection.

### 9.4 Unique Characteristics of Insurance Contracts

**FIVE Special Characteristics** (memorize):

```
INSURANCE CONTRACT CHARACTERISTICS
├── 1. Aleatory
├── 2. Unilateral
├── 3. Conditional
├── 4. Personal
└── 5. Adhesion
```

---

#### 1. ALEATORY Contract

**Definition**: Unequal exchange - parties may receive unequal values.

**How it works in insurance**:
```
Scenario 1: No Claim
Insured pays: RM2,000 (premium)
Insurer pays: RM0
Insurer "wins"

Scenario 2: Major Claim
Insured pays: RM2,000 (premium)
Insurer pays: RM500,000 (claim)
Insured "wins"
```

**vs. COMMUTATIVE Contract** (equal exchange):
- Buy car: Pay RM50,000 → Receive car worth RM50,000
- Mutual agreement on value exchanged

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Insurance = **ALEATORY** (dependent on uncertain event), not commutative (equal exchange).

---

#### 2. UNILATERAL Contract

**Definition**: Only ONE party (insurer) makes enforceable promise.

**How it works**:
- **Insurer**: Legally bound to pay if loss occurs (enforceable promise)
- **Insured**: NO legal obligation to continue paying premiums (can lapse)

**Example**:
```
After paying first premium:
Insured: Can stop paying (policy lapses, no lawsuit)
Insurer: MUST pay claim if loss occurs during coverage (enforceable)
```

**vs. BILATERAL Contract** (both parties bound):
- Employment contract: Employee must work, Employer must pay

---

#### 3. CONDITIONAL Contract

**Definition**: Insurer's obligation dependent on insured fulfilling conditions.

**Common Conditions**:
- Timely premium payment
- Truthful information at application
- Notify insurer of changes
- Claims notification within specified time
- Cooperation in claims investigation

**Example**:
```
Condition: Notify claim within 30 days
Insured notifies after 60 days
Result: Insurer may deny claim (condition breached)
```

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Contract fulfillment **CONDITIONAL** on compliance with terms.

---

#### 4. PERSONAL Contract

**Definition**: Contract is personal to the insured, not the property.

**Key Points**:
- Protects PERSON/ENTITY, not property itself
- Based on insured's character, morals, credit status
- **NOT automatically transferable**
- If property sold, policy **DOES NOT follow** new owner

**Example**:
```
Mr. Tan owns house, has fire insurance
Sells house to Mr. Lee
Insurance: Does NOT transfer to Mr. Lee
Mr. Lee: Must apply for NEW insurance
(Insurer assesses Mr. Lee's character/credit separately)
```

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: Property insurance is **PERSONAL** - does NOT automatically transfer with property sale!

---

#### 5. ADHESION Contract

**Definition**: Insured must accept contract "as is" - no negotiation.

**Characteristics**:
- Insurer designs and prints policy
- Terms **pre-printed**
- Insured: **Take it or leave it** (no modification)
- **No negotiation** between parties

**Legal Protection for Insured**:
```
DOCTRINE: Contract of Adhesion
↓
Court Interpretation Rule:
"Any AMBIGUITY in contract interpreted IN FAVOR OF INSURED"
↓
Reason: Insurer drafted it, insured had no input
```

**Presumption of Intent Doctrine**:
- Person who signs contract **PRESUMED** to have:
  - Read the contract
  - Understood terms
  - Agreed to conditions
- Bound by signed terms

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Adhesion contracts = **No negotiation**, but ambiguities favor insured (court protection).

### 9.5 Law of Agency in Insurance

**Definition**: Legal relationship where agent acts on behalf of principal (insurer).

---

#### 9.5.1 Agency Relationship

**Structure**:
```
PRINCIPAL (Insurer)
    ↓ (appoints)
AGENT (Insurance Agent)
    ↓ (interacts with)
THIRD PARTY (Policyholders/Clients)
```

**Agent's Role**:
- Market insurance products
- Collect premiums
- Provide customer service
- Act on behalf of insurer

---

#### 9.5.2 Three Types of Agent Authority

**CRITICAL CONCEPT** (HIGH EXAM FREQUENCY):

| Authority Type | Source | Description | Example |
|---------------|--------|-------------|---------|
| **EXPRESS Authority** | Explicitly granted (written/oral) | Direct authorization from insurer | Agency contract states: "Can solicit applications, collect premiums" |
| **IMPLIED Authority** | Incidental to express | Actions reasonably necessary to execute express authority | Express: Sell policies → Implied: Answer client questions, arrange meetings |
| **APPARENT Authority** | Insurer's representations | Public believes agent has authority based on insurer's conduct | Insurer gives agent business cards, office = Public assumes agent authorized |

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: 
- **Express** = Directly stated
- **Implied** = Necessary to carry out express
- **Apparent** = Looks authorized to public

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 7** *(Links to Section 9.5.2 - Types of Authority)*

**Question**: "Agent's authority to act can be in following ways:
i. Express authority
ii. Implied authority
iii. Apparent authority
iv. Secret authority

A. I only
B. I, II and III ✅
C. I and II only
D. All the above"

**Answer**: B (I, II and III)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: THREE recognized types of authority - Express, Implied, Apparent.

**ANALYSIS**:

**i. Express Authority** ✅ VALID:
- Explicitly stated in agency contract
- Written or oral authorization
- Clear, direct appointment

**ii. Implied Authority** ✅ VALID:
- Incidental to express authority
- Reasonably necessary powers
- Not stated but understood

**iii. Apparent Authority** ✅ VALID:
- Ostensible authority
- Based on insurer's representations
- Public perception of authority

**iv. Secret Authority** ❌ NOT VALID:
- **NO such concept** in agency law!
- Authority cannot be "secret"
- Either authorized or not
- Made-up option (exam trap!)

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Express" = Explicitly stated
- "Implied" = Incidental/necessary
- "Apparent" = Ostensible/perceived

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 8** *(Links to Section 9.5.2 - Express vs Implied)*

**Question**: "In agency law, ___ is incidental authority required to execute ___ of principal.
A. Express authority; implied authority
B. Implied authority; express authority ✅
C. Apparent authority; implied authority
D. Implied authority; Apparent authority"

**Answer**: B (Implied authority; express authority)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **IMPLIED** authority is what's needed to EXECUTE **EXPRESS** authority!

**RELATIONSHIP**:
```
EXPRESS Authority (Stated)
    ↓ (requires for execution)
IMPLIED Authority (Incidental)
```

**EXPLANATION**:
- **EXPRESS** = What you're told to do (sell insurance)
- **IMPLIED** = What you need to do it (answer questions, meet clients)

**EXAMPLE**:
```
EXPRESS: "You are authorized to sell life insurance policies"
    ↓ (To accomplish this, you IMPLIED authority to:)
IMPLIED: 
- Answer client questions about products
- Arrange meetings with prospects
- Explain policy features
- Collect proposal forms
- Provide premium quotes
```

**WHY OTHER OPTIONS WRONG**:

❌ **A**: Backwards - Express is not incidental to implied
❌ **C**: Apparent is not incidental to implied (different concept)
❌ **D**: Implied is not incidental to apparent (wrong relationship)

**MEMORY TIP**: **"IMPLIED helps you EXECUTE EXPRESS"**

---

#### 9.5.3 Agent Filling Proposal Forms (FSA 2013, Schedule 9, Paragraph 12)

**CRITICAL PROVISION** (HIGH EXAM FREQUENCY):

**When agent fills proposal form**:
- Agent acts for **INSURER** (principal), **NOT** client
- Agent's knowledge = Insurer's knowledge
- Agent's statements = Insurer's statements

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 4** *(Links to Section 9.5.3 - Agent Filling Forms)*

**Question**: "When agent fills proposal form from client, who is he acting for under FSA 2013 Schedule 9 Para 12?
A. For the Insurer - principal ✅
B. For the client
C. For himself
D. For the beneficiary"

**Answer**: A (For the Insurer - principal)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: Agent filling form = Acts for **INSURER**, NOT client (protects client).

**WHY THIS MATTERS**:

**If agent acts for CLIENT**:
```
Client: Tells agent wrong information
Agent: Writes it on form
Problem: Who's responsible? Client!
Issue: Client may not understand technical terms
Result: UNFAIR to client
```

**If agent acts for INSURER** (FSA 2013 rule):
```
Client: Tells agent information
Agent: Writes it on form (acts for INSURER)
Problem: Agent writes incorrectly
Result: INSURER responsible (agent is their representative)
Protection: CLIENT protected from agent errors
```

**FSA 2013 Schedule 9, Paragraph 12**:
- Agent completing proposal = Agent of **INSURER**
- Protects proposer from agent mistakes
- Insurer cannot deny claim based on agent's filling errors

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Filling proposal form" = Critical activity
- "Acting for insurer" = Agent represents principal
- "Schedule 9 Para 12" = Specific provision

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 5** *(Links to Section 9.5.3 - Agent Fraudulent Statement)*

**Question**: "If agent Jeffery makes fraudulent statement to induce client to buy policy, his statement deemed to be statement made by ___, under Para 12(2) Schedule 9 FSA 2013.
A. Agent himself
B. Agent's manager
C. Principal-insurer ✅
D. CEO of company"

**Answer**: C (Principal-insurer)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: Agent's fraudulent statement = **INSURER'S statement** (insurer bears liability).

**LEGAL PRINCIPLE**:
```
Agent's Actions = Principal's Actions
Agent's Knowledge = Principal's Knowledge
Agent's Statements = Principal's Statements
```

**WHY THIS RULE?**:
1. **Protection for Client**: Client relies on agent as insurer's representative
2. **Agency Law**: Principal liable for agent's acts within scope of authority
3. **Fair Attribution**: Insurer hired agent, insurer bears risk

**EXAMPLE**:
```
Agent Jeffery: "This policy covers ALL diseases" (FALSE - fraud)
Client: Buys policy based on this statement
Reality: Policy excludes certain diseases
Claim: Denied for excluded disease

Legal Position:
- Jeffery's fraudulent statement = INSURER'S statement
- Insurer liable for agent's fraud
- Cannot deny claim based on exclusion
- Client protected
```

**FSA 2013 Schedule 9, Paragraph 12(2)**:
Statement by agent = Statement by insurer (for purpose of proposal)

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Fraudulent statement" = False info to induce
- "Deemed to be" = Legally treated as
- "Principal-insurer" = Liability rests with insurer

### 9.6 Negligence and Tort Liability

#### 9.6.1 Definition of Tort

**TORT** = Private wrong/civil wrong (not criminal)

**Key Quote** (memorize):
> "Tortious liability arises from breach of duty primarily fixed by law; duty is towards persons generally; breach is redressible by action for unliquidated damages."

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 1** *(Links to Section 9.6.1 - Tort Definition)*

**Question**: "A private wrong is violation of rights of another person, such wrong is ___, wrongdoer is ___.
A. crime; criminal
B. liability; risk taker
C. penalty; criminal
D. tort; tort feasor ✅"

**Answer**: D (tort; tort feasor)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: Know the correct TERMINOLOGY - Tort & Tort Feasor!

**DEFINITIONS**:
- **TORT** = Private/civil wrong (violation of another's rights)
- **TORT FEASOR** = Person who commits tort (wrongdoer)

**vs. CRIME**:
```
CRIME (Public Wrong):
- Wrong against STATE/SOCIETY
- Prosecuted by government
- Punishment: Fine, imprisonment
- Person: CRIMINAL

TORT (Private Wrong):
- Wrong against INDIVIDUAL
- Sued by injured party
- Remedy: DAMAGES (money)
- Person: TORT FEASOR
```

**EXAMPLES OF TORTS**:
- Negligence (car accident)
- Trespass (entering property without permission)
- Defamation (false statements damaging reputation)
- Nuisance (interfering with enjoyment of property)

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Private wrong" = Not crime, civil matter
- "Tort" = Civil wrong
- "Tort feasor" = Wrongdoer in tort

**MEMORY TIP**: **"TORTfeasor = MADE-a-TORT"** (person who made the tort)

---

#### 9.6.2 Types of Torts

**TWO Categories**:

| Type | Description | Intent | Examples |
|------|-------------|--------|----------|
| **INTENTIONAL Torts** | Carried out with malicious intent | DELIBERATE | Assault, battery, slander, fraud |
| **UNINTENTIONAL Torts** | Result of negligence/carelessness | ACCIDENTAL | Car accidents, professional errors |

⚠️ **EXAM ALERT**: Most insurance-related torts = **UNINTENTIONAL** (negligence).

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 2** *(Links to Section 9.6.2 - Intentional Torts)*

**Question**: "___ are those tortuous behaviours carried out with malicious intent.
A. Intentional torts ✅
B. Unintentional torts
C. Negligence
D. Professional negligence"

**Answer**: A (Intentional torts)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **"Malicious intent"** = Key phrase for INTENTIONAL torts!

**INTENTIONAL TORTS**:
- **Malicious** = With bad intent
- **Premeditated** = Planned
- **Deliberate** = On purpose

**EXAMPLES**:
- **Assault**: Threatening harm intentionally
- **Battery**: Intentional harmful touching
- **Slander**: Deliberately false spoken statements
- **Fraud**: Intentional deception for gain

**vs. UNINTENTIONAL**:
- **Negligence**: Carelessness (no intent to harm)
- **Professional negligence**: Professional error (no malice)

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Malicious intent" = Deliberately harmful
- "Intentional" = On purpose

---

#### 9.6.3 NEGLIGENCE (Unintentional Tort)

**Definition**: Breach of legal duty to take care, resulting in undesired damage.

**Three Elements Required**:
1. **Duty of Care** existed
2. **Breach** of that duty
3. **Damage/Loss** resulted from breach

**Examples**:
- Flower pot on ledge blown by wind, injures pedestrian
- Lawyer designs poor contract → client loses money
- Surgeon leaves scissors in patient's abdomen

---

**Financial Planner's Duty of Care**:
- Provide **prudent and diligent** advice
- Effect appropriate instruments for client's intention
- Exercise professional competence

**Breach Example**:
```
Financial planner drafts will
Error: Didn't get two witnesses to sign
OR: Got beneficiary to sign as witness
Result: Will invalid, beneficiary loses inheritance
Liability: Financial planner breached duty of care
Remedy: Client can sue for damages
```

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Duty of care = Professional must act with reasonable care and skill.

---

#### 9.6.4 CONTRIBUTORY NEGLIGENCE

**Definition**: Injured party hurt partly due to **OWN** negligence.

**Principle**: Everyone has duty to look after own safety.

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 3** *(Links to Section 9.6.4 - Contributory Negligence)*

**Question**: "___ means injured party hurt partly due to own negligence.
A. Intentional negligence
B. Unintentional negligence
C. Contributory negligence ✅
D. Professional negligence"

**Answer**: C (Contributory negligence)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **"OWN negligence"** = Contributory negligence (injured party contributed).

**CONCEPT**:
```
Total Damage: RM100,000
Defendant's fault: 70%
Plaintiff's fault: 30% (own negligence = CONTRIBUTORY)

Damages Awarded: RM70,000 (reduced by 30%)
```

**EXAMPLE**:
```
Car Accident:
- Driver A: Speeding (negligent)
- Driver B: Not wearing seatbelt (contributory negligence)

Driver B injured:
Medical bills: RM50,000
Court finds:
- Driver A: 80% at fault
- Driver B: 20% contributory negligence (no seatbelt)

Damages awarded: RM40,000 (RM50,000 × 80%)
Reduced by B's contribution
```

**Legal Basis (Malaysia)**:
Civil Law Act 1956, Section 12:
- Damages reduced by claimant's share of responsibility
- Court determines "just and equitable" reduction

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Contributory" = Contributing to
- "Own negligence" = Plaintiff's fault too
- "Partly due to" = Shared responsibility

**MEMORY TIP**: **"Contributed to OWN harm"** = Contributory negligence

---

#### 9.6.5 PROFESSIONAL NEGLIGENCE

**Definition**: Negligent acts by professionals causing breach of duty and damage.

**Professionals Subject to This**:
- Doctors
- Lawyers
- Accountants
- Architects
- **Financial Planners**

**Not ALL professional acts = liability**:
Only negligent acts that:
1. Breach duty
2. Cause damage/loss/injury

**Example - Doctor**:
```
Scenario 1: NOT Negligence
- Surgery performed with due care
- Patient dies (surgery complication)
- Result: NO breach (inherent risk, not negligence)

Scenario 2: NEGLIGENCE
- Surgery performed with due care
- BUT: Left scissors inside patient
- Scissors pierce organs → excessive bleeding → death
- Result: PROFESSIONAL NEGLIGENCE (breach of duty)
```

**Coverage**: **Professional Indemnity Insurance**

### 9.7 Anti-Money Laundering Act 2001 (AMLA)

**Full Name**: Anti-Money Laundering, Anti-Terrorism Financing, and Proceeds of Unlawful Activities Act 2001

**Background**:
- Based on FATF (Financial Action Task Force) recommendations
- FATF: Inter-governmental organization (Paris, 1989)
- 40 + 9 recommendations (money laundering + terrorism financing)
- Malaysia enacted: July 5, 2001
- Effective: January 15, 2002
- Latest amendment: November 8, 2014

---

#### 9.7.1 Money Laundering Process (THREE STAGES)

**CRITICAL CONCEPT** (HIGH EXAM FREQUENCY):

| Stage | Description | Insurance Example |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| **1. PLACEMENT** | Introduce illicit funds into financial system | Purchase insurance policies with large cash payments |
| **2. LAYERING** | Move funds through transactions to obscure origin | Borrow against policies, make claims on insured valuables, switch policies |
| **3. INTEGRATION** | Integrate laundered money into economy as legitimate | Receive claim payouts, use for business investments |

📌 **EXAM TIP**: Memorize the **THREE STAGES** - Placement, Layering, Integration (order matters!).

---

#### 9.7.2 Suspicious Transaction Indicators

**Front-End Indicators** (at application):

| Red Flag | Example |
|----------|---------|
| **Not proportionate to financial standing** | Low-income person buying RM1 million policy |
| **Reluctant to provide details** | Avoids personal/corporate information |
| **Multiple small policies cancelled** | Buy many, cancel together (>RM50,000 cash) |
| **Exceeds threshold** | Meets red flag criteria |
| **Frequently changing insurer** | Pattern of switching |
| **Different addresses** | Home ≠ mailing address |
| **Foreign client seeking lump sum** | Offers foreign currency payment |
| **Premiums exceed apparent means** | Cannot afford based on stated income |
| **Not interested in performance** | Only asks about early cancellation/surrender |
| **Change beneficiaries** | To non-family, unusual parties |
| **Anonymous payment instruments** | Bearer instruments, cash equivalents |
| **Unclear fund sources** | Cannot explain where money from |

**Back-End Indicators** (during policy):

| Red Flag | Example |
|----------|---------|
| **Claims to unusual beneficiaries** | Not next of kin, no insurable interest |
| **Series of policy loans** | Multiple loans/repayments short period |
| **Large payments to 3rd parties** | Unrelated person, other country |
| **High risk acceptance** | Willing to pay for cases likely rejected/claimed |

---

#### 9.7.3 Compliance Requirements

**Record-Keeping**:
- **Minimum**: 6 years post-transaction
- All transactions documented
- Customer interactions recorded
- Audit trail maintained

**Reporting**:
- Suspicious transactions → Financial Intelligence Unit (Bank Negara Malaysia)
- Mandatory reporting
- Timely submission

**Training**:
- Initial AML/CFT training
- Ongoing training
- Risk-based approach
- Agent awareness of legal responsibilities

**Customer Due Diligence (CDD)**:
- Verify identity
- Assess risk profile
- Understand nature of business
- Source of funds

### 9.8 Minor's Capacity (FSA 2013, Schedule 8, Paragraph 4)

**Age Requirements**:

| Age | Capacity | Requirement |
|-----|----------|-------------|
| **10-16 years** | Can enter life insurance contract | WITH written consent from parent/guardian |
| **16+ years** | Can enter contract independently | NO consent needed (full capacity) |

---

**📝 EXAM QUESTION 9** *(Links to Section 9.8 - Minor's Capacity)*

**Question**: "Para 4 Schedule 8 FSA 2013, minor may enter life insurance contract on:
A. Age 9, no consent
B. Age 10, no consent
C. Age 9, with consent
D. Age 10, with consent ✅"

**Answer**: D (Age 10, with parental consent)

**🔑 KEY INSIGHT**: **Age 10** is the threshold, but **CONSENT required** until age 16.

**AGE FRAMEWORK**:
```
Below Age 10: CANNOT enter contract (even with consent)
Age 10-15: CAN enter WITH written parental/guardian consent
Age 16+: CAN enter INDEPENDENTLY (no consent needed)
```

**WHY Age 10**?:
- Legal capacity starts at 10 (Malaysian law)
- Balance between protection and autonomy
- Parental oversight until 16

**WHAT MINOR CAN DO** (ages 10-16 with consent):
- Enter life insurance on OWN life
- Enter life insurance on ANOTHER life (with insurable interest)
- Assign policies
- Receive assignments

**KEYWORDS**:
- "Age 10" = Minimum age
- "With consent" = Parent/guardian written approval
- "Minor" = Under 18 (but 16+ has full capacity for insurance)
