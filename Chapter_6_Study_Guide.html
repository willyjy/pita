Chapter 6: Annuities

Comprehensive Exam Preparation Guide with Question Mapping

📚 Chapter Overview

Annuities are the OPPOSITE of life insurance - they protect against living TOO LONG, not dying too soon. This chapter covers retirement income products that convert lump sums into steady income streams.

Exam Weight: 8-10% of total exam
Study Time Recommendation: 6-8 hours

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you must be able to:

  1. Comprehend the role of annuities in retirement planning (longevity risk)
  2. Distinguish between types of annuities (fixed vs variable, immediate vs deferred, etc.)
  3. Understand the two phases of annuities (accumulation vs liquidation)
  4. Evaluate tax treatment of annuities in Malaysia
  5. Analyze suitability of annuities for different demographics
  6. Compare annuities with other retirement products

📖 Complete Chapter Summary

6.0 Introduction

The Core Problem: Risk of “Living Too Long” and outliving savings

Solution: Annuities = systematic liquidation of capital to provide lifetime income

🔑 KEY CONCEPT - The Opposite of Life Insurance:

Product Protects Against Focus Payment Direction
Life Insurance Dying TOO SOON (premature death) Mortality risk You pay premiums → Insurer pays beneficiaries on death
Annuity Living TOO LONG (outliving savings) Longevity risk You pay lump sum → Insurer pays you periodic income

📌 EXAM TIP: Annuity = REVERSE life insurance. Life insurance converts small payments into large sum; annuity converts large sum into small periodic payments.

Malaysian Context:

Status Details Reason
Current Popularity LOW - not actively promoted Limited public awareness
Future Trend Expected to GROW Tax changes, aging population, EPF concerns
Global Comparison USA, Japan = Very popular Aging population, inflation concerns, retirement needs

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: While not currently popular in Malaysia, annuities are expected to become more important as population ages.

📝 EXAM QUESTION 1 (Links to Section 6.0 - Introduction)

Question: “Which is most suited product for client’s retirement years? A. Whole life policy with profits B. Endowment policy maturing at age 60 C. A life annuity ✅ D. All of the above”

Answer: C (A life annuity)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: For RETIREMENT INCOME needs, annuity is the BEST solution!

WHY ANNUITY IS BEST: - ✅ Provides LIFETIME income (can’t outlive it) - ✅ Regular payments for living expenses - ✅ Specifically designed for retirement years - ✅ Protects against longevity risk

WHY OTHERS ARE LESS SUITABLE: - A. Whole Life: Pays on DEATH, not for retirement income - Benefit goes to beneficiaries, not you - Doesn’t provide regular income stream

  • B. Endowment (age 60): ONE-TIME lump sum at maturity
    • What if you live to 90? (30 more years!)
    • Must manage lump sum yourself
    • Can outlive the money

KEYWORDS: - “Retirement years” = Need INCOME ✅ - “Most suited” = Annuity = PURPOSE-BUILT for retirement ✅ - “Life annuity” = LIFETIME payments ✅

MEMORY TIP:

RETIREMENT = Need INCOME (not lump sum)
INCOME for LIFE = ANNUITY

6.1 Understanding Annuities

Definition: Contract with insurer where insurer makes periodic payments to annuitant, starting immediately or future date, for lifetime or specified term.

Formal Definition (memorize): > “An annuity is a contract with an insurance company providing periodic payments to the annuitant for a lifetime or specified term, serving as reverse life insurance focusing on longevity risk.”

6.1.a Function of Annuities

Primary Function: Mitigate LONGEVITY RISK

What is Longevity Risk? - Risk of outliving your assets - Living longer than expected - Running out of money in old age

How Annuities Help: - Convert lump sum → steady income stream - Provide financial security - Give peace of mind (guaranteed income) - Can’t outlive payments (if lifetime annuity)

📝 EXAM QUESTION 2 (Links to Section 6.1.a - Function)

Question: “What is basic function of annuity? A. To provide lifetime replacement or supplementary income immediately ✅ B. To provide inflation adjusted living during retirement C. To protect against medical expenses in old age D. To guarantee high income and enjoyable retirement”

Answer: A (Provide lifetime replacement/supplementary income)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Basic function = INCOME PROVISION, nothing fancy!

ANALYSIS OF EACH OPTION:

A ✅ CORRECT - The BASIC function: - “Lifetime” = Core feature (longevity protection) - “Replacement or supplementary” = Can replace work income OR supplement other income - “Immediately” = For immediate annuities (can also be deferred) - This is the FUNDAMENTAL purpose

B ❌ WRONG - Too specific: - Inflation adjustment is OPTIONAL feature, not basic function - Not all annuities are inflation-adjusted - This is a NICE-TO-HAVE, not core purpose

C ❌ WRONG - Wrong product! - Medical expenses = HEALTH INSURANCE function - Annuities = INCOME, not medical coverage - Completely different purpose

D ❌ WRONG - Too optimistic/unrealistic: - “Guarantee high income” = FALSE (depends on amount invested) - “Enjoyable retirement” = Subjective, not insurance function - Overpromises what annuity can do

KEYWORDS: - “Basic function” = CORE purpose (income provision) - “Lifetime” = LONGEVITY protection - “Replacement/supplementary” = INCOME focus

EXAM TIP: Don’t be distracted by fancy features (inflation, medical, “enjoyable”). Basic function = PROVIDE INCOME.

6.1.b Life Cycle of Annuities

TWO PHASES (CRITICAL to understand):

ANNUITY LIFE CYCLE

Phase 1: ACCUMULATION → Phase 2: LIQUIDATION
(Building)              (Paying Out)

6.1.b.i Accumulation Phase

Definition: Period of capital investment into annuity.

What Happens:

Activity Description Result
Premium Payment Annuity holder pays premiums Fund grows
Payment Type Lump-sum OR periodic Flexibility
Interest Accrual Invested amount earns interest Principal increases
Investment Growth Based on underlying investment strategy Fund accumulation

Example:

Age 30-60 (30 years accumulation):
Monthly premium: RM500
Total paid: RM500 × 12 × 30 = RM180,000
With 5% annual return: Fund grows to ~RM416,000

📌 EXAM TIP: Accumulation phase = PAY IN period (you’re building the fund).

6.1.b.ii Liquidation Phase

Definition: Period where accumulated funds distributed back to annuitant.

What Happens:

Activity Description Result
Payment Commencement Regular payments begin Income stream starts
Payment Structure Fixed period OR lifetime Depends on annuity type
Fund Depletion Systematic withdrawal of capital Provides income
Longevity Protection Payments continue (lifetime annuity) Can’t outlive

Example:

Age 60+ (liquidation begins):
Accumulated fund: RM416,000
Monthly payment: RM3,000 (calculated actuarially)
Duration: Lifetime (even if live to 100+)
If live 30 years: Receive RM3,000 × 12 × 30 = RM1,080,000!
(Much more than paid in due to pooling and mortality credits)

📌 EXAM TIP: Liquidation phase = PAY OUT period (insurer pays you income).

Comparison Table:

Aspect Accumulation Phase Liquidation Phase
Direction Money IN (you → insurer) Money OUT (insurer → you)
Duration Variable (years/decades) Fixed period OR lifetime
Purpose Build capital Provide income
Who Benefits Future you Current you
Risk Investment risk Longevity risk covered

6.1.c Classification of Annuities

FIVE Classification Bases (memorize):

  1. Number of Lives Covered
    • Single life
    • Multiple lives (e.g., husband & wife)
  2. Method of Premium Payment
    • Single premium
    • Periodic premiums
  3. Timing of Income Payments
    • Immediate (starts now)
    • Deferred (starts later)
  4. Method of Disposing Proceeds
    • Immediate distribution
    • Deferred to specific date
    • Payments to certain age (e.g., age 65)
  5. Denomination of Benefits
    • Various currencies (USD, RM, etc.)

📌 EXAM TIP: These classifications form the basis for understanding different annuity types.

6.1.d.i Fixed-Sum vs Variable Annuity

Fixed-Sum (Fixed) Annuity:

Feature Description Example
Payment Amount FIXED, predetermined RM2,000/month (never changes)
Guarantee Amount guaranteed Certainty of income
Inflation Risk HIGH - purchasing power erodes RM2,000 today ≠ RM2,000 in 20 years
Investment Risk LOW - insurer bears risk Safe but inflexible
Suitability Conservative, risk-averse Those wanting certainty

Example:

Fixed Annuity: RM2,000/month
Year 1: RM2,000 (buys 1,000 eggs @ RM2 each)
Year 20: RM2,000 (inflation: eggs now RM4, buys only 500 eggs)
Result: Same nominal amount, LESS real purchasing power

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Fixed annuities = INFLATION RISK (purchasing power erodes over time).

Variable Annuity:

Feature Description Example
Payment Amount VARIES based on investment performance RM2,000-4,000/month (fluctuates)
Guarantee NO guarantee on amount Uncertainty
Inflation Risk LOWER - can keep pace with inflation Payments may increase
Investment Risk HIGH - annuitant bears risk Market exposure
Suitability Growth-oriented, can handle volatility Those wanting potential growth

Example:

Variable Annuity tied to equity index:
Good year: RM3,500/month (index up 15%)
Bad year: RM1,800/month (index down 10%)
Average: ~RM2,500/month (better than fixed over long term if markets grow)

📌 EXAM TIP: - Fixed = Certainty but inflation risk - Variable = Growth potential but market risk

6.1.d.ii Immediate vs Deferred Annuity

Immediate Annuity:

Feature Description Malaysian Context
Payment Start IMMEDIATELY (within 1 year) Right after purchase
Purchase Lump sum only EPF lump sum, inheritance, retirement gratuity
Accumulation Phase NONE No growth period
Typical Buyer Already retired, needs income NOW Age 60+

Example:

Mr. Tan retires at 60:
EPF lump sum: RM500,000
Buys immediate annuity: RM3,500/month for life
Payments start: Next month
No accumulation needed - income begins immediately

Deferred Annuity:

Feature Description Malaysian Context
Payment Start LATER (after accumulation period) 10-30 years later
Purchase Single OR periodic premiums Regular savings during working years
Accumulation Phase YES - fund grows Important growth period
Typical Buyer Still working, planning ahead Age 30-50

Example:

Ms. Lee age 35:
Buys deferred annuity: RM500/month premium
Accumulation: Age 35-60 (25 years)
Fund grows with interest
Payments start: Age 60
Monthly income: RM4,000/month for life

📌 EXAM TIP: - Immediate = For those who need income NOW (already retired) - Deferred = For those PLANNING AHEAD (still working)

Historical Note - Malaysia:

EPF Life Annuity Scheme (discontinued):
- Launched to convert EPF to lifetime income
- Two versions: Conventional & Takaful
- Both immediate and deferred options
- Discontinued due to pricing, administration, sales practice issues

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: EPF annuity scheme existed but DISCONTINUED - know this for Malaysian context questions.

6.1.d.iv Single Payment vs Instalment Annuity

Single Payment (Single Premium) Annuity:

Feature Immediate Annuity Deferred Annuity
Payment ONE lump sum ONE lump sum
Income Start Immediately After deferral period
Refund if Death N/A (already paying out) Purchase price refundable (with/without interest)
Surrender Value N/A Allowed during deferral

Example:

Immediate Single Premium:
Pay: RM500,000 (one time)
Receive: RM3,500/month immediately

Deferred Single Premium:
Pay: RM200,000 (one time, age 40)
Wait: 20 years (accumulation with interest)
Receive: RM4,000/month starting age 60

Instalment (Periodic Premium) Annuity:

Feature Details
Payment Regular premiums over time (monthly, annually)
Nature ALWAYS deferred (need time to accumulate)
Refund if Death Premiums paid refundable during accumulation
Surrender Value Available during accumulation period
Flexibility Can adjust payments (some plans)

Example:

Instalment Annuity:
Pay: RM500/month for 25 years (age 35-60)
Total paid: RM150,000
Fund grows: ~RM300,000 (with interest)
Receive: RM3,000/month starting age 60

If death at age 45 (during accumulation):
Beneficiaries receive: Premiums paid + interest

📌 EXAM TIP: Instalment plans are ALWAYS DEFERRED (by nature, need time to pay installments).

6.1.d.v Single Life vs Joint Life Annuity

Single Life Annuity:

Feature Description Example
Coverage ONE person only Mr. Tan only
Payment Duration As long as annuitant lives Until Mr. Tan dies
Death of Annuitant Payments STOP No more payments to anyone
Cost Lower (shorter expected duration) Pays for one life

Example:

Mr. Tan, age 60:
Buys single life annuity: RM3,500/month
Lives to 85 (25 years): Receives RM3,500 × 12 × 25 = RM1,050,000
Dies at 85: Payments STOP, nothing to wife/children

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Single life = Payments stop at death, NOTHING to beneficiaries (unless annuity certain - see later).

Joint-Life Annuity:

Feature Description Example
Coverage TWO people (usually spouses) Mr. & Mrs. Tan
Payment Duration Until FIRST death Stops when EITHER dies
Death of First Payments STOP completely Surviving spouse gets NOTHING
Cost Moderate Shorter than survivorship

Example:

Mr. & Mrs. Tan:
Joint-life annuity: RM4,000/month
Mr. Tan dies age 75: Payments STOP
Mrs. Tan (still alive age 72): NO MORE PAYMENTS
Problem: Left survivor without income!

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Joint-life = Stops at FIRST death (not ideal for protecting survivor).

Survivorship Annuity (Last-to-Die):

Feature Description Example
Coverage TWO people Mr. & Mrs. Tan
Payment Duration Until LAST death Continues as long as ONE alive
Death of First Payments CONTINUE to survivor Protects surviving spouse
Cost HIGHEST (longest expected duration) Pays longest
Variation Reduced payment after first death (e.g., 75% of original) Common structure

Example:

Mr. & Mrs. Tan:
Survivorship annuity: RM4,000/month
Mr. Tan dies age 75: Payments CONTINUE
Mrs. Tan receives: RM4,000/month (or reduced to RM3,000)
Until Mrs. Tan dies age 88
Total duration: 28 years (age 60-88)

📌 EXAM TIP: Survivorship = Most expensive but BEST protection for couples (survivor continues to receive income).

📝 EXAM QUESTION 5 (Links to Section 6.1.d.v - Joint Life vs Survivorship)

Question: “For , annuity payments cease on first death. For , annuity payments until death of last annuitant. A. survivorship annuity; joint-life B. joint-life; survivorship annuity ✅ C. Single life annuity; joint life annuity D. Immediate life annuity; survivorship annuity”

Answer: B (joint-life; survivorship annuity)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Distinguish between FIRST death vs LAST death!

ANALYSIS:

First blank: “cease on FIRST death” - = JOINT-LIFE annuity ✅ - When EITHER person dies (first death), payments STOP - Survivor gets NOTHING

Second blank: “until death of LAST annuitant” - = SURVIVORSHIP annuity ✅ - Payments continue as long as ONE person alive - Stops only when BOTH dead (last death)

MEMORY TIP:

JOINT-LIFE = JOINS together, DIES together
(Stops at FIRST death - both must be alive)

SURVIVORSHIP = SURVIVOR continues
(Continues until LAST death - protects survivor)

VISUAL AID:

Joint-Life:
Husband dies → STOP ❌
Wife still alive, but no payments

Survivorship:
Husband dies → CONTINUE ✅
Wife still receives payments
Until wife dies → STOP

KEYWORDS: - “Cease on FIRST death” = JOINT-LIFE - “Until LAST annuitant” = SURVIVORSHIP

6.1.d.vi Pure Life vs Annuity Certain

Pure Life Annuity (Straight Life Annuity):

Feature Description Risk/Benefit
Payment Duration Lifetime of annuitant ONLY Can’t outlive ✅
At Death Payments STOP immediately Nothing to heirs ❌
Fully Liquidated Fund considered fully used up No residual value ❌
Payment Amount HIGHEST (for same premium) Maximum income ✅
Gamble If die early, lose money; if live long, win Longevity bet

Example:

Mr. Ahmad age 60:
Pure life annuity: RM4,000/month
Paid: RM500,000 premium

Scenario 1: Dies age 62 (2 years)
Received: RM4,000 × 12 × 2 = RM96,000
Lost: RM404,000 (insurer keeps) ❌

Scenario 2: Lives age 90 (30 years)
Received: RM4,000 × 12 × 30 = RM1,440,000
Gained: RM940,000 more than paid! ✅

Scenario 3: Dies age 70 (10 years)
Received: RM480,000
About break-even

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Pure life = HIGHEST monthly payment but NOTHING to beneficiaries at death.

📝 EXAM QUESTION 4 (Links to Section 6.1.d.vi - Pure Life Annuity)

Question: “Ah Seng purchased annuity, receives payments as long as he lives. Amount invested fully liquidated on death. This is: A. Pure life annuity ✅ B. Deferred annuity C. Instalment annuity D. Annuity certain”

Answer: A (Pure life annuity)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Two key phrases identify PURE LIFE: 1. “As long as he lives” = LIFETIME payments 2. “Fully liquidated on death” = NOTHING left for heirs

KEYWORD ANALYSIS:

✅ “Receives payments as long as he lives” - = LIFETIME duration - Continues until death - Can’t outlive payments

✅ “Amount invested fully liquidated on death” - = NO residual value - Nothing to beneficiaries - Fund completely used up - This is THE defining feature of PURE LIFE!

WHY NOT OTHERS:

❌ B. Deferred - This refers to WHEN payments START (immediate vs deferred), not the TYPE of payout structure

❌ C. Instalment - This refers to HOW PREMIUM IS PAID (single vs periodic), not payout characteristics

❌ D. Annuity Certain - This has GUARANTEED period payments regardless of life/death (opposite of pure life)

MEMORY TIP:

PURE LIFE = PURE gamble on longevity
- Live long = WIN BIG ✅
- Die early = LOSE ❌
- Nothing left = PURE (nothing for heirs)

Annuity Certain (Period Certain / Term Certain):

Feature Description Risk/Benefit
Payment Duration FIXED period regardless of life/death Guaranteed minimum ✅
If Death During Period Payments CONTINUE to beneficiaries Heirs protected ✅
NOT Life-Dependent Payments made regardless of survival Certainty ✅
Payment Amount LOWER (for same premium) Trade-off for guarantee ❌
Types Can be immediate OR deferred Flexible

Common Structures:

  1. Life Annuity with Period Certain (e.g., 10 years certain)
Mr. Lee age 60:
Life annuity with 10 years certain: RM3,500/month

Scenario 1: Dies age 65 (5 years)
Payments continue to beneficiaries: 5 more years
Total paid: 10 years guaranteed

Scenario 2: Lives to 85 (25 years)
Payments continue: All 25 years (beyond 10-year guarantee)
Protects against both early death AND longevity
  1. Installment Refund Annuity
Premium paid: RM500,000
Monthly payment: RM3,000

Dies after receiving: RM180,000 (5 years)
Balance refunded to heirs: RM320,000
Ensures premium fully returned
  1. Cash Refund Annuity
Similar to installment refund but balance paid as LUMP SUM

📌 EXAM TIP: “Certain” = GUARANTEED period, payments continue even if annuitant dies.

Comparison Table: Pure Life vs Annuity Certain

Feature Pure Life Annuity Certain
At Death Payments STOP Payments CONTINUE (if within certain period)
Beneficiaries Get NOTHING Get remaining payments/balance
Monthly Payment HIGHEST LOWER
Longevity Protection YES YES (plus minimum guarantee)
Early Death Risk HIGH (lose money) LOW (protected)
Suitability Healthy, no heirs concern Family protection important

📝 EXAM QUESTION 3 (Links to Section 6.1.d - Types of Annuities)

Question: “Which is generally true of annuity? A. For those who expect to live long, annuity not good option B. Computation exactly same as life insurance C. Lump sum deposited with insurer in exchange for stream of payments ✅ D. Younger person receives larger annuity payment than older”

Answer: C (Lump sum deposited for stream of payments)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: This is the FUNDAMENTAL DEFINITION of how annuities work!

ANALYSIS OF EACH OPTION:

C ✅ CORRECT - This IS what annuity does: - “Lump sum deposited” = You give money upfront (or accumulate) - “Exchange for” = Contract trade - “Stream of payments” = Regular periodic income - “In future” = Over time (liquidation phase) - This is the ESSENCE of annuity concept!

A ❌ FALSE - Complete OPPOSITE of truth! - Annuities are PERFECT for those expecting to live long - Live longer = Get more total payments - Protects against outliving savings - This statement is BACKWARDS!

B ❌ FALSE - Computation is OPPOSITE: - Life Insurance: Small premiums → Large lump sum (mortality focus) - Annuity: Large lump sum → Small periodic payments (longevity focus) - Actuarial methods related but NOT the same

D ❌ FALSE - OPPOSITE is true: - OLDER person receives LARGER payment (shorter life expectancy) - YOUNGER person receives SMALLER payment (longer life expectancy) - Same fund must last longer for younger = smaller per payment

Example Proof (Why D is wrong):

Fund: RM500,000

Age 60 annuitant:
Life expectancy: 20 more years
Payment: RM500,000 / 20 years = RM2,083/month

Age 40 annuitant:
Life expectancy: 40 more years
Payment: RM500,000 / 40 years = RM1,042/month

OLDER person gets LARGER payment (same fund, shorter expected duration)

KEYWORDS: - “Lump sum” = UPFRONT capital - “Stream of payments” = REGULAR income - “Exchange” = CONVERSION process

MEMORY TIP:

ANNUITY = OPPOSITE of saving
SAVING: Little by little → Build big amount
ANNUITY: Big amount → Distribute little by little

6.2 Tax Treatment of Annuity in Malaysia

Regulatory Framework: Public Ruling No. 4/2014 by Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia (LHDN)

THREE Key Tax Benefits:

6.2.a Deductibility of Premiums

Tax Relief Details:

Aspect Details Exam Importance
Amount Up to RM3,000 per year ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Eligible Products Deferred annuity OR PRS (Private Retirement Scheme) ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Eligibility Malaysian residents only ⭐⭐⭐
Period Year of assessment 2012-2025 ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Purpose Encourage retirement savings ⭐⭐⭐

Example Calculation:

Mr. Tan's income tax calculation:

Chargeable income: RM100,000
Tax without relief: ~RM14,000

Deferred annuity premium paid: RM5,000
Tax relief claimed: RM3,000 (maximum)

New chargeable income: RM100,000 - RM3,000 = RM97,000
Tax with relief: ~RM13,400
Savings: RM600 (immediate tax benefit!)

📌 EXAM TIP: Maximum deductible = RM3,000 (not unlimited!). Period ends 2025.

6.2.b Exemption of Annuity Income

Tax Exemption Details:

Aspect Details Benefit
Income Received From deferred annuity policy TAX-FREE ✅
All Payments Every annuity payment exempt No tax reporting needed
Net Benefit Increases effective income More money in pocket
Advantage Makes annuities attractive Better than taxable investments

Example:

Without annuity income exemption:
Annuity payment: RM3,000/month = RM36,000/year
Tax (assume 14% bracket): RM5,040
Net received: RM30,960

With annuity income exemption:
Annuity payment: RM3,000/month = RM36,000/year
Tax: RM0 (EXEMPT!)
Net received: RM36,000
Extra benefit: RM5,040/year!

📌 EXAM TIP: Annuity income is TAX EXEMPT - this is a MAJOR benefit making annuities attractive.

📝 EXAM QUESTION 6 (Links to Section 6.2.b - Tax Treatment)

Question: “What is tax position of annuity received from life and Takaful companies? A. Taxed as income B. Taxed as capital gain C. Not taxed as income but as retirement gratuity D. Tax exempt as of year of assessment 1995 ✅”

Answer: D (Tax exempt as of 1995)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Annuity income is FULLY TAX EXEMPT in Malaysia!

HISTORICAL CONTEXT: - Before 1995: Annuity income was taxable - From 1995 onwards: Tax exemption granted - Reaffirmed 2012: Public Ruling 4/2014 confirmed exemption - Purpose: Encourage retirement savings, make annuities attractive

WHY OTHERS WRONG:

❌ A. Taxed as income: FALSE - Was true before 1995, but NOT anymore

❌ B. Taxed as capital gain: FALSE - Not treated as capital gain at all

❌ C. Not taxed but as retirement gratuity: FALSE - Simply tax exempt, not reclassified as gratuity

PRACTICAL IMPACT:

Mr. Lee's retirement income:
Annuity: RM3,000/month = RM36,000/year (TAX-FREE) ✅
Part-time work: RM20,000/year (TAXABLE) ❌

Tax calculation:
Taxable income: RM20,000 only (not RM56,000!)
Tax savings significant!

KEYWORDS: - “Tax exempt” = NO TAX on annuity payments - “1995” = Year exemption started - “Life and Takaful” = Both covered

MEMORY TIP: “Tax-Free Ninety-FIVE” (1995 = tax-free annuity)

Insurer Tax Benefits:

Aspect Details Purpose

Why This Matters: - Insurers can offer better rates - More annuity products developed - Market growth stimulated - Win-win: Government, insurers, consumers

6.3.c Tax Planning

Strategic Considerations:

  1. Maximize Tax Relief

📝 EXAM QUESTION 8 (Links to Section 6.1 - Annuity Terminology)

Question: “For annuity, person whose life governs annuity payments is , while money considerations in exchange for payment is . A. insured; order price B. annuitant; purchase price ✅ C. insured; retirement fund D. annuitant; retirement fund”

Answer: B (annuitant; purchase price)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Know the CORRECT TERMINOLOGY for annuity contracts!

KEY TERMS:

1. ANNUITANT (not “insured”): - = Person whose life governs the payments - = Receives the annuity income - = Analogous to “life insured” in life insurance - BUT: Correct term in annuities = ANNUITANT

2. PURCHASE PRICE (not “premium”): - = Money paid to buy the annuity - = Can be lump sum or accumulated premiums - = Consideration for the contract - Also called: “Consideration” or “Principal”

WHY “ANNUITANT” not “INSURED”?

Life Insurance: INSURED (person whose death triggers payment)
Annuity: ANNUITANT (person whose LIFE triggers payments)

Different context, different terminology!

COMPLETE ANNUITY TERMINOLOGY: - Annuitant: Person receiving payments (life governs duration) - Purchase Price: Money paid for annuity - Annuity Period: Duration of payments - Annuity Commencement Date: When payments begin - Beneficiary: Receives remaining benefits if annuity certain

MEMORY TIP:

ANNUITANT = ANNUITY recipient
PURCHASE PRICE = PRICE you purchase annuity for

6.4.b Impact of Economic Factors

Inflation Sensitivity (CRITICAL ISSUE):

Problem with Fixed Annuities:

Year 1: RM3,000/month (buys basket of goods)
Year 10 (3% inflation): RM3,000/month (buys 25% less goods)
Year 20 (3% inflation): RM3,000/month (buys 45% less goods)
Year 30 (3% inflation): RM3,000/month (buys 58% less goods)

Purchasing power ERODED!

Solution: Cost-of-Living Adjustments (COLAs) - Payments increase with inflation - Maintain purchasing power - Higher initial cost (lower starting payment) - Better long-term protection

Market Volatility: - Affects variable annuities - Investment performance impacts payments - Diversification important - Risk/return trade-off

📝 EXAM QUESTION 7 (Links to Section 6.4.b - Annuity Limitations)

Question: “Which is FALSE regarding purchase of annuity? A. With fixed-income annuities, inflation can erode purchasing power B. Those who cannot risk lump sum should consider annuity C. Converting funds to annuity ensures regular income D. Converting savings to annuity is way to provide for annuitant’s heirs ✅”

Answer: D (Provide for heirs - FALSE)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Annuities are for ANNUITANT’S INCOME, not for LEAVING INHERITANCE!

ANALYSIS OF EACH:

A ✅ TRUE statement: - Fixed annuities DO suffer from inflation - RM3,000 today ≠ RM3,000 in 20 years - This is a KNOWN PROBLEM with fixed annuities

B ✅ TRUE statement: - If can’t handle investment risk with lump sum - Annuity = Professional management - Guaranteed income (especially fixed) - Good option for risk-averse

C ✅ TRUE statement: - Annuity = Conversion of lump sum to income stream - Regular, predictable payments - This IS what annuity does!

D ❌ FALSE statement - This is the WRONG answer: - Annuities are for ANNUITANT’S RETIREMENT INCOME - NOT designed to leave money to heirs - Pure life annuity: NOTHING to heirs - If you want to leave inheritance: BUY LIFE INSURANCE instead!

DISTINCTION TO UNDERSTAND:

Want regular income for self? → ANNUITY ✅
Want to leave money to heirs? → LIFE INSURANCE ✅

Annuity = LIVE TOO LONG protection (for you)
Life Insurance = DIE TOO SOON protection (for heirs)

EXCEPTION TO NOTE: - Annuity certain: CAN provide for heirs (if die during certain period) - But this is NOT the PRIMARY purpose - Main purpose still = Income for annuitant

KEYWORDS: - “Provide for heirs” = LIFE INSURANCE function, not annuity - “FALSE” = Look for incorrect statement - “Annuitant’s heirs” = Wrong product focus

EXAM TIP: Don’t confuse annuity (income for living) with life insurance (money for heirs)!

6.4.d Practical Limitations

DISADVANTAGES of Annuities:

1. Liquidity & Flexibility

Issue Details Impact
Locked In Funds not easily accessible Can’t access for emergencies
Early Withdrawal High penalties (surrender charges) Expensive to exit
Inflexible Can’t adjust easily once set up Limited control

Emergency need: RM100,000 (medical)
Surrender annuity:
Surrender value: RM400,000 (penalty applied)
Loss: RM100,000 (20% penalty!)
Plus: Lost future guaranteed income

Result: Severe financial loss

📌 EXAM TIP: Liquidity is a MAJOR DRAWBACK - only commit funds you won’t need!

2. Costs and Charges

Fee Type Description Impact
Administrative Fees Ongoing management Reduces effective return
Surrender Charges Early exit penalties (can be 5-10%) Locks you in

3. Inflation Risk (Fixed Annuities) - Already discussed - purchasing power erosion - Fixed payments lose value over time - Long retirement (30+ years) = severe impact

4. Opportunity Cost - Money locked in annuity - Could potentially earn higher returns elsewhere - Trade-off: Security vs Growth potential

5. Complexity - Many types and options - Difficult for average person to understand - Requires professional advice (additional cost)

📝 EXAM QUESTION 9 (Links to Section 6.1 - Annuity Definition)

Question: “An annuity may be defined as insurance contract that provides ___. A. Fixed sum for fixed period to annuitant’s heirs on death B. Variable sum of money payable to those reaching age 55 C. Regular payment stream for specific period, either fixed period or lifetime ✅ D. Lump sum payment at intervals such as every 5 years”

Answer: C (Regular payment stream for specific period or lifetime)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Focus on KEY DEFINING FEATURES - regular payments, specific period OR lifetime.

ANALYSIS:

C ✅ CORRECT - Captures essence of annuity: - “Regular payment stream” = Not lump sum, but periodic ✅ - “Specific period” = Can be fixed term (annuity certain) ✅ - “OR lifetime” = Can be life-based (pure life) ✅ - Covers BOTH main types: certain and life annuities

A ❌ WRONG - Multiple errors: - “To heirs” = NO, annuities are for ANNUITANT, not heirs - “On death” = NO, annuities are for LIVING (longevity protection) - This describes LIFE INSURANCE, not annuity!

B ❌ WRONG - Too narrow/specific: - “Variable sum” = Only variable annuities (not fixed) - “Age 55” = Too specific, can start at any agreed age - Misses the essence of regular payments

D ❌ WRONG - Wrong structure: - “Lump sum” = Annuities provide REGULAR payments, not lump sums - “Intervals every 5 years” = Too infrequent (usually monthly/annually) - This is NOT how annuities work

DEFINITION TO MEMORIZE: > “Annuity = Contract providing REGULAR, PERIODIC payments for FIXED PERIOD or LIFETIME in exchange for premium/lump sum”

KEY ELEMENTS: 1. Regular = Not one-time 2. Periodic = At intervals (monthly, annually) 3. Fixed period OR Lifetime = Two duration options 4. Payment stream = Flow of income

MEMORY TIP:

ANNUITY = ANNUAL payments (historically yearly, now various periods)
Regular + Periodic + Lifetime option = Definition

📝 EXAM QUESTION 10 (Links to Section 6.1 - Annuity Funding)

Question: “Which are possible sources of capital sum to purchase annuity? i. EPF ii. Bank savings iii. Cash values of life policy

A. i only B. ii only C. i & iii only D. All the above ✅”

Answer: D (All of the above)

🔑 KEY INSIGHT: Annuities can be purchased with ANY LUMP SUM - very flexible funding!

ANALYSIS OF EACH SOURCE:

i. EPF (Employees Provident Fund) ✅ VALID: - Common source in Malaysia - Lump sum withdrawal at retirement (age 55+) - Can convert to lifetime income via annuity - Historical note: EPF even had own annuity scheme (discontinued)

Example:

Mr. Wong retires age 60:
EPF Account 1: RM400,000
Option 1: Take lump sum, manage yourself
Option 2: Buy immediate annuity → RM3,000/month for life ✅
Many retirees choose annuity to ensure income can't run out

ii. Bank Savings ✅ VALID: - Any cash savings can be used - Fixed deposits, savings accounts - Accumulated wealth over years - Convert lump sum to income stream

Example:

Inherits RM500,000
Instead of keeping in bank at 3% interest (RM15,000/year)
Buy annuity: RM3,500/month for life (RM42,000/year)
Higher income + longevity protection

iii. Cash Values of Life Policy ✅ VALID: - Surrender life insurance policy - Use accumulated cash value - Convert death protection to income protection - Suitable when death protection no longer needed

Example:

Mr. Lee age 65:
Whole life policy cash value: RM300,000
Children grown, death coverage less important
Surrender policy, buy immediate annuity
Convert to retirement income: RM2,200/month

OTHER POSSIBLE SOURCES (Not in question but valid): - Inheritance/gift - Sale of property/business - Investment liquidation - Retirement gratuity/pension lump sum - Lottery/windfall

KEY PRINCIPLE: Annuity = LUMP SUM IN → INCOME STREAM OUT Source of lump sum doesn’t matter - ANY capital works!

KEYWORDS: - “Sources of capital sum” = Where lump sum comes from - “All the above” = Multiple sources valid - “EPF” = Common Malaysian source

MEMORY TIP: “Any Lump Sum Works” - EPF, Savings, Insurance - ALL valid sources!

Formula & Calculations

Basic Annuity Payment Calculation (Conceptual):

Annual Payment = Purchase Price / Expected Years
(Simplified; actual uses actuarial tables and mortality rates)

Example:
Purchase Price: RM500,000
Life Expectancy: 25 years
Annual Payment: ~RM20,000/year = RM1,667/month
(Actual higher due to mortality credits and interest earnings)

Tax Benefit Calculation:

Tax Savings = Deferred Annuity Premium (max RM3,000) × Tax Rate

Example:
Premium paid: RM5,000
Maximum deductible: RM3,000
Tax rate: 14%
Tax savings: RM3,000 × 14% = RM420/year

Inflation Impact:

Future Value = Present Value / (1 + inflation)^years

Example:
Today's payment: RM3,000/month
Inflation: 3% per year
In 20 years:
Real value = RM3,000 / (1.03)^20 = RM3,000 / 1.806 = RM1,661
Lost 45% of purchasing power!
