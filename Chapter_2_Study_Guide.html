Chapter 2: Risk Management and Insurance Planning

Comprehensive Exam Preparation Guide

📚 Chapter Overview

This chapter builds on Chapter 1’s foundation and introduces the systematic process of managing risks in financial planning. Understanding the 6-step risk management process is CRITICAL for exam success, as it forms the framework for all practical risk management questions.

Exam Weight: Expect 15-20% of exam questions from this chapter - one of the highest weighted chapters.

Study Time Recommendation: 10-12 hours

🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you must be able to:

  1. Distinguish between speculative and pure risks in financial planning contexts
  2. Apply the 6-step risk management process systematically
  3. Evaluate and select appropriate risk management techniques for different scenarios
  4. Analyze case studies to recommend suitable risk management strategies
  5. Identify different types of pure risks (personal, property, liability)

📖 Complete Chapter Summary

Section 2.1: Introduction - Impact of Risk in Financial Planning

Two Broad Categories in Financial Planning

Category Focus Primary Risks Risk Type Examples in Malaysia
Wealth Accumulation Growing assets through investments Market volatility, currency fluctuations, economic conditions SPECULATIVE RISKS Unit trusts, equity investments in Bursa Malaysia, property speculation
Wealth Conservation Protecting accumulated wealth to sustain lifestyle Health issues, disability, premature death, inability to liquidate assets PURE RISKS Critical illness, disability, death affecting dependents

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Module 2 focuses on PURE RISK (insurance-related). Speculative risks are covered in Module 3.

📌 CRITICAL DISTINCTION: - Speculative Risk = Possibility of GAIN or LOSS (investment activities) - Pure Risk = Possibility of LOSS or NO LOSS (insurable events)

Speculative Risks in Malaysian Context

Definition: Risks involving the possibility of gaining or losing based on market fluctuations or economic conditions.

Key Characteristics: 1. Potential for profit 2. Voluntarily undertaken 3. NOT insurable through traditional insurance 4. Managed through investment strategies

Malaysian Examples:

Investment Type Speculative Risks Involved Management Approach
Bursa Malaysia stocks Market volatility, company performance, sector-specific risks Portfolio diversification, asset allocation
Property investment Property value fluctuations, rental yield changes, location risks Market research, diversification across areas
Currency trading Exchange rate fluctuations, geopolitical factors Hedging strategies, limit exposure
Unit trust funds Market downturns, fund manager performance Dollar-cost averaging, fund selection

Impact on Financial Planning Decisions: - Asset allocation strategies - Portfolio diversification requirements - Risk tolerance assessment - Cash holdings for liquidity

⚠️ EXAM TIP: If question mentions “investment”, “market volatility”, or “potential for gain” → Speculative Risk

Pure Risks in Malaysian Context

Definition: Risks representing the possibility of losses WITHOUT the opportunity for gain.

Key Characteristics: 1. No potential for profit 2. Fortuitous (happen by chance) 3. INSURABLE 4. Definite and measurable

Three Main Types of Pure Risks:

Type Definition Malaysian Examples Insurance Solutions
Personal Risks Risks affecting individual’s earning capacity and life Premature death, disability, critical illness, unemployment Life insurance, disability income, critical illness, medical insurance
Property Risks Risks of financial loss from damage/destruction of property House fire, flood damage, car accidents, burglary Fire insurance, home insurance, motor insurance
Liability Risks Risks of being held legally responsible for injury/damage to others Car accident injuring others, domestic worker injury, professional negligence Third-party motor insurance, employer’s liability, professional indemnity

📌 MEMORY AID - “PPL”: - Personal (affects YOU) - Property (affects YOUR STUFF) - Liability (affects OTHERS through YOUR actions)

Personal Risks - Detailed Breakdown

Four Major Categories (MUST MEMORIZE):

Risk Type Definition Financial Impact Example
Risk of Premature Death Death before expected lifespan Loss of income for dependents, funeral costs, debt obligations Breadwinner dies at 40, leaving family with mortgage
Risk of Retirement Outliving financial resources Insufficient retirement income, erosion of purchasing power Living to 90 but savings only planned for 75
Risk of Health Deterioration Illness or medical conditions requiring treatment Medical expenses, loss of income during treatment Critical illness like cancer requiring expensive treatment
Risk of Unemployment Loss of employment/income Inability to meet financial obligations Retrenchment during economic downturn

⚠️ EXAM QUESTION PATTERN: “Which pure risk situations fall under personal risks?” Answer: ALL FOUR categories above (Premature death, Retirement, Health deterioration, Unemployment)

Section 2.2: The Risk Management Concept

Definition of Risk Management

MEMORIZE THIS: Risk management is the systematic process of dealing with risks faced by an individual.

⚠️ EXAM TRAP: - ❌ NOT “eliminating all risks” (impossible) - ❌ NOT “using insurance as sole method” (only one technique) - ❌ NOT “reducing risk exposures to increase wealth” (too narrow) - ✅ “Systematic process of DEALING with risks”

Dynamics of Risk

Key Concept: Risks are NOT static; they evolve with changes in: - Individual circumstances (marriage, children, career changes) - Economic conditions (recession, inflation, market crashes) - Regulatory environment (new laws, tax changes) - Technology (cybersecurity risks, digital fraud) - Demographics (aging population, healthcare advances)

Malaysian Context Examples:

Dynamic Factor How It Changes Risk Impact on Risk Management
COVID-19 Pandemic Increased health risks, business disruptions, job losses Higher demand for medical insurance, business interruption coverage
EPF/SOCSO Regulation Changes Modified contribution rates, coverage expansions Adjust retirement planning, review social security adequacy
Digital Economy Growth New cybersecurity risks, digital fraud Need for cyber insurance, data protection measures
Aging Population Increased healthcare needs, longer retirement periods More critical illness coverage, longer-term retirement planning
Climate Change More frequent floods, landslides in Malaysia Enhanced property insurance, location-based risk assessment

⚠️ EXAM TIP: Questions about changing circumstances or evolving risks test understanding of risk dynamics.

2.3.a Step 1: Establishing Risk Management Objectives

Purpose: Define what the client wants to achieve through risk management.

Key Objectives (Common in Malaysia):

Objective Description Example
Financial Security for Dependents Ensure family can maintain lifestyle if breadwinner dies Life insurance coverage equal to 10x annual income
Debt Repayment Clear outstanding debts in case of death/disability MRTA (mortgage reducing term assurance) for housing loan
Education Funding Ensure children’s education continues Education insurance or trust fund
Retirement Income Maintain lifestyle in retirement Annuities, retirement plans beyond EPF
Healthcare Cost Coverage Protect against medical expenses Medical insurance, critical illness coverage
Asset Protection Preserve accumulated wealth Property insurance, liability coverage

Process: 1. Understand client’s financial goals 2. Identify dependents and obligations 3. Determine risk tolerance 4. Prioritize objectives based on importance and urgency

⚠️ EXAM TIP: Objectives must be SPECIFIC and MEASURABLE, not vague like “be safe” or “avoid risk.”

2.3.b Step 2: Gathering Information for Analysis

Purpose: Collect comprehensive data about the client’s situation.

Categories of Information Needed:

A. Personal Information

Category What to Collect Why It Matters
Demographics Age, health status, occupation, family size Affects risk exposure and insurance premiums
Lifestyle Hobbies, travel frequency, smoking/drinking habits Physical hazards affecting insurability
Financial Obligations Mortgage, car loans, credit card debt, children’s education Determines coverage needs
Current Coverage Existing insurance policies, EPF, SOCSO Identifies gaps in protection

B. Financial Information

Category Details Malaysian Context
Income Sources Salary, business income, rental income, dividends Stable vs. variable income affects risk capacity
Assets Property, investments, EPF balance, savings Determines wealth conservation needs
Liabilities Home loan, car loan, personal loans, business debts Must be covered in case of death/disability
Expenses Monthly living costs, insurance premiums, loan repayments Affects affordability of risk management solutions

C. Risk Exposure Information

Table 2-1: Classification of Resources (CRITICAL TABLE - Memorize this)

Resource Type Definition Examples Liquidity
Cash in Bank Immediate liquid funds Savings accounts, current accounts, fixed deposits HIGH - Immediately available
Cash Resources Resources received in cash form on death within reasonable time EPF death benefit, Contractual death gratuities, Life insurance proceeds MEDIUM-HIGH - Available within weeks/months
Cash Equivalent Resources Not in cash form but can be converted without much difficulty Unit trusts, Listed shares, Bonds MEDIUM - May take time to liquidate
Non-liquid Resources Difficult to convert to cash quickly or at fair value Property (house, land), Business assets, Unlisted shares LOW - May take months/years to sell

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: This table appears frequently. Know which category each asset falls under.

📌 Common Exam Question: “EPF death benefit is classified as?” → Cash Resources (not cash in bank)

2.3.c Step 3: Analyze Information to Identify and Evaluate Risks

Purpose: Systematically identify all risks and assess their priority for action.

Risk Identification Framework:

A. Personal Risks Analysis

Risk Frequency Severity Priority Typical Solution
Premature Death Low (but catastrophic if occurs) Very High CRITICAL Life insurance
Critical Illness Moderate (increasing with age) High HIGH Critical illness insurance
Disability Low to Moderate High HIGH Disability income insurance
Medical Expenses High (frequent small claims) Moderate to High HIGH Medical insurance
Unemployment Moderate (depends on economy/industry) Moderate MEDIUM Emergency fund, income protection

B. Property Risks Analysis

Risk Frequency Severity Priority Typical Solution
House Fire Very Low Very High HIGH Fire insurance (mandatory for mortgaged properties)
Flood Damage Moderate (location-dependent in Malaysia) High MEDIUM-HIGH Flood coverage add-on
Burglary Moderate (urban areas) Moderate MEDIUM Home contents insurance
Car Accident Moderate to High Moderate to High HIGH Motor insurance (mandatory)

C. Liability Risks Analysis

Risk Frequency Severity Priority Typical Solution
Motor Third-Party Liability Moderate Very High (unlimited potential) CRITICAL Third-party motor insurance (mandatory by law)
Employer’s Liability Low (if have domestic workers/employees) High HIGH Maid insurance, SOCSO coverage
Professional Liability Low (profession-specific) Very High HIGH Professional indemnity insurance

Risk Evaluation Criteria:

Frequency Analysis: - High Frequency, Low Severity → Risk Retention or Loss Control (e.g., minor car scratches - use own funds) - Low Frequency, High Severity → Risk Transfer/Insurance (e.g., death - buy life insurance) - High Frequency, High Severity → Risk Avoidance (e.g., dangerous activities - don’t do them) - Low Frequency, Low Severity → Risk Retention (e.g., losing wallet - accept and budget for replacement)

Matrix for Decision Making:

              HIGH SEVERITY                    LOW SEVERITY
HIGH      |  AVOID or TRANSFER  |         |  CONTROL or RETAIN  |
FREQUENCY |  (Very Risky)       |         |  (Nuisance Risks)   |
----------|---------------------|---------|---------------------|
LOW       |  TRANSFER           |         |  RETAIN             |
FREQUENCY |  (Insurance)        |         |  (Self-Insure)      |

⚠️ EXAM STRATEGY: When evaluating risks in case studies, always consider BOTH frequency AND severity.

Making Informed Decisions:

Key Principles: 1. Not every risk needs to be managed - Prioritize based on impact and probability 2. Resource limitations require prioritization - Focus on most significant risks first 3. Transparent communication with client - Explain which risks are addressed and which are not 4. Document unmitigated risks - Client must understand consequences of gaps

📌 EXAM TIP: If question asks “which risk should be prioritized?”, look for low frequency + high severity = Insurance priority.

2.3.d Step 4: Develop Risk Management Plan

Purpose: Select and apply cost-effective risk management techniques.

Four Main Risk Management Techniques (MUST MEMORIZE)

Technique Description When to Use Strategies Examples
1. Risk Avoidance Completely avoid the risk When risk is unacceptable and can be avoided Elimination, Substitution, Separation, Rational Planning Don’t engage in dangerous hobbies; Don’t invest in extremely volatile assets; Don’t drive if vision impaired
2. Risk Control (Loss Control) Reduce likelihood or severity of losses When risk cannot be avoided but can be managed Loss Prevention, Loss Reduction, Loss Sharing Install fire alarm (prevention); Use sprinkler system (reduction); Form business partnership (sharing)
3. Risk Retention Accept and retain the risk, cover losses yourself When risk impact is affordable or unavoidable Active Retention (deliberate), Passive Retention (unintentional) Maintain emergency fund; Self-insure small losses; Accept deductibles in insurance
4. Risk Transfer Transfer risk to another party When risk impact is too high to retain Non-insurance Transfer, Insurance Transfer Lease instead of buy equipment; Purchase life insurance; Buy property insurance

Detailed Breakdown of Each Technique:

1. RISK AVOIDANCE

Sub-Strategies:

Strategy Definition Example
Elimination Remove the source of risk entirely Sell investment property in flood-prone area
Substitution Replace risky activity with safer alternative Take train instead of motorcycle for daily commute
Separation Divide assets/activities to prevent concentrated loss Store cash in multiple banks (not all in one)
Rational Planning Careful planning to develop alternatives Create multiple income streams instead of single source

Malaysian Examples: - Avoid buying property in flood-prone areas (Kelantan, Pahang low-lying areas) - Choose stable job over volatile commission-based work (if risk-averse) - Don’t keep all savings in one bank (separation) - Avoid high-risk investments if approaching retirement

⚠️ EXAM TIP: Avoidance is NOT always the best solution - sometimes it means missing opportunities.

2. RISK CONTROL (LOSS CONTROL)

Three Sub-Techniques:

Sub-Technique Focus Mechanism Example
Loss Prevention REDUCE FREQUENCY (prevent loss from happening) Reduce likelihood of risk occurrence Install alarm system to prevent burglary; Annual medical checkup to detect illness early; Fire drills in office
Loss Reduction REDUCE SEVERITY (minimize damage when loss occurs) Minimize impact when loss happens Install sprinkler system to limit fire damage; Wear seatbelt to reduce injury severity; Keep fire extinguisher in kitchen
Loss Sharing SPREAD RISK (distribute impact among parties) Share risk with others Business partnership (share business risks); Join takaful (Islamic risk-sharing); Form neighborhood watch

Malaysian Context Examples:

Risk Prevention (Reduce Frequency) Reduction (Reduce Severity) Malaysian-Specific Measure
House Fire No smoking indoors, regular electrical checks Sprinkler system, fire extinguisher Follow BOMBA (Fire Department) safety guidelines
Flood Damage Build house on elevated ground Keep valuables on upper floors, sandbags Check JPS (Department of Irrigation and Drainage) flood maps before buying property
Car Accident Defensive driving course, avoid speeding Airbags, crumple zones, ABS brakes Install dashcam (common in Malaysia for evidence)
Health Issues Regular exercise, balanced diet, no smoking Early health screening, maintain healthy weight Participate in employer wellness programs, use SOCSO health screening

📌 MEMORY TRICK: - Prevention = Prevent = BEFORE it happens - Reduction = Reduce damage = AFTER it starts

3. RISK RETENTION

Two Types:

Type Description When It Happens Example
Active (Intentional) Retention Deliberately accept risk, often with planning Conscious decision with preparation Keep RM10,000 emergency fund for small medical expenses instead of insuring; Accept insurance deductible
Passive (Unintentional) Retention Unknowingly retain risk due to lack of awareness Accidental, no preparation Unaware of flood risk in new property; Forgot to renew insurance policy

When Risk Retention is Appropriate: 1. Low-severity risks - Impact is affordable (e.g., small scratches on car) 2. High-frequency, low-severity - Insurance would be too expensive (e.g., minor medical consultations) 3. Deliberate cost management - Build emergency fund instead of comprehensive insurance 4. Unavoidable residual risks - After all other techniques applied

Malaysian Examples: - Keep emergency fund of 3-6 months expenses (active retention) - Accept RM500 deductible on car insurance to lower premiums - Self-fund minor medical consultations (under RM200) - Budget for smartphone replacement every 2-3 years

⚠️ EXAM TRAP: Retention ≠ Doing Nothing. Active retention requires planning and reserves.

📌 CRITICAL POINT: Risk retention does NOT change frequency or severity of loss - it only determines HOW you’ll pay for it when it occurs.

4. RISK TRANSFER / RISK SHIFTING

Two Main Categories:

Type Mechanism Examples Advantages Disadvantages
Non-Insurance Transfer Contractually shift risk to another party Lease instead of buy; Outsource risky operations; Hold harmless agreements Lower cost than insurance; Flexible terms Other party must be reliable; May not fully transfer risk
Insurance Transfer Transfer pure risk to insurance company through premium payment Life insurance; Property insurance; Medical insurance; Motor insurance Professional risk handling; Large loss capacity; Certainty of coverage Premium costs; Policy exclusions; Claim procedures

Non-Insurance Transfer Examples (Malaysia):

Situation How Risk is Transferred Malaysian Context
Lease Equipment Lessor retains ownership and associated risks Lease company car instead of buying (maintenance, depreciation transferred)
Outsource Functions Service provider assumes operational risks Outsource payroll to ADP Malaysia (liability for errors transferred)
Hold Harmless Clause Contractual shift of liability Vendor contract states they’re liable for defective products
Tenancy Agreement Tenant responsible for damage during rental period Landlord transfers property damage risk to tenant

Insurance Transfer Examples (Malaysia):

Risk Type Insurance Product Coverage Typical Provider
Death Life Insurance / Term Life Lump sum on death Prudential, Great Eastern, AIA, Allianz
Critical Illness Critical Illness Rider/Standalone Lump sum on diagnosis of specified illnesses Same as above
Medical Expenses Medical Card / Health Insurance Hospitalization, surgery, treatment costs Same as above + Takaful operators
Property Damage Fire Insurance / Home Insurance Fire, flood (if covered), burglary MNRB, Zurich, Allianz General
Motor Accident Motor Insurance (Comprehensive or Third-Party) Own damage + Third-party liability Same as above
Mortgage MRTA (Mortgage Reducing Term Assurance) Outstanding loan on death/TPD Usually from bank or appointed insurer

⚠️ EXAM DISTINCTION: - Insurance transfer = Transfer to INSURER (pay premium, get coverage) - Non-insurance transfer = Transfer through CONTRACT (no premium to insurer)

Comprehensive Technique Selection Framework:

Technique Selection Matrix:

Risk Characteristic Best Technique Rationale
High Frequency, Low Severity Risk Retention or Loss Control Insurance too expensive; affordable to self-pay
Low Frequency, High Severity Risk Transfer (Insurance) Cannot afford impact; insurance cost-effective
High Frequency, High Severity Risk Avoidance Too dangerous; if unavoidable, combine Control + Transfer
Low Frequency, Low Severity Risk Retention Not worth insurance cost; budget for it
Unacceptable Risk Risk Avoidance Eliminate entirely
Manageable Impact Loss Control + Partial Retention Reduce severity, retain small portion

Table: Comprehensive Risk Management Techniques Summary

Technique Avoidance Control Retention Transfer (Non-Insurance) Transfer (Insurance)
Changes Frequency? Yes (to zero) Yes (reduces) No No No
Changes Severity? Yes (to zero) Yes (reduces) No No No
Who Bears Loss? N/A (no risk) Client (reduced) Client Other party Insurer
Cost Opportunity cost Implementation cost Loss occurrence cost Contract/fees Premium
Best For Unacceptable risks Controllable risks Affordable risks Contractual situations Pure, high-severity risks

⚠️ EXAM STRATEGY: Case study questions often ask “Which technique should be used?” - Apply the matrix above.

2.3.e Step 5: Implement Risk Management Plan

Purpose: Execute the risk management plan strategically and effectively.

Implementation Process:

A. Prioritization of Actions

Priority Level Criteria Action Examples
CRITICAL (Immediate) High severity + Legal requirement Third-party motor insurance; MRTA for home loan; Fire insurance for mortgaged property
HIGH (Within 3 months) High severity + Affects dependents Life insurance for breadwinner; Medical insurance for family
MEDIUM (Within 6-12 months) Moderate severity or frequency Critical illness coverage; Property contents insurance; Income protection
LOW (Ongoing/As budget allows) Low severity + Optional Investment-linked policies; Comprehensive motor insurance (if old car); Travel insurance

B. Resource Allocation

Budget Guidelines for Malaysian Context:

Category Recommended % of Monthly Income Priority Notes
Pure Insurance (Protection) 10-15% HIGH Life, medical, critical illness
Savings/Emergency Fund 10-20% HIGH 3-6 months expenses
EPF/Retirement 11% (employee) + 12-13% (employer) MANDATORY Statutory requirement
Debt Repayment Max 30-40% HIGH Housing, car, personal loans
Investments (Wealth Accumulation) 10-20% MEDIUM After protection covered

C. Matching Techniques to Risks

Example Implementation Plan:

Risk Identified Technique Selected Specific Action Timeline Budget Required
Premature Death Transfer (Insurance) Purchase RM1 million term life Immediate RM150/month
Medical Expenses Transfer (Insurance) Medical card RM100k annual limit Immediate RM250/month
House Fire Transfer (Insurance) Fire insurance Immediate RM50/month
Minor Car Damage Retention Maintain RM5,000 emergency fund Month 1-6 Save RM800/month
Car Accident (Major) Transfer (Insurance) Comprehensive motor insurance Immediate RM200/month
Burglary Control + Partial Retention Install alarm system + basic contents coverage Month 3 RM2,000 (one-time) + RM30/month

D. Execution Checklist

✅ Insurance Purchases: - [ ] Compare at least 3 quotations - [ ] Verify coverage terms and exclusions - [ ] Complete medical underwriting (if required) - [ ] Review policy documents thoroughly - [ ] Set up automatic premium payment - [ ] Store policy documents securely

✅ Loss Control Measures: - [ ] Install safety equipment (alarms, fire extinguishers) - [ ] Implement safety procedures - [ ] Train family members on emergency protocols - [ ] Schedule regular maintenance

✅ Financial Reserves: - [ ] Open separate emergency fund account - [ ] Set up automatic transfers - [ ] Establish target amount (3-6 months expenses) - [ ] Review monthly progress

⚠️ EXAM TIP: Implementation questions test whether you understand that execution must be systematic and prioritized, not random.

2.3.f Step 6: Review, Monitor, and Revise Risk Management Plan

Purpose: Ensure plan remains effective and relevant through continuous evaluation.

Review Frequency:

Event Type Review Trigger Action Required
Annual Review Every 12 months Comprehensive plan evaluation
Major Life Change Marriage, birth of child, divorce Immediate review and adjustment
Financial Change Promotion, job loss, inheritance Reassess coverage needs and budget
Asset Acquisition Buy house, car, start business Add appropriate insurance coverage
Regulatory Change New laws, tax changes Ensure compliance and optimization
Economic Shift Recession, inflation spike Review affordability and priorities

Monitoring Checklist:

A. Policy Monitoring

Item Frequency Check Points
Premium Payments Monthly Verify auto-debit successful; maintain sufficient funds
Coverage Adequacy Annually Does coverage still match needs? Inflation adjustment?
Policy Renewals Before expiry Review terms, compare alternatives, renew timely
Claim Procedures When needed Know how to file claims efficiently

B. Life Situation Monitoring

Change Impact on Risk Management Required Action
Marriage Added dependent; dual income potential Increase life coverage; add spouse to medical insurance
Birth of Child New dependent; education costs Increase life coverage; start education fund; add child to medical insurance
Buy Property New asset; new liability (mortgage) Add fire/home insurance; consider MRTA
Job Change Income change; loss of group insurance Review individual insurance needs; adjust coverage
Approaching Retirement Reduced income; higher medical needs Shift focus to medical coverage; review retirement adequacy
Debt Clearance Reduced obligations Can reduce coverage or reallocate premium budget

C. Performance Evaluation

Key Questions to Ask:

  1. Coverage Adequacy: Is current insurance sufficient for today’s needs?
    • Life insurance: Still covers mortgage + family expenses for X years?
    • Medical insurance: Adequate for rising healthcare costs?
    • Property insurance: Reflects current property value?
  2. Cost Effectiveness: Are premiums still affordable and competitive?
    • Compare with market rates
    • Look for better products
    • Review rider benefits vs. cost
  3. Risk Exposure Changes: Have new risks emerged?
    • New business venture?
    • New property purchase?
    • Health deterioration?
  4. Claim Experience: How effective was the coverage during claims?
    • Were claims paid smoothly?
    • Were exclusions encountered?
    • Is coverage comprehensive enough?

Revision Triggers:

When to Revise Plan:

Trigger Why Revision Needed Example Revision
Income Increase Can afford better coverage Upgrade medical insurance to higher annual limit; add critical illness rider
Income Decrease Must reduce premiums Reduce coverage amounts; increase deductibles; eliminate low-priority riders
New Dependent Greater financial responsibility Increase life insurance coverage amount
Debt Increase Higher liabilities to cover Add term life to cover new debt
Asset Accumulation More to protect Add property insurance; increase liability coverage
Health Change Different risk profile Add critical illness if diagnosed with pre-condition (if allowed); increase medical coverage
Career Change Different occupation risks Adjust coverage for new occupation hazards; review income protection

Malaysian Context Revisions:

Malaysian-Specific Event Revision Required
EPF i-Sinar/i-Lestari Withdrawal Review retirement adequacy; may need more personal retirement savings
EPF Rate Change Adjust personal retirement contributions to compensate
BNM Interest Rate Change Review insurance-linked investment performance
SOCSO Coverage Expansion Reassess need for separate disability coverage
MySalam Introduction Government-provided critical illness - may reduce need for private coverage (for eligible B40)

⚠️ EXAM TIP: Review questions test understanding that risk management is CONTINUOUS, not one-time.

📌 CRITICAL POINT: Failure to review and revise can result in: - Under-insurance (inadequate coverage) - Over-insurance (wasting money) - Coverage gaps (new risks not addressed) - Outdated beneficiaries (e.g., ex-spouse still named)

🎯 Case Studies Analysis

Case Study 1: Property Investment Risk Management - Mr. Hafiz

Background: - Property investor in Kuala Lumpur - Owns several rental properties - Concerned about: property damage, tenant defaults, fluctuating market values

Risk Identification:

Risk Type Classification Frequency Severity
Property damage (flood, fire) Property Risk - Pure Low High
Tenant defaults Financial Risk - Pure Moderate Moderate
Market value fluctuation Investment Risk - Speculative High Moderate

Risk Management Strategies Applied:

Risk Technique Specific Action Reasoning
Property Damage Transfer (Insurance) Comprehensive property insurance covering flood, fire, landslides Low frequency, high severity - classic insurance case; Floods common in some KL areas
Tenant Defaults Control + Retention Background checks + emergency fund Reduce frequency (prevention); Retain moderate impact through reserves
Market Fluctuation Retention + Control Emergency fund for temporary income loss; Diversify property locations Cannot insure speculative risk; Control through diversification

Malaysian Context Insights: - Flood insurance critical in certain KL areas (e.g., Taman Sri Muda, parts of Ampang) - Landslide coverage important in hilly areas (e.g., Bukit Antarabangsa) - Background checks via CTOS/CCRIS to screen tenants - Legal protection through proper tenancy agreement (non-insurance transfer)

⚠️ EXAM INSIGHT: Notice how Mr. Hafiz uses COMBINATION of techniques, not just one.

Case Study 2: Life Insurance Planning - Farah and Azlan (Young Family)

Background: - Young couple in Johor Bahru - First child - Want to ensure family’s financial security

Risk Identification:

Risk Impact if Occurs Priority
Premature death of breadwinner(s) Loss of income, family can’t pay mortgage/expenses CRITICAL
Critical illness Medical costs + loss of income during treatment HIGH
Normal medical expenses Ongoing treatment costs HIGH

Risk Management Strategy Applied:

Risk Technique Product Selected Coverage Details
Premature Death Transfer (Insurance) Investment-Linked Life Insurance Cover mortgage + living expenses for X years
Critical Illness Transfer (Insurance) Critical Illness Rider Lump sum on diagnosis of specified illnesses
Medical Expenses Transfer (Insurance) Medical Insurance (implied) Hospitalization and surgery costs

Analysis of Product Choice:

Feature Investment-Linked Policy (ILP) Pure Term Life Which is Better?
Death Coverage Yes Yes Equal
Investment Component Yes No ILP offers growth potential
Premium Higher Lower Term cheaper for same coverage
Flexibility Can adjust coverage Fixed ILP more flexible
Suitability Young family, long-term Pure protection only ILP acceptable if budget allows; Term better if budget tight

Malaysian Considerations: - MRTA vs. MLTA for mortgage coverage - MRTA (Mortgage Reducing Term Assurance) = Coverage decreases with loan balance - MLTA (Mortgage Level Term Assurance) = Fixed coverage throughout - Critical illness rider cost-effective vs. standalone - Rising medical costs in private hospitals (important to get adequate medical insurance)

⚠️ EXAM TIP: ILP mentioned because it provides both protection AND investment - suitable for young family with long horizon. But question could also accept recommendation for pure term life (more cost-effective).

Case Study 3: Medical Insurance - Mr. and Mrs. Lim (Retirees)

Background: - Retirees in suburban Kuala Lumpur - Concerned about rising healthcare costs - Fixed retirement income - Want to protect savings from medical expenses

Risk Analysis:

Factor Impact on Risk Management
Age Higher premium costs; higher medical risk

📝 Self-Assessment Questions (30 Questions)

Section A: Fundamental Concepts (Questions 1-8)

1. Simon Lim bought a house for RM600,000. Which best describes the pure risk situation? - A. Risk that house may be burnt down in a fire accident ✅ - B. Risk that house value may drop in a downturn - C. Risk of losing money had he invested elsewhere - D. None of the above

Explanation: - A = Pure risk (only loss or no loss, no gain) - B = Speculative risk (property value can go up or down) - C = Opportunity cost/speculative risk Exam Tip: Pure risk = NO possibility of profit, only loss or no loss.

2. Which best defines risk management? - A. Methods used in eliminating all risks faced by an individual - B. Systematic process of dealing with risks faced by an individual ✅ - C. Use of insurance as the sole method to manage risks - D. Process of increasing wealth by reducing risk exposures

Explanation: - A = Wrong - cannot eliminate ALL risks - B = Correct definition - SYSTEMATIC PROCESS of DEALING (not eliminating) - C = Wrong - insurance is only ONE technique - D = Wrong - too narrow, focus is on managing risks not just wealth increase Exam Tip: Word-for-word definition question - memorize “systematic process of dealing with risks”

3. As a financial practitioner, which best describes “risk”? - A. Uncertainty about financial losses from an exposure ✅ - B. Certainty about financial losses from an exposure - C. Exposure to investment risks - D. Exposure to systemic risks

Explanation: - A = Correct - exact definition from Chapter 1 & 2 - B = Wrong - UNCERTAINTY not certainty - C = Wrong - too narrow (only investment) - D = Wrong - too narrow (only systemic) Exam Tip: This is the MODULE 2 definition of risk - know it word-for-word

4. Fill in the blanks: In risk management, _____ is the unpleasant outcome of _____. - A. Loss; risk ✅ - B. Risk; Loss - C. Exposure; loss - D. Loss; business ventures

Explanation: - LOSS is the unpleasant OUTCOME (result) - RISK is what might cause it (the possibility) - Therefore: Loss is outcome OF risk Exam Tip: Think cause and effect - Risk causes Loss

5. There are two forms of loss. _____ is a loss that may happen; _____ is a loss that has precipitated. - A. Occurred loss; loss exposure - B. Loss exposure; occurred loss ✅ - C. Loss event; loss risk - D. Potential loss; actual loss (also acceptable, but B is the terminology used in the text)

Explanation: - Loss EXPOSURE = potential/may happen (future) - Occurred/Precipitated loss = already happened (past) Exam Tip: “Exposure” = future possibility; “Occurred/Precipitated” = already happened

6. Harban Singh (construction worker, high-risk area, large accident policy, single, no dependents, pressed by credit card debt). Insurer turns down proposal. What are likely grounds?

  1. High physical hazard: works in high-risk area
  2. Likely moral hazard: in debts
  3. Possible morale hazard: no dependents
  • A. i only
  • B. i & ii
  • C. None of the above
  • D. All the above ✅

Explanation: - i = TRUE: High-risk job = physical hazard - ii = TRUE: Debt + large policy = potential fraud (moral hazard) - iii = TRUE: No dependents + large policy = may not care about safety (morale hazard) All three concerns valid - insurer right to decline Exam Tip: Multiple hazards can exist simultaneously in one scenario

7. _____ is a risk that affects the entire economy; _____ is a risk that affects only individuals. - A. Fundamental risk; particular risk ✅ - B. Particular risk; fundamental risk - C. Speculative risk; pure risk - D. Static risk; dynamic risk

Explanation: - Fundamental = affects MANY (economy/community) - Particular = affects FEW (individuals) Exam Tip: “Fundamental” sounds big = affects everyone; “Particular” sounds specific = affects specific person

8. Which are the three main types of pure risk faced by individuals? - A. Group risks, personal risks, and liability risks - B. Insurable risks, property risks, and liability risks - C. Personal risks, property risks, and liability risks ✅ - D. Death risks, disability risks, and illness risks

Explanation: The “PPL” framework: - Personal risks - Property risks - Liability risks Exam Tip: Memorize “PPL” - these are the three categories of pure risks

Section B: Personal Risks (Questions 9-12)

9. Which pure risk situations fall under personal risks?

  1. Risk of Premature death
  2. Risk of Retirement
  3. Risk of Health Deterioration
  4. Risk of Unemployment
  • A. i & ii only
  • B. ii & iii only
  • C. i, ii & iii only
  • D. All of the above ✅

Explanation: ALL FOUR are personal risks: - Premature death = affects earning capacity - Retirement = outliving resources - Health deterioration = medical costs + lost income - Unemployment = loss of income Exam Tip: When in doubt with personal risks, usually “all of the above” if all relate to the person’s earning/living capacity

10. (Fire scenario - Mr. Kong’s factory) Property loss can be direct or indirect. Which loss is classified as INDIRECT? - A. Loss of the factory - B. Loss of the special printing machines - C. Loss of profits ✅ - D. Loss of printing materials

Explanation: - Direct loss = Physical damage (factory, machines, materials) - Indirect loss = Consequential/resulting loss (profits, rental income, business interruption) Exam Tip: Direct = You can TOUCH it (physical); Indirect = You CANNOT touch it (consequential)

11. Which of the following is a speculative risk? - A. Risk of house fire - B. Risk of car accident - C. Risk of starting a new business ✅ - D. Risk of critical illness

Explanation: - A, B, D = Pure risks (only loss or no loss) - C = Speculative (business can profit OR lose) Exam Tip: If there’s potential for GAIN/PROFIT = Speculative

12. A young family with a new baby is considering insurance. Which risk should be the HIGHEST priority? - A. Travel insurance - B. Investment-linked policy - C. Life insurance for breadwinner ✅ - D. Car comprehensive insurance

Explanation: Priority for family with dependent: 1. Life insurance (protect dependent if breadwinner dies) = CRITICAL 2. Medical insurance (healthcare for family) 3. Critical illness (protect income) 4. Others (travel, car comprehensive) = lower priority Exam Tip: Dependent child = Life insurance becomes CRITICAL priority

Section C: Risk Management Techniques (Questions 13-20)

13. Which risk management technique involves completely avoiding the risk? - A. Risk control - B. Risk avoidance ✅ - C. Risk retention - D. Risk transfer

Explanation: Four techniques: - Avoidance = Eliminate completely - Control = Reduce frequency/severity - Retention = Accept and self-pay - Transfer = Shift to others Exam Tip: “Avoid” = “Eliminate” = don’t do the risky activity

14. Installing a fire alarm system is an example of: - A. Risk avoidance - B. Loss prevention ✅ - C. Loss reduction - D. Risk transfer

Explanation: - Fire alarm = Detects early = PREVENTS fire from growing - Fire alarm is part of RISK CONTROL technique - Specifically: LOSS PREVENTION (reduces FREQUENCY) Exam Tip: Prevention = BEFORE/STOP it happening; Reduction = MINIMIZE damage AFTER it starts

15. Installing sprinkler systems in a building is an example of: - A. Risk avoidance - B. Loss prevention - C. Loss reduction ✅ - D. Risk retention

Explanation: - Sprinklers activate AFTER fire starts = don’t prevent, but REDUCE damage - Part of RISK CONTROL - Specifically: LOSS REDUCTION (reduces SEVERITY) Exam Tip: Sprinkler/fire extinguisher = REDUCTION (fire already started, minimize damage)

16. Maintaining an emergency fund to cover unexpected medical expenses is: - A. Risk avoidance - B. Risk control - C. Risk retention ✅ - D. Risk transfer

Explanation: - Emergency fund = Prepare to SELF-PAY when loss occurs - This is RISK RETENTION (accept risk, plan to cover it yourself) - Specifically: ACTIVE retention (deliberate with preparation) Exam Tip: Emergency fund = Retention (you’re retaining/accepting the risk)

17. Purchasing comprehensive motor insurance is an example of: - A. Risk avoidance - B. Risk control - C. Risk retention - D. Risk transfer ✅

Explanation: - Buying insurance = Transfer risk to insurer - Pay premium, insurer pays claims - This is INSURANCE-BASED TRANSFER Exam Tip: Buying insurance = always TRANSFER technique

18. Which technique is most appropriate for LOW frequency, HIGH severity risks? - A. Risk avoidance - B. Risk control - C. Risk retention - D. Risk transfer (Insurance) ✅

Explanation: Frequency-Severity Matrix: - Low frequency + High severity = INSURANCE (transfer) - Rarely happens, but devastating when it does - Examples: Death, major accident, house fire Exam Tip: Low frequency + High severity = classic insurance situation

19. Which technique is most appropriate for HIGH frequency, LOW severity risks? - A. Risk avoidance - B. Risk control - C. Risk retention ✅ - D. Risk transfer

Explanation: - High frequency + Low severity = Self-pay (retention) - Happens often but small impact - Insurance would be too expensive - Examples: Minor car scratches, small medical consultations Exam Tip: High frequency + Low severity = too expensive to insure, better to self-pay

20. Leasing equipment instead of buying it is an example of: - A. Risk avoidance - B. Risk control - C. Non-insurance transfer ✅ - D. Insurance transfer

Explanation: - Leasing = Lessor retains ownership and risks - Risk transferred through CONTRACT, not insurance - This is NON-INSURANCE TRANSFER Exam Tip: Contract-based shift = Non-insurance transfer; Premium-based = Insurance transfer

Section D: Risk Management Process (Questions 21-26)

21. What is the FIRST step in the risk management process? - A. Gather information - B. Establish risk management objectives ✅ - C. Analyze risks - D. Develop a plan

Explanation: 6-Step Process: 1. Establish objectives (define what you want to achieve) 2. Gather information 3. Analyze and evaluate 4. Develop plan 5. Implement 6. Review and revise Exam Tip: Must know goals BEFORE gathering data

22. Which step involves identifying whether risks are high or low priority? - A. Step 2: Gather information - B. Step 3: Analyze and evaluate risks ✅ - C. Step 4: Develop plan - D. Step 5: Implement plan

Explanation: - Step 3 = ANALYZE information to IDENTIFY and EVALUATE (prioritize) risks - Evaluation includes assessing frequency, severity, and priority Exam Tip: “Evaluate” and “prioritize” happen in Step 3 (Analysis)

23. EPF death benefit is classified as: - A. Cash in bank - B. Cash resources ✅ - C. Cash equivalent resources - D. Non-liquid resources

Explanation: Table 2-1 Classifications: - Cash in bank = Immediately accessible - Cash resources = Received in cash on death within reasonable time (EPF, insurance, gratuities) - Cash equivalent = Can convert to cash (unit trusts, shares) - Non-liquid = Hard to convert (property) Exam Tip: EPF death benefit = Cash Resources (NOT cash in bank - needs processing time)

24. Listed shares on Bursa Malaysia are classified as: - A. Cash in bank - B. Cash resources - C. Cash equivalent resources ✅ - D. Non-liquid resources

Explanation: - Listed shares = Can sell on exchange = Convert to cash easily - Not immediate like bank account - Not cash received on death - Therefore: CASH EQUIVALENT Exam Tip: Shares, unit trusts, bonds = Cash Equivalent (can liquidate fairly easily)

25. Which is NOT a step in the risk management process? - A. Gather information for analysis - B. Develop a risk management plan - C. Eliminate all identified risks ✅ - D. Review, monitor, and revise the plan

Explanation: - Cannot eliminate ALL risks (unrealistic) - Risk management = MANAGE risks (not eliminate all) - Options A, B, D are actual steps in the 6-step process Exam Tip: “Eliminate all risks” = red flag wrong answer

26. How often should a risk management plan be reviewed? - A. Only when a claim is made - B. Once every 5 years - C. Annually and when life circumstances change ✅ - D. Never - set it and forget it

Explanation: Review triggers: - Annual review (comprehensive) - Major life changes (marriage, child, job change) - Financial changes (promotion, inheritance) - Asset acquisition (house, car) Exam Tip: Risk management is CONTINUOUS, not one-time

Section E: Case Study Application (Questions 27-30)

27. A retiree couple on fixed income is choosing medical insurance. They should prioritize: - A. Lowest premium regardless of deductible - B. Highest deductible to save on premium - C. Lower deductible even if premium is higher ✅ - D. No insurance - use government hospitals

Explanation: - Fixed income = predictable expenses important - Lower deductible = lower out-of-pocket when claim - Higher premium = predictable monthly cost - Better than high deductible with unpredictable large out-of-pocket Exam Tip: Fixed income + retiree = prefer predictable costs = lower deductible

28. A young couple with first child and mortgage should prioritize: - A. Investment-linked policy for returns - B. Term life insurance covering mortgage and family expenses ✅ - C. Saving for child’s university - D. Comprehensive motor insurance

Explanation: Priority ranking: 1. Life insurance (protect dependent and mortgage) = CRITICAL 2. Medical insurance (family healthcare) 3. Critical illness (income protection) 4. Other priorities Exam Tip: New dependent + mortgage = Life insurance becomes #1 priority

29. A property investor concerned about tenant defaults should use: - A. Risk avoidance (don’t rent out) - B. Risk transfer (insurance for tenant defaults) - C. Risk control (background checks) + Retention (emergency fund) ✅ - D. Only risk retention

Explanation: - Tenant default = moderate frequency, moderate severity - CONTROL: Background checks reduce likelihood - RETENTION: Emergency fund to cover temporary loss - Combination approach most appropriate Exam Tip: Moderate risks often need COMBINATION of techniques

30. Which statement about risk management is TRUE? - A. All identified risks must be insured - B. Risk management is a one-time process - C. Risk retention means doing nothing about the risk - D. Risk management techniques can be used in combination ✅

Explanation: - A = False (not all risks need insurance) - B = False (continuous process - Step 6 is ongoing review) - C = False (active retention involves planning and reserves) - D = TRUE (often use multiple techniques together) Exam Tip: Real-world risk management = combination of techniques, not single approach
