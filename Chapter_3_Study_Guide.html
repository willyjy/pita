Chapter 3: Insurance Needs Analysis

Comprehensive Exam Preparation Guide

Exam Weight: 20-25% (HIGHEST weighted chapter - critical for exam success)
Study Time: 12-15 hours

🎯 Critical Learning Objectives

  1. Master TWO life insurance valuation methods (HLV & Capital Needs)
  2. Calculate coverage for death, disability, critical illness, retirement, education
  3. Distinguish between Capital Liquidation vs. Capital Conservation
  4. Apply Total Needs vs. Single Needs approach
  5. Analyze business-related insurance needs

📖 Core Concepts Summary

3.1 Role of Insurance in Financial Planning

FOUR Key Functions: 1. Risk Protection - Guard against immediate financial risks 2. Goal Achievement - Secure long-term financial aspirations 3. Legacy Planning - Ensure intergenerational wealth transfer (critical in Malaysia) 4. Estate Liquidity - Provide funds for settlement of obligations

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Malaysia has NO estate taxes, but insurance still crucial for: - Settlement of debts - Legal fees - Providing liquidity (avoid forced asset sale) - Equal distribution among heirs

3.2 Personal Risk Exposures (7 Types)

Risk Type Definition Insurance Solution Exam Frequency
Mortality Risk Premature death Life insurance ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Health Risk Illness/medical expenses Medical insurance, critical illness ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐
Disability Risk Unable to work Disability income insurance ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Longevity Risk Outlive resources Annuities ⭐⭐⭐
Dependency Risk Dependents’ needs Life insurance with adequate coverage ⭐⭐⭐⭐
Education Fund Risk Can’t fund children’s education Education plans, life insurance riders ⭐⭐⭐
Legacy Risk Estate distribution issues Life insurance for estate planning ⭐⭐⭐

📌 Memory Aid - “MHDDLE”: Mortality, Health, Disability, Dependency, Longevity, Education, Legacy

💰 3.3 Life Insurance Valuation Methods

Method 1: Human Life Value (HLV) Approach

Concept: Present value of future income that would be lost if person dies.

Formula:

HLV = A × (1 + r) × [(1 - (1 + r)^-n) / r]

Where:
A = Annual income for dependents
r = Interest rate (discount rate)
n = Years to retirement

CRITICAL: Set calculator to BEGIN mode (income received at start of year)

Example 3-1: Mr. Aziz - Age: 30, Retirement: 55 (n = 25 years) - Annual income: RM60,000 - Income for dependents: RM40,000 (2/3 of income) - Interest rate: 6%

Calculator Steps: 1. Set to BEGIN mode 2. n = 25 3. i = 6 4. PMT = 40,000 5. Compute PV = RM542,014.30

Interpretation: RM542,014.30 is the total present value of income lost if Mr. Aziz dies prematurely.

Limitations of HLV (MUST KNOW for exam):

Limitation Reality
❌ Assumes salary unchanged ✅ Salary typically increases
❌ Assumes constant interest rate ✅ Rates fluctuate
❌ Ignores inflation ✅ Inflation erodes purchasing power
❌ Ignores lifestyle changes ✅ Needs change (marriage, children)
❌ Assumes zero value at retirement ✅ May have post-retirement income
❌ HLV decreases as age increases ✅ Actually may increase with salary growth

⚠️ EXAM TRAP: Questions may present scenario where HLV seems to decrease with age, but ask you to identify this as a limitation.

📌 EXAM TIP: Despite limitations, HLV is “a good method to get started - at least there’s a numerical value to focus on with client.”

Method 2: Capital Needs Approach

Concept: Calculate total capital needed to meet ALL financial needs of dependents.

TWO Sub-Methods:

A. Capital Liquidation Approach

Concept: Both principal AND interest consumed over time. Capital eventually depleted.

Formula:

Capital Needed = PV of income needs (using PVIFA)

Where income is consumed over fixed period

Example: RM60,000 annual income, 7 years, 6% interest

Calculator Steps: 1. Set to BEGIN mode 2. n = 7 3. i = 6 4. PMT = 60,000 5. Compute PV = RM374,457

Interpretation: RM374,457 needed if capital will be fully used up over 7 years.

B. Capital Conservation Approach

Concept: Only interest consumed. Principal preserved intact for heirs.

Formula:

Capital Needed = Annual Income Needed / Interest Rate

Capital Needed = A / r

Example: RM60,000 annual income, 6% interest

Capital Needed = RM60,000 / 0.06 = RM1,000,000

Interpretation: RM1,000,000 invested at 6% generates RM60,000 annually FOREVER, principal intact.

Comparison Table (CRITICAL for Exam):

Aspect Capital Liquidation Capital Conservation
Principal Consumed with interest Preserved (not touched)
Duration Fixed period only Indefinite (perpetuity)
Amount Needed Lower Higher (always more)
Purpose Income for dependents only Income + Legacy for heirs
Formula PVIFA calculation A / r
Example (RM60k, 6%) RM374,457 (7 years) RM1,000,000 (forever)

⚠️ EXAM ALERT: Capital Conservation ALWAYS requires MORE capital than Liquidation.

📌 EXAM PATTERN: Questions often ask “which requires more capital?” Answer: Conservation

3.3 Complete Capital Needs Calculation

Total Life Insurance Need Formula:

Total Need = [Cash Needs + Income Replacement + Legacy Needs] - Available Resources

Where:
Cash Needs = Debts + Funeral + Final expenses
Income Replacement = Liquidation OR Conservation amount
Legacy Needs = Charitable giving, inheritance
Available Resources = Cash, EPF, existing insurance, investments

Example 3-4: Raymond Wong

Given: - Cash/equivalent resources: RM300,000 - Annual income needs: RM60,000 for 5 years - Interest rate: 6% - Cash needs at death: RM200,000 - Legacy (charity): RM80,000

Method 1: Capital Liquidation

Income Replacement (5 years, 6%):
PV = RM267,908 (calculator: BEGIN mode, n=5, i=6, PMT=60,000)

Total Need = [200,000 + 267,908 + 80,000] - 300,000
          = 547,908 - 300,000
          = RM247,908 (DEFICIENCY)

Method 2: Capital Conservation

Income Replacement (perpetual, 6%):
Capital = 60,000 / 0.06 = RM1,000,000

Total Need = [200,000 + 1,000,000 + 80,000] - 300,000
          = 1,280,000 - 300,000
          = RM980,000 (DEFICIENCY)

⚠️ EXAM TIP: Deficiency = Insurance amount to buy. If negative (surplus), no insurance needed for that component.

🏥 3.4 Critical Illness Coverage

Growing Need in Malaysia: - Rising medical costs (10-15% annual increase) - Common critical illnesses: Cancer, heart disease, stroke, kidney failure - Private hospital costs: RM50,000-300,000+ per major treatment

Valuation Method Components:

Component Calculation Malaysian Context
Immediate Costs Hospital, surgery, initial treatment RM50,000-100,000 typical
Long-term Costs Follow-up, rehabilitation, modifications RM20,000+ annually
Income Replacement Lost income during recovery (Monthly income × Recovery months)
Inflation Adjustment Apply medical inflation rate Use 10-15% (higher than general inflation)
Existing Coverage Deduct current insurance/savings Company medical, personal savings
Lifestyle Costs Home care, dependent support RM10,000-30,000 annually

Example 3-5: Abdullah Family

Immediate medical: RM50,000
Ongoing treatment (annual): RM20,000
Income replacement (2 years): RM200,000 (RM100k × 2)
Lifestyle/care: RM10,000 annually
Less: Existing coverage: (RM30,000)
Less: Emergency savings: (RM50,000)

Total Critical Illness Need = RM200,000+

⚠️ EXAM TIP: Questions may ask to calculate total need - add all costs, subtract existing resources.

🦽 3.5 Disability Income Coverage

Formula (Capital Conservation):

Capital Needed = Annual Income to Replace / Interest Rate

Capital = A / r

Example: - Annual income to replace: RM72,000 - Interest rate: 7% - Capital Needed = 72,000 / 0.07 = RM1,028,571

Important Considerations: - Disability often more financially devastating than death (ongoing costs) - May need lifetime coverage (vs. death which is one-time) - Include medical costs + income replacement - Social Security (SOCSO) provides some disability coverage but often insufficient

🎓 3.7 Education Funding

Approach: Calculate future cost and work backwards to present value.

Process: 1. Estimate total education cost (university 4 years) 2. Apply education inflation (typically 6-8% in Malaysia) 3. Calculate lump sum needed TODAY to fund future cost 4. OR calculate annual savings needed

Malaysian Context: - Local public university: RM30,000-50,000 total - Local private university: RM100,000-200,000 total - Overseas (UK/US): RM400,000-800,000 total

Solutions: - Education insurance plans - SSPN (National Education Savings Scheme) - Life insurance with education rider - Investment-linked plans

🏢 3.13 Business-Related Insurance Needs

Key Risks for Business Owners:

Risk Impact Insurance Solution
Death of Owner Business continuity, succession Buy-sell agreement funded by life insurance
Disability of Owner Business operations disrupted Disability income, key person insurance
Key Person Loss Loss of critical employee Key man insurance
Property Damage Business interruption Business property, interruption insurance
Employee Benefits Retirement, death gratuity obligations Group insurance, retirement plans

Buy-Sell Agreement Funding: - Partners insure each other - Upon death, proceeds buy deceased’s share - Ensures smooth transition, fair value for heirs

📊 3.10 Total Needs vs. Single Needs

Critical Distinction:

Approach Scenario Calculation
Single Needs Single individual, no dependents Own debts + final expenses + personal goals
Total Needs Married with dependents Both spouses’ income replacement + family obligations

Married Couples - TWO Approaches:

Option 1: Single Need Per Person - Calculate husband’s need independently - Calculate wife’s need independently - Buy separate policies

Option 2: Total Combined Need - Calculate total family need if breadwinner dies - May result in different coverage amounts - Often more accurate for joint expenses

⚠️ EXAM SCENARIO: Question presents married couple, asks for insurance calculation. - If asks for “husband’s coverage” = Single need for husband - If asks for “family protection” = Total needs approach

💡 Exam Preparation - Critical Formulas

MUST MEMORIZE:

1. Human Life Value (BEGIN mode)

HLV = A × (1+r) × [(1-(1+r)^-n)/r]
or use PV function with BEGIN mode

2. Capital Conservation

Capital = Annual Income / Interest Rate
Capital = A / r

3. Capital Liquidation

Use PVIFA / PV calculation with END or BEGIN mode

4. Total Insurance Need

Need = [Cash + Income Replacement + Legacy] - Resources

5. Income Replacement (Disability)

Capital = Annual Income × (1 - Tax Rate) / Interest Rate

📝 Self-Assessment Questions (30 Questions)

Section A: Concepts (1-10)

1. Premature death in Malaysia is typically defined as death before age: - A. 50 - B. 55 ✅ - C. 60 - D. 65

Exam Tip: Standard retirement age in Malaysia is 55.

2. The Human Life Value approach calculates: - A. Total assets of the deceased - B. Present value of future income for dependents ✅ - C. Future value of current assets - D. Replacement cost of the person

Exam Tip: HLV = PV of income stream lost.

3. Which is a limitation of the HLV method? - A. Too complex to calculate - B. Assumes salary remains constant ✅ - C. Requires medical examination - D. Cannot be used for self-employed

Exam Tip: Know all 5-6 limitations by heart.

4. In HLV calculation, the financial calculator should be set to: - A. END mode - B. BEGIN mode ✅ - C. Either mode - D. Continuous compounding mode

Exam Tip: BEGIN mode because income received at start of each year.

5. Capital Conservation approach differs from Liquidation because: - A. It uses lower interest rate - B. Principal is preserved, not consumed ✅ - C. It has shorter time period - D. It ignores inflation

Exam Tip: “Conservation” = “Conserve the principal”

6. Which approach requires MORE capital? - A. Capital Liquidation - B. Capital Conservation ✅ - C. Both require same amount - D. Depends on interest rate

Exam Tip: Conservation ALWAYS requires more (principal preserved).

7. The formula Capital = A/r is used for: - A. Human Life Value - B. Capital Liquidation - C. Capital Conservation ✅ - D. Education funding

Exam Tip: Simple perpetuity formula = Conservation approach.

8. If annual income need is RM60,000 and interest rate is 6%, capital conservation requires: - A. RM360,000 - B. RM600,000 - C. RM1,000,000 ✅ - D. RM1,200,000

Calculation: 60,000 / 0.06 = 1,000,000

9. Legacy planning in Malaysia focuses on: - A. Minimizing estate taxes - B. Ensuring smooth wealth transfer ✅ - C. Avoiding insurance - D. Reducing liabilities

Exam Tip: Malaysia has NO estate tax, but smooth transfer still critical.

10. Critical illness insurance inflation rate in Malaysia is typically: - A. 3-5% - B. 6-8% - C. 10-15% ✅ - D. 20-25%

Exam Tip: Medical inflation higher than general inflation.

Section B: Calculations (11-20)

11. Mr. Ali, age 30, retires at 55. Annual income for dependents: RM50,000. Interest: 5%. HLV (approx): - A. RM500,000 - B. RM710,000 ✅ - C. RM850,000 - D. RM1,000,000

Calculation: n=25, i=5, PMT=50,000, BEGIN mode, PV ≈ 710,000

12. Capital liquidation for RM80,000 annual income, 6 years, 7% interest (approx): - A. RM420,000 ✅ - B. RM480,000 - C. RM540,000 - D. RM600,000

Calculation: n=6, i=7, PMT=80,000, BEGIN mode, PV ≈ 420,000

13. Capital conservation for RM90,000 annual income, 6% interest: - A. RM1,200,000 - B. RM1,350,000 - C. RM1,500,000 ✅ - D. RM1,800,000

Calculation: 90,000 / 0.06 = 1,500,000

14. Total need: Cash RM100k, Income RM500k, Legacy RM50k. Resources: RM200k. Deficiency: - A. RM350,000 - B. RM400,000 - C. RM450,000 ✅ - D. RM650,000

Calculation: (100+500+50) - 200 = 450,000

15. Disability income replacement: RM72,000 annually, 8% interest. Capital needed: - A. RM800,000 - B. RM850,000 - C. RM900,000 ✅ - D. RM950,000

Calculation: 72,000 / 0.08 = 900,000

16. Critical illness immediate cost RM60k, ongoing RM25k annually for 2 years, income loss RM150k. Existing coverage RM40k. Total need: - A. RM195,000 - B. RM220,000 ✅ - C. RM260,000 - D. RM300,000

Calculation: 60,000 + (25,000×2) + 150,000 - 40,000 = 220,000

17. If HLV at age 30 is RM800k, at age 35 with higher salary, HLV is: - A. Definitely lower (less years to retirement) - B. Could be higher despite fewer years ✅ - C. Must be exactly RM800k - D. Cannot calculate

Exam Tip: This tests understanding of HLV limitation - can increase with salary growth.

18. Annual income RM100k, 60% for dependents, 20 years to retirement, 6% interest. HLV (approx): - A. RM689,000 ✅ - B. RM720,000 - C. RM850,000 - D. RM1,000,000

Calculation: PMT = 60,000 (60% of 100k), n=20, i=6, BEGIN, PV ≈ 689,000

19. Conservation vs Liquidation (same income, same rate). Conservation is approx: - A. 50% more - B. Same amount - C. 100-200% more - D. 300-400% more ✅

Exam Tip: Conservation typically 3-4x liquidation amount (depends on years).

20. Education funding: RM200k needed in 10 years, 5% interest. Lump sum needed today (approx): - A. RM122,000 ✅ - B. RM150,000 - C. RM175,000 - D. RM200,000

Calculation: FV=200,000, n=10, i=5, PV ≈ 122,782

Section C: Application (21-30)

21. A single person with no dependents needs coverage for: - A. Income replacement for dependents - B. Own debts and final expenses ✅ - C. Children’s education - D. Spouse’s retirement

Exam Tip: Single, no dependents = only own obligations.

22. For married couple with 2 children, MOST critical coverage is: - A. Breadwinner’s life insurance ✅ - B. Car insurance - C. Travel insurance - D. Investment-linked plan

Exam Tip: Dependents = Life insurance priority #1.

23. Capital conservation approach is preferred when: - A. Client wants to leave inheritance ✅ - B. Client has limited budget - C. Coverage is temporary need - D. Interest rates are low

Exam Tip: Conservation = preserve capital for heirs.

24. Business buy-sell agreement is typically funded by: - A. Bank loans - B. Life insurance on partners ✅ - C. Business savings - D. Government grants

Exam Tip: Cross-purchase insurance on partners.

25. Key person insurance protects against: - A. Employee theft - B. Loss of critical employee ✅ - C. Property damage - D. Market competition

Exam Tip: Compensates business for loss of key employee.

26. For critical illness, which cost typically HIGHEST? - A. Funeral expenses - B. Income replacement during recovery ✅ - C. Immediate medical treatment - D. Insurance premiums

Exam Tip: Lost income over months/years > one-time medical bill.

27. EPF death benefit is considered: - A. Cash in bank - B. Cash resources ✅ - C. Cash equivalent - D. Non-liquid asset

Exam Tip: Received in cash on death = Cash Resources (from Chapter 2 Table 2-1).

28. SOCSO provides coverage for: - A. Retirement only - B. Disability and employment injury ✅ - C. Critical illness only - D. Education funding

Exam Tip: Employment injury, disability, survivors’ benefits.

29. In calculating total needs for married couple, if both work: - A. Only consider higher earner - B. Calculate each spouse’s contribution ✅ - C. Ignore lower earner - D. Use average of both incomes

Exam Tip: Both incomes matter for family lifestyle.

30. Legacy planning through insurance is important in Malaysia because: - A. High estate taxes - B. Provides liquidity and smooth transfer ✅ - C. Required by law - D. Reduces family conflict (partially true, but B is primary reason)

Exam Tip: No estate tax, but liquidity and smooth transfer crucial.
